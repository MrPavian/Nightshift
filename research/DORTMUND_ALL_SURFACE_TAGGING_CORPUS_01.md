# ALL SURFACE — Dortmund Tagging and Handstyle Corpus (W02.28)

**Date:** 2026-11-14  
**Purpose:** establish *ALL SURFACE* as a distinct visual research corpus for Dortmund tags and handstyles, and define a rights-aware inspection protocol. This is a source/access dossier, not a page-level visual analysis.

## 1. Publication record and advertised scope

The Hitzerot / publisher shop page identifies *ALL SURFACE – Tagging in Dortmund* as a book published in November 2022 by Holger Wiese and Tobias Sawitzki. The current listing gives 244 pages, 17 × 24 cm, open-spine binding, blind embossing, German language and an edition of 500. The listing displayed €39.90 and “Only 1 left in stock” when checked on 2026-11-14; price and stock are volatile retailer data.

The publisher describes the book as a focused record of marker and aerosol-can tags from Dortmund in the 1980s and 1990s. It says the documentation of everyday tags was rare because photographs tended to privilege standout pieces and analog photo development was expensive. It advertises hundreds of mostly unknown photographs and short recollections by LOOMIT, ZEBSTER, BABAK, MASON, DRY, KLEH, SALZ, DAM, BALOO, SER, SHYE/SATYR, DENAR and KEY.

Source: https://hitzerot.com/shop/all-surface/

**Evidence boundary:** the above are publisher/editorial descriptions and bibliographic metadata. No page, image, caption, contributor text or colophon from the physical book has been inspected in this pass. The contributor list is not a crew roster and does not prove that every named person appears as a tag author in the image corpus.

## 2. Historical claims and attribution

The product description attributes to SHARK a recollection that a visit to Amsterdam in the mid-1980s, and seeing a heavily written city, influenced the ambition to bring similar tagging intensity to Dortmund. The page connects this account to a named group of Dortmund writers.

Treat this as an attributed practitioner recollection reproduced by the publisher, not as independently established causal history. It is consistent with the broader Amsterdam-influence lead already recorded in the Dortmund dossiers, but it does not settle exact travel dates, participants, direction of influence or stylistic transfer.

The publisher's explanation for the scarcity of old tag photographs is plausible contextual testimony, not a measured survey of the surviving photographic record.

## 3. Why this corpus changes the NIGHTSHIFT research plan

Earlier Dortmund research has leaned heavily on pieces, panels, wholecars, wholetrains and later artist retrospectives. *ALL SURFACE* is explicitly organized around the everyday signature layer. It should therefore be treated as a separate corpus rather than folded into a generic “Dortmund style” sample.

Research questions enabled by legitimate page-level inspection:

1. How do tag structures vary in rhythm, slant, compression, letter continuity, terminal treatment and stroke economy?
2. Which observations recur across multiple credited or uncredited examples, and which are individual handstyles?
3. Are marker and aerosol tags distinguishable in the source through captions or visible material evidence, rather than guesswork?
4. What evidence exists for location, date, surface and author attribution on each page?
5. How do tag and handstyle practices relate to, but differ from, the more elaborate piece / train compositions documented in *Da kukse wa* and *IRON CITY*?

Do not convert the publisher's broad description into numerical style parameters. Do not infer a single canonical Dortmund handstyle.

## 4. Item-level inspection protocol

For every selected image, record only what the page or independently corroborated source supports:

- book page and image/frame identifier;
- visible format: tag / handstyle / throw-up / piece / other / uncertain;
- medium: marker / aerosol / other / uncertain;
- support: wall / street furniture / paper / train / other / uncertain;
- exact writer credit as printed, or “uncredited”;
- date and location only when captioned or independently corroborated;
- observable formal descriptors, written neutrally and without imitation instructions;
- whether the image is a crop, detail, sequence or full context;
- rights holder, license/permission and permitted uses, or “unresolved”.

Keep direct observations separate from interpretation. A single tag cannot define a writer's full handstyle, and multiple examples do not automatically justify a city-level rule.

## 5. Rights and non-attribution rule

A book purchase or publisher product page does not grant NIGHTSHIFT reproduction, scanning, public display, model-training or derivative-use rights. Do not copy pages or photographs into the repository or game assets. Request explicit written permission for any reproduction or commercial use.

Never reproduce an identifiable living writer's signature, tag or distinctive piece as a generator preset or template. Research may inform abstract, non-attributive dimensions—such as stroke rhythm, slant range, compression, continuity and terminal behavior—only after cross-writer review and human calibration.

## 6. Additional institutional archive route: Stadtarchiv Dortmund

The official Stadtarchiv describes extensive collections of photographs, graphics, films, maps and local press documentation. The Archive.NRW record says audiovisual and photographic cultural material is preserved for public research and that image/media material must be pre-ordered by email. It also states that photographs and digitized archival materials published on the Archive.NRW site are under CC BY-SA 4.0, subject to attribution, archival signature, any named creator credit and share-alike conditions.

This is a promising route for contextual photographs of Dortmund streets, infrastructure and public space. It is **not** evidence that the archive holds a specific graffiti collection or that every item is online / CC BY-SA. Rights must be checked at item level. For physical media, request the relevant finding aid and pre-order material before visiting.

- Stadtarchiv Dortmund: https://www.dortmund.de/rathaus/verwaltung/kulturbetriebe/stadtarchiv/
- Archive.NRW record and rights statement: https://www.archive.nrw.de/stadtarchiv-dortmund
- Contact: stadtarchiv-dortmund@stadtdo.de

Suggested enquiry topics: private photographer estates / Nachlässe; photographs of public space and rail infrastructure, 1980–2000; local press photo collections; film holdings documenting urban change; and any holdings indexed under Graffiti, Sprayer, Jugendkultur, Bahn / Nahverkehr or named publications. These are search terms, not claims that matching holdings exist.
