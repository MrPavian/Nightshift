# German Graffiti Publications & Archive Pathways 01

**Research date:** 14 November 2026  
**Status:** source map and bibliographic pilot; not a complete catalogue and not image-rights clearance.

## Purpose

This dossier advances W02.6 and the BLACKBOOK ARCHIVE source-research roadmap. It identifies publication-level sources that can anchor a German scene history, records only issue-level facts actually located, and defines a repeatable path for building item-level records.

## 1. EINE STADT WIRD BUNT — Hamburg 1980–1999

### Bibliographic record

- Title: *EINE STADT WIRD BUNT. Hamburg Graffiti History 1980–1999*
- Publication: 2021
- Extent: 560 pages; more than 1,300 images
- Language: German, with additional English texts
- Project: long-running research and exhibition project; the book emerged after more than six years of research and production.
- Editors/project curators named on the official site: Oliver Nebel (Davis One), Mirko Reisser, Frank Petering and Andreas Timm (Cario).
- Official sources: project information and contents [GP-01; GP-02]; museum exhibition record [GP-03].

### What the contents establish

The published contents page identifies chapters on:
- Hamburg urban development and open spaces in the 1970s/80s;
- graffiti as youth/subculture and the emergence of the local scene;
- early meeting places and the Dammtor corner;
- arrests, public discourse and the figure of the “Bomber der Stadt”;
- the second wave and Steilshoop;
- OZ and the detail of his visual practice;
- punk, pop, *Wild Style* and the breakdance wave;
- the first magazines and Hamburg graffiti/hip-hop publications;
- press, films, literature and an index.

This is a strong editorial structure for an archive because it links visual work to urban space, social history, media, conflict and participant testimony. It also gives a direct route into a Hamburg publication bibliography.

### Named scene participants / provenance cautions

The official project page lists contributors and interviewees, including writers and music figures. The editors' biographies state, for example, that Davis One was active since 1988 and edited *Dosenspuk* from 1992 and *Beastie Boyz* until 2001; Andreas Timm/Cario was active since 1988 and edited *Daily Bombs* from 1992 to 1999; Frank Petering was active in the scene from 1986 to 1995; Mirko Reisser/DAIM has been active since 1989. [GP-01; GP-04]

These are attributed biographies from the project, not independently cross-checked crew membership records. Preserve aliases and periods exactly as the source presents them.

### NIGHTSHIFT implications

- Treat Hamburg as a documented local scene with its own social, spatial and media history, not just a regional subheading under “Germany”.
- Add publication records for *Dosenspuk*, *Beastie Boyz*, *Daily Bombs* and *Backspin* as distinct serials; do not conflate them.
- Add a Hamburg timeline that links locations, publications, events and writers only where the book/source explicitly supports the relation.
- OZ requires a dedicated entry with the project's nuanced social/legal framing; avoid turning his practice into a letter-style preset.

## 2. DECADES — Berlin 1990–2000

### Bibliographic record

- Title: *DECADES Vol. 1 — 1990–2000*
- Author: Peter Stelzig
- Publisher: Racoon Books
- Publication: 2021
- Extent: 324 pages; over 860 photo illustrations
- Language: German and English
- Focus: Berlin graffiti writing from 1990 to 2000, with a strong train-writing focus, plus walls and private photographs.
- Official publisher and bibliographic description: [GP-05]; secondary catalogue record: [GP-06].

### Named writers and crews in the published description

The publisher/catalogue description names BAS2, BOY, CATS, CHILL, CHIO, DEON, DISNE, DMB, DRUM, FOK, INKA, KEL, KMER, KAMEL, LYTE, ODEM, POET62, REW, RUZD, DSYER, SEKD, SHEK, SOME, STEAK, TFB, TOK, TORCH, TOWN, VDS and WEX, among others.

This is an index of names the book says it portrays; it is not a complete roster of the Berlin scene and does not establish crew membership for every listed writer.

### NIGHTSHIFT implications

- Berlin's post-Wall 1990s history needs its own chronological treatment.
- ODEM can be connected to this publication as a documented Berlin 1990s subject, while the specific *On the Run* memoir remains a separate first-person source.
- The volume is a lead for names, dates and visual examples, but item-level image rights remain with the relevant rights holders.
- Later volumes 2000–2010 and 2010–2020 should be indexed separately, not assumed to share identical scope.

## 3. STYLEFILE — Aschaffenburg / European and international scene

### Publication record

- Magazine founded in 1999 in Aschaffenburg.
- First issue sold out within weeks, according to the Grafflibrary history.
- Editorial scope described by its founder and publisher as a mix of ongoing graffiti activity, primarily European, with international coverage.
- Grafflibrary records the run as 1999–2025; Publikat's current collection and founder pages document the revived publication and back issues. [GP-07; GP-08; GP-09]

### Verified issue-level starter index

| Issue | Date | Cover / interview / local focus | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stylefile 1 | September 1999 | Cover JOAN; interview TOWN & FASER; local special JUNK | [GP-10] |
| Stylefile 2 | March 2000 | Cover SAFIR; interview AOD; local special GLOCK | [GP-11] |
| Stylefile 12 | July 2003 | European train coverage; walls; Ma'Claim special noted in contemporary review | [GP-12] |
| Stylefile 61 | 2025 | COMEBACKfile; HORST cover/interview; FORCE, NIKE, RENS feature | [GP-13] |
| Stylefile 62 | listed as recent issue | Issue exists in Grafflibrary dashboard; detailed contents not verified in this pass | [GP-14] |

**Important correction to catalogue practice:** issue 1 is described by Grafflibrary as September 1999; one reseller lists a “Prototypefile” issue 1 as November 1999. Record these as separate descriptions until the physical issue/editorial colophon resolves the discrepancy. Do not merge dates silently. [GP-10; GP-15]

### NIGHTSHIFT implications

- Stylefile is publication infrastructure, not a crew, style or writer.
- Its issue index can connect city reports, crews, writers, trains, walls and blackbook culture across countries.
- Build records from actual issue contents, not from cover names alone.
- The archive's first issue-level index is deliberately partial. Do not invent contents for missing issues.

## 4. Martha Cooper Library at URBAN NATION — Berlin

### Institutional record

The Martha Cooper Library specializes in literature on urban art, street art and graffiti since the 1960s, with a focus on style writing from the 1970s onward and the U.S. tradition. Its holdings include monographs, scholarly works, 1980s/90s graffiti magazines, fanzines, rare small-edition publications and archival material. The library states that its catalogue (OPAC) can be searched remotely; most items are in closed stacks and must be ordered to the reading room. It is a reference library, not a lending library. [GP-16; GP-17]

### What was and was not verified

Verified:
- the institution and its scope;
- the existence of a public OPAC;
- the collection's stated inclusion of graffiti magazines and fanzines;
- access conditions and closed-stack status.

Not verified in this pass:
- whether specific Stylefile issues are held;
- whether named German magazines or specific issues are held;
- item-level catalogue records, accession details or image permissions.

**Do not claim that the MCL holds a named magazine or issue until a title-level OPAC record is located.** A collection-level description is not proof of a specific holding.

### Research protocol

1. Search OPAC by exact title and known variants: Stylefile, Daily Bombs, Dosenspuk, Beastie Boyz, Backspin, On the Run, and relevant Berlin/Hamburg zines.
2. Record exact title, issue/volume, publication date, creator/editor, publisher, catalogue identifier, format, holding status and any access restrictions.
3. Distinguish catalogue records from physical inspection.
4. Record image reproduction rights separately from reading-room access.
5. If the OPAC does not return a record, mark “not located in searched catalogue” rather than “not held”.

## Editorial boundary

A book's public description, index or cover can establish scope and listed names; it cannot establish every historical claim that might be inferred from it. Before publication, each biography, crew affiliation, date and visual caption needs its own source pointer. Reading access, catalogue presence and reproduction rights are three separate things.
