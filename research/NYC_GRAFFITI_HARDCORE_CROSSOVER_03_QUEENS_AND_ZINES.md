# NYC Graffiti–Hardcore Crossover: Queens Networks, Band Roles and DIY Print 03

**Scope:** source-deepening dossier focused on Queens networks, dated band participation and fanzine production. It supplements the two earlier crossover dossiers; it does not replace them.

**Method:** distinguish first-person recollection, author-curated band/crew listings, publisher descriptions and direct visual evidence. A source that describes a person as a writer in a band is stronger for membership than a band-themed artwork; neither automatically proves a crew affiliation.

## 1. Queens as a relationship network: Dmize, MQ and DMS

### Rob “Beto” Rosario's account (2014)
In a 20 October 2014 No Echo interview, Dmize guitarist Rob “Beto” Rosario gives a specific account of how the band came together:
- He met Hoya in 1984 at I.S. 61.
- He says he and Hoya met Richie, Jere and Chiqui through graffiti writer MQ around late 1987 or 1988.
- He says Richie and Jere joined in 1988–89; Jere left after the first demo and Chiqui stepped in.
- He describes Dmize as a Corona/Jackson Heights band and acknowledges a connection to DMS, while explicitly declining to overstate the band's involvement.
- He recalls the first show at a Brooklyn biker club, the Iron Knights, after other bands cancelled.

**Evidence status:** first-person recollection by a band member. High value for Beto's own account of the social route through MQ; dates are retrospective and should remain attributed rather than converted into exact archival dates.

### Chiqui Rodriguez's account (2016)
A 3 March 2016 No Echo interview with Dmize vocalist Chiqui Rodriguez adds detail:
- He identifies Jere as the original Dmize frontman and a childhood friend from Jackson Heights.
- Asked about MQ, he describes his own writing as limited mainly to Jackson Heights, “a block or two,” and contrasts it with writers who covered all Queens or the world.
- The interviewer states that Jere sang on the first Dmize demo released in 1990; Chiqui responds from his perspective as someone present through the period.
- The interview describes Dmize performing at CBGB's in the early 1990s and notes that members later moved into other bands.

**Chronology caution:** Beto says Jere left soon after the first demo; Chiqui's interview identifies Jere as singer on that 1990 demo. These accounts are compatible at a broad level, but they do not establish the exact date of the lineup change. Preserve the sequence as attributed, not as a day/month timeline.

### What can be said about DMS
- The Powerhouse Arena 2017 event description says original DMS crew members discussed the founding of their organization and that MQ described his influences.
- i-D's 2017 feature calls MQ/MKUE a DMS-affiliated writer and describes DMS as made up of members from the NYHC scene.
- Beto acknowledges Dmize's connection to DMS but does not define the band's membership or claim Dmize itself was a DMS band.

**Do not infer:** a complete DMS roster, exact founding date, or that every Dmize member was in DMS.

## 2. Band-by-band evidence: more precise role distinctions

Freddy Alva's 30 October 2017 No Echo article supplies a band-by-band list of writers who played in bands. This is an author-curated secondary source, but it is unusually specific and is directly connected to his Urban Styles research.

| Band | Named connection in Alva's list | What the source supports |
|---|---|---|
| Frontline | HYPE, ME 62, RATE 5, RAGE, NOAH | Identified as band lineup; formed 1981 |
| Adam 12 | REVS, FRIPP, ENO | Identified as early-1980s band members |
| Terminal Confusion | VOYER, NIM, MAR, BUNK | 1986 demo-era lineup; writers affiliated with TMR |
| Loud and Boisterous | MANOR, SATYR | Band lineup; Brooklyn, formed 1985 |
| DIE 116 | NATZ, CREEP; name linked to SANE/SMITH alternate tagging name | Band name and named participants; distinguish naming reference from membership |
| Second 2 None | BUNK, ZZ, ACID, TRETAL | Band lineup; TMR-related |
| Scoundrel | HESA | HESA described as Brooklyn writer and STAFF member who played in the band |
| Last Cause | NOW, WOES, YOURS 174 | Named band participants; additional band histories for NOW and WOES |

### Important individual notes
- **HESA / STAFF:** Alva identifies HESA as a Brooklyn writer from the all-city crew STAFF (expanded in the source as “Satanic Teens Attacking for Fame”) and says he played in Scoundrel. This is a writer-to-band connection, not proof that all STAFF members were musicians.
- **NOW:** Alva says NOW played in Fit of Anger in the late 1980s and later Everybody Gets Hurt.
- **WOES:** Alva says WOES played in Taste of Fear and continued performing under the Woe Is Me name.
- **YOURS 174:** Alva calls him a “toy” from Jackson Heights, Queens. This is the author's characterization and should not be repeated as a neutral historical label without context.
- **BUNK / Chris Bunkley:** Alva links BUNK to Terminal Confusion and Second 2 None and credits him with publishing The Village Noize and Yes Zista. A separate No Echo zine retrospective says Chris Bunkley worked on The Village Noize, then published two issues of Yes Zista in 1988 and 1989, and later sang in Second 2 None and Terminal Confusion.

## 3. Fanzines: dated evidence for the Queens DIY network

A No Echo feature titled “Queens Fanzine Explosion” provides a useful retrospective account of Yes Zista:
- Chris Bunkley from Queens Village worked on The Village Noize before starting Yes Zista.
- Yes Zista focused primarily on NYHC bands and published two issues, dated 1988 and 1989.
- Bunkley recalls the DIY “for the kids by the kids” ethic and describes physical cut-and-paste production using scissors and rubber cement.
- The retrospective says Bunkley later sang for Queens bands Second 2 None and Terminal Confusion.
- It notes an interview with Raw Deal (Killing Time) in the second issue.

**Why this matters:** this is more than a zine title attached to a graffiti writer. It traces a person through print production, local music journalism and later band participation. It documents a DIY communications network in which the same people could move between writing, editing, reviewing and performing.

**Still unverified:** original scans of both Yes Zista issues and The Village Noize have not been inspected here. Do not invent issue contents beyond the specific second-issue Raw Deal interview reported by the retrospective.

## 4. Visual material and artifact trail

### What the sources actually show or credit
- Alva's 2017 article includes an image caption identifying RAGE, HYPE, ME 62 and NOAH as Frontline at CBGB in 1982.
- It includes a caption for NIM, VOYER and MAR from TMR.
- It explicitly credits Urban Styles cover art to STAK TFP, layout to Orlando Arce and handstyle to JERE DMS.
- It identifies a reproduced DIE 116 piece as by SANE SMITH.
- The book's publisher and bookseller descriptions say the volume draws on record and demo covers, flyers, T-shirts, paintings, interviews and first-hand accounts; this establishes the book's source mix, not the provenance of every individual image.
- A Bandcamp release page for the Urban Styles mixtape lists tracks by Frontline, Adam 12, Terminal Confusion and Loud and Boisterous. This is useful discographic corroboration that recordings were compiled, but it is not a substitute for original demo sleeves or dated flyers.

### Research handling
For each artifact added to the BLACKBOOK ARCHIVE, capture:
1. artifact type (demo sleeve, flyer, zine page, photo, painting, cassette);
2. exact visible credit or caption;
3. named creator and whether attribution is printed, oral-history based or retrospective;
4. original date versus later reproduction date;
5. source URL / holding institution / access date;
6. rights status and permitted use;
7. whether the artifact proves a band role, a visual credit, a crew affiliation, or only scene proximity.

No original flyer, demo sleeve or zine scan has been directly inspected for this dossier. The captions and credits above are attributed to the published secondary source.

## 5. SEEN / Richard “Richie” Mirando — core writer profile, not a forced crossover

- Artnet's artist biography identifies SEEN as Richard Mirando, born in the Bronx in 1961, beginning to paint in 1973 with United Artists, and notes the crew's association with Pjay, Duster, Sin and his brother Mad.
- A 2013 interview published by Jin Wong includes SEEN's first-person recollection of starting on trains in 1973 and waiting to photograph his work as trains emerged from the yard.
- A 2015 Molotow studio feature repeats the UA names and describes his subway work and later tattoo studio.
- These sources support a strong standalone SEEN/UA historical profile. They do not establish a direct SEEN membership or collaboration link to the specific NYHC bands in the crossover dossiers.

**Archive decision:** maintain SEEN as a major Bronx / UA writer entity with a separate relationship graph. Add a NYHC edge only if a dated artifact, direct interview or reliable attributed source supports it.

## 6. NIGHTSHIFT design implications — inference, not history

1. Model a scene as a temporal graph with distinct edge types: band member, crew member, writer, zine editor, zine publisher, artwork credit, skate team member, venue attendance, and friend introduction.
2. Preserve provenance at edge level: first-person interview, author-curated list, period artifact, retrospective caption or publisher copy.
3. Support partial and uncertain date ranges. “Late 1987 or 1988” should remain an approximate interval, not be silently converted to an exact date.
4. Allow one person to have multiple identities and roles without collapsing aliases into a single unsupported record.
5. Represent Queens as overlapping local networks (Corona, Jackson Heights, Queens Village, northern Queens) rather than a single monolithic scene.
6. Keep visual attribution and musical participation independent. An artwork representing a band is not evidence that its creator played in it.
