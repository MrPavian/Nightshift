**Date:** 2026-11-14

## 1. Why Heidelberg matters

Heidelberg is not merely a famous hip-hop city or a side note in a Mannheim/Karlsruhe comparison. It is a documented early writing scene whose local style was recognised beyond the city. The strongest current synthesis is Heidi Pfeiffenberger's 2017 KIT dissertation, *Style Writing: Skripturale Ästhetik zwischen Text und Bild am Beispiel von Karlsruhe und Mannheim*. Its historical chapters treat Munich, Heidelberg and Stuttgart separately, and its later chapters analyse the regional exchange between Heidelberg, Mannheim and Karlsruhe.

The dissertation describes Heidelberg as a comparatively small scene with a distinctive style identity and influence in nearby cities. It also records an important counterpoint: strong imitation of KANE's personal style reportedly limited stylistic diversity at one point. NIGHTSHIFT must preserve both the influence and the danger of flattening a scene into one signature.

### Important distinction: the first train

There are at least three separate historical claims in the sources:
1. a first panelpiece at Tennisplätze-Yard in 1987;
2. Stieber's account that his group painted an Autobahndreieck in 1988 and that trains followed shortly after;
3. a later archival / oral-history reconstruction of the first train sprayed in Heidelberg, presented at a 2024 event by the Freies Hip-Hop Institut and University of Heidelberg.

Do not merge these into one date or event. The 2024 event is a priority route for corroboration and may clarify the chronology.

## 3. Heidelberg Style — documented visual characteristics

Pfeiffenberger's dissertation describes the Heidelberg Style / Parallelstyle / Heidelberg-Wildstyle as a local development from Wildstyle and older New York vocabulary. Its attributed characteristics include:
- alternating thick and thin letter widths;
- block-like letter bodies;
- large 3D blocks, sometimes extending down to and visually resting on the ground;
- interlocked and dynamically connected letter structures;
- strong attention to typography and letterform construction;
- a traditionalist relationship to New York roots, alongside influence from Berlin.

These are **historically attributed tendencies**, not a universal recipe. The dissertation explicitly notes that the strong influence of KANE's personal style reportedly led many local writers to imitate him, limiting stylistic diversity. It also records that later generations and crews deliberately distanced themselves from the classic Heidelberg style.

### NIGHTSHIFT research translation — hypotheses, not historical facts

Build original exercises that test:
- variable stroke weight within one word;
- block-volume construction and 3D massing;
- baseline contact / grounded extrusion;
- dense interlocking and overlap planning;
- controlled directional flow through connected letter structures;
- separation of a shared regional vocabulary from an individual writer's signature.

Do not encode a “KANE preset” or reproduce a specific historical piece. Study several independent examples and author neutral glyphs that demonstrate the general construction problems.

## 4. Regional network: Heidelberg, Mannheim, Karlsruhe and beyond

The dissertation describes regular exchange among southern German scenes and specifically discusses a Heidelberg influence in Mannheim and Karlsruhe. It also records the influence of Heidelberg panelpieces seen by writers in other cities, and the movement of visual ideas through train routes, shared crews, magazines and personal contact.

Useful regional links to research:
- **Mannheim:** early scene, writers such as GONZ / Gonzalo Maldonado Morales, GISMO, SKARE, MIKO, PAST and BASCO; compare its rougher Oldschool identity with Heidelberg's blockier Wildstyle-derived vocabulary.
- **Karlsruhe:** compare local personal styles and the exchange of style elements with Heidelberg and Mannheim.
- **Stuttgart and Basel:** the dissertation describes early Stuttgart links to Munich and Basel; compare these with Heidelberg's network rather than treating the southwest as a single scene.
- **Rhein-Main:** the dissertation places Heidelberg in a wider network with Frankfurt, Wiesbaden and Mainz.

Do not assume a one-way diffusion model. Record who travelled, which crews overlapped, what publications circulated, and when a claimed influence is first documented.

## 5. Primary-source and archive routes

### Heidelberg City Archive — Torch collection

The City of Heidelberg reports that around 5,000 objects from Torch / Frederik Hahn's collection were delivered to the city archive for inspection and cataloguing. The official description includes photographs, sound carriers, posters, flyers, magazines, drawings, personal papers and sketches of early graffiti trains. This is a major archival lead, but it does not mean every item is already catalogued, publicly accessible or cleared for reproduction.

Research questions for the archive:
- Is there an item-level finding aid or inventory for the graffiti-related photographs and train sketches?
- Are there original photographs documenting the 1987 panelpiece, first trains, HCA, SCM or early Heidelberg crews?
- Are there blackbooks, letters, flyers, magazines or dated contact sheets?
- What are the access, reproduction and publication conditions for research and commercial product development?

### Freies Hip-Hop Institut / University of Heidelberg

A 2024 event at the Friedrich-Ebert-Haus brought together the Freies Hip-Hop Institut and University of Heidelberg to discuss Heidelberg street art and graffiti. The institution's 2024 annual report says the event used archival material and eyewitness accounts to reconstruct the first train sprayed in the city. This is the best current route to clarify the first-train chronology and locate further oral histories.

### Practitioner testimony

Martin Stieber's 2014 interview is a valuable first-person source for:
- the arrival of US subway-art / breakdance material in 1984;
- meeting Torch and early Heidelberg pieces;
- the 1988 Autobahndreieck and subsequent train activity;
- Heidelberg's reputation for Wildstyle and typography;
- Gonzalo / GONZ's ability to adapt and develop US style vocabulary.

It is a retrospective interview, not a contemporaneous record. Attribute recollections and seek corroboration.

## 7. NIGHTSHIFT implications

1. Keep **type**, **lettering style**, **design element**, **carrier medium**, **surface**, **technique** and **context** as separate dimensions.
2. Create a regional research layer for Heidelberg / Mannheim / Karlsruhe, but do not implement a single “South German” preset.
3. Add a non-attributive letter-construction study for variable stroke weight, block mass, interlocking, 3D volume and baseline grounding.
4. Keep train-panel composition separate from wall-piece composition; the available sources indicate trainwriting is historically important in the Heidelberg region.
5. Build a visual evidence ledger with dates, source IDs, photographer, location, rights and confidence before any image is used in the editor.
6. No commercial use of Torch archive material, INGRID images, publication scans or third-party photographs without item-level permission / license review.
