# RAMMELLZEE, GOTHIC FUTURISM AND KASE 2’S COMPUTER ROCK

**Scope:** two distinct approaches to radicalizing letterforms in New York graffiti culture.

## Why they are connected — and distinct

RAMMELLZEE and KASE 2 both pushed lettering beyond straightforward name-writing, but through different approaches. RAMMELLZEE developed an artist-authored theory about the alphabet, language, power and the weaponization of letters. KASE 2 developed an individual visual approach he called “computer rock”, camouflaging the letters of his name. They are parallel experiments, not one style.

## RAMMELLZEE: Gothic Futurism

The Studio Museum in Harlem identifies RAMMELLZEE (1960–2010; born in Queens) as an artist working across graffiti, hip-hop, painting and sculpture. Its account describes his 1979 manifesto, Iconic Treatise on Gothic Futurism, as arguing that letters separated from ordinary literary function could become weapons against oppressive linguistic systems. It connects his thinking to medieval monastic script and alphabet history. [A] https://radicalpresence.studiomuseum.org/index0acb.html?page_id=1790

The Museum of Fine Arts Boston describes RAMMELLZEE and the Tag Master Killers (TMK) crew — originally A-One, Kool Koor and Toxic — as guided by his philosophy. It summarizes his imagery as letters armed for battle, intergalactic vessels and nebulae, and states that he stopped writing on trains in 1980 to focus on the art world. [A] https://www.mfa.org/exhibition/writing-the-future/dive-into-writing-the-future

Art Basel adds context on Gothic iconography, typography, science fiction and horror imagery, but its curator quotations are exhibition interpretation. [B] https://www.artbasel.com/stories/rammellzee-graffiti-street-art-1980s-new-york-nyc-jean-michel-basquiat-jim-jarmusch-palais-tokyo-paris

A Red Bull Music Academy discussion with Jeff Mao explicitly cautions that interpreting Gothic Futurism from outside is difficult. Mao describes it as a system of principles central to RAMMELLZEE’s work and recounts the artist’s connection between graffiti lettering and medieval calligraphy. Retain this caution; do not flatten a complex personal theory into decorative keywords. [B] https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2018/05/jeff-mao-on-rammellzee/

Gothic Futurism is not simply graffiti letters with medieval decoration or science-fiction styling. It is an artist-authored theory and interdisciplinary body of work. Motifs should be presented as examples, not as a complete definition.

## KASE 2: Computer Rock

MoMA’s collection entry for KASE 2 (Jeff Brown) documents a felt-tip sketch titled Kase, dated broadly c. 1970–86. MoMA describes it as one of many sketches for graffiti planned for walls and subway cars, and identifies “computer rock” as the writer’s own name for an approach that camouflaged the letters of his alias to the point of illegibility. [A] https://www.moma.org/collection/works/288915

This is unusually useful institutional documentation: it ties the term to a specific artist, work on paper and named approach. Do not generalize it into a universal movement or label all abstract graffiti “computer style”.

## Comparison

| Dimension | RAMMELLZEE | KASE 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Core focus | Alphabet, language, power, historical/speculative theory | Camouflage of a writer’s own name |
| Named concept | Gothic Futurism; Ikonoklast Panzerism | Computer Rock |
| Media | Graffiti, painting, sculpture, performance | Graffiti sketches and planned wall/subway compositions |
| Legibility | Challenges ordinary linguistic function within a larger theory | Alias letters camouflaged toward illegibility |

## Archive records

**RAMMELLZEE:** writer/artist; concepts Gothic Futurism and Ikonoklast Panzerism; 1979 manifesto; TMK connection. Museum documentation supports biography and manifesto; interpretations must remain attributed.

**KASE 2:** writer/artist; alias Jeff Brown; named approach Computer Rock. MoMA documents the term and approach. Treat it as an individual practice, not a general style family.

## Generator inference

The archive needs separate fields for historical style families, individual named approaches, artist-authored theory and the generator’s own abstract operations. The generator may teach segmentation, camouflage, counter-shaping, modular construction and controlled loss of legibility, but should not offer an “authentic Rammellzee mode” or clone KASE 2’s signature. Use neutral educational labels and rights-cleared references.
