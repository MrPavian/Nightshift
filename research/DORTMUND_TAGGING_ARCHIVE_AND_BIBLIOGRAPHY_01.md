## 1. *ALL SURFACE* — authoritative bibliographic anchor

The Deutsche Nationalbibliothek's 2023 Series B bibliography records:

- **Title:** *All surface: tagging in Dortmund in den 1980er und 1990er Jahren*
- **Creators:** Holger Wiese and Tobias Sawitzki
- **Edition:** 1st edition
- **Place / publisher:** [Dortmund] : Holger Wiese, Tobias Sawitzki
- **Year:** 2022
- **Extent:** 238 pages
- **Format:** 24 cm, paperback
- **ISBN:** 978-3-00-071073-5
- **Classification:** Bildband

The record is in the Deutsche Nationalbibliografie, 2023 B 14, dated 5 April 2023. This is the strongest bibliographic anchor located in this pass.  
Source: https://d-nb.info/1285451368/34 (entry at printed page 26; PDF text lines 6215–6223).

### Metadata discrepancy — keep visible

The Hitzerot shop, Mr. Graffiti and the Stiftung Historische Museen Hamburg event page describe the book as **244 pages**. The DNB lists **238 pages**. The bookseller Le Grand Jeu independently repeats 244 pages and identifies it as self-published by the editors, edition of 500. This may reflect differing conventions for counted/unnumbered pages or a catalogue error, but that explanation is not verified.

**Repository rule:** use the DNB record as the bibliographic authority for the formal citation (238 pages); retain “244 pages” as a commonly published retail/event description and mark the conflict. Do not silently normalize it. The 17 × 24 cm measurements found in specialist listings also differ in orientation/order from the DNB's 24 cm height-only catalogue field; do not infer a true discrepancy from dimension ordering alone.

## 2. What the sources establish — and what they do not

The publisher/specialist shop description says the book concentrates on marker and aerosol tags from Dortmund in the 1980s and 1990s; it reports hundreds of photographs and recollections by scene participants. It also attributes an important Amsterdam influence to SHARK's mid-1980s visit and names SHARK, ZODIAK, CHINTZ, CHANA, SATYR, ZHIT, SPOOK, KINOR and ATAIR in connection with the resulting wave of tagging.

The SHMH institutional event text independently confirms the book's subject, title, 244-page public description and 500-copy edition, and places it in the context of contemporary graffiti publishing. It does **not** independently verify the book's individual image captions, dates, locations, or every influence claim.

The Amsterdam narrative remains an **attributed scene account reproduced in the publication's sales/editorial description**, not a measured or independently triangulated causal explanation. NIGHTSHIFT should phrase it as “the book's editors/publisher report…” or “SHARK is reported to have recalled…”, not as a universal origin story for Dortmund handstyle.

## 3. *Da kukse wa* — publication and reprint provenance

The author page confirms the 1987 publication, Marianne Brentzel's texts, Hubert Moormann's photographs and interviews with CHINTZ, SHARK, CHANA and ZODIAK. It also reproduces an excerpt and describes Moormann's photographic work as spanning more than two years.

The Dortmunder U's official *I WAS HERE* exhibition page states that the digital 360° exhibition includes an online browse-through of the *Da kukse wa* brochure and that a reprint was made available through the museum. It thanks Brentzel and Moormann for enabling the reprint. This is stronger provenance for the 2020 reprint than a retailer listing alone.

A specialist retailer says the 2020 second edition is identical to the 1987 original except for the preface. This is **not** confirmed by a page-by-page collation in this research pass. Keep the statement attributed to the retailer until the two editions are compared.

Sources:
- Author / excerpt: https://www.mariannebrentzel.de/da-kukse-wa-leseprobe.html
- Official Dortmunder U exhibition and digital access note: https://dortmunder-u.de/i-was-here-360/
- Retailer reprint description: https://www.mrgraffiti.nl/product/da-kukse-wa-dortmunder-graffiti-2nd-edition/

## 4. Chronology and crew-name caution

The KIT dissertation places writing in Dortmund in 1983 and TUF's formation in 1984/85; it links ZODIAK (then BIMBO) to an early piece and TUF's formation, and cites *Da kukse wa*, Behforouzi, Wiese, Schwarzkopf and Treeck. The KWI regional article calls CHINTZ, ZODIAK and SHARK TUF members and expands the name as The United Force. A 2022 local-history newsletter describes TUF as covering the city from 1985.

These sources broadly converge on a mid-1980s TUF formation, but they do not provide a complete, stable membership register. Do not infer that every name appearing in *ALL SURFACE* belonged to TUF. The larger tag corpus and the crew are different entity sets.

Sources:
- KIT dissertation: https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000079409/6209138
- KWI regional article: https://blog.kulturwissenschaften.de/graffiti-im-ruhrgebiet/
- Unionviertelzeitung 56 (2022), p. 18: https://www.unionviertel.de/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/56_unionviertelzeitung__web.pdf

## 5. NIGHTSHIFT archive and generator implications

1. Maintain separate content entities for **publication**, **person/writer**, **crew**, **place**, **image/item** and **style hypothesis**. Do not turn a book's contributor list into a crew roster.
2. Preserve two independent Dortmund research tracks:
   - **Piece / wall / train corpus:** *Da kukse wa* and dated contextual imagery.
   - **Tag / handstyle corpus:** *ALL SURFACE* and separately captioned tag records.
3. For *ALL SURFACE*, the useful abstract dimensions are tag format (marker/aerosol), letter rhythm, repeated letterform behavior, joins, terminals, slant, scale and surface context. These are fields to inspect in the actual book, not findings already verified from its sales description.
4. For any visual item, require page number, caption, credited writer, photographer, date/location confidence and rights status before adding a structural observation.
5. Do not copy a named writer's tag, signature, or identifiable piece into a generated preset or glyph template. Learn only abstract, non-attributive properties after human review.
6. Do not claim that NIGHTSHIFT has inspected the book's 500+ images or the *Da kukse wa* flipbook. This pass verifies bibliographic and access records only.
