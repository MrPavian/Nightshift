## 1. *Dirty Hands* (2022) — two Dortmund-specific chapters

Springer VS's official book record identifies two directly relevant chapters in *Dirty Hands: Interdisziplinäre Perspektiven auf die Graffiti-Szene* (eds. Paul Eisewicht and Laura-Maria Lintzen, 2022):

1. Harald Hinz, **“Formale Aspekte der Dortmunder Graffiti – eine lokalstilistische Einordnung”**, pp. 253–267, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-658-37799-1_15
2. Rahel H. Grothus, **“Stilbeschreibung des Œuvres des Künstlers Mason”**, pp. 269–278, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-658-37799-1_16

Official book record: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-658-37799-1

### 1.1 Hinz — formal features and the limits of a city style

The publisher's abstract for Hinz states that Lisa Gottlieb's 2008 classification of a “Graffiti-Kunst-Stil Dortmund” uses three defining characteristics:
- sharp letter contours;
- consistent stem / stroke thickness;
- opaque colour application.

Hinz applies these criteria to a 2006 work by MOSES and observes that the execution has a softer ductus than its predecessors. This is a useful, concrete analytical starting point, but it is **not** an A–Z construction specification, a universal description of every Dortmund writer, or a measurement study. It is a published interpretation of a locally classified style.

The chapter's accessible notes also show a valuable source practice:
- It compares a reconstructed 2006 MOSES train scene with images in Wiese (1996) and Treeck & Todt (1995).
- It discusses the relation between a MOSES piece and a prior MASON work, explicitly framing the reuse as historical self-quotation / self-cooptation rather than automatically equating every visual resemblance with copying.
- It cites a direct communication from SHARK dated 30 January 2020 and a 2020 television documentary, *The Rise of Graffiti Writing*.
- It cites a conversation with Krell / COLE / CHANA on 14 February 2020.
- It cites David Radon in a conversation dated 9 December 2020 for a late-1990s move toward sculptural / illusionistic fill-in treatment.

**Evidence boundary:** the abstract and notes are accessible; the full argument, all figures and exact context of the interview references have not been inspected. Do not attribute more detailed statements to SHARK, CHANA or Radon than the accessible chapter itself makes explicit.

Chapter page: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-658-37799-1_15

### 1.2 Grothus — MASON as a longitudinal individual case

The publisher's abstract says Grothus analyses MASON's signature oeuvre across four time periods, from the beginning of his work in 1986 through 2020, identifying both change and persistent characteristics. The abstract states that the analysis combines artist conversations with viewing and comparing artworks, and argues that MASON emancipated himself from prevailing styles around the signature in the 1990s.

This is particularly valuable because it distinguishes:
- a **city-level/local-style question** (Hinz);
- an **individual artist's longitudinal development** (Grothus).

NIGHTSHIFT must not collapse the latter into a generic Dortmund preset. A single artist's distinctive development is not a regional average.

Chapter page: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-658-37799-1_16

## 3. Newer longitudinal scene research — Lintzen (2025)

Laura Maria Lintzen's *Wandel der Graffiti-Szene in Deutschland: Eine lebensweltanalytische Ethnographie* (Springer VS, 2025) is a major new research route. The publisher describes an account of the German graffiti scene from the 1980s across three generations, focusing on changing participation practices, scene structures and entry conditions, with a typology of aesthetics, writer types and spots from an insider-oriented ethnographic perspective.

- Print ISBN: 978-3-658-46189-8
- eBook ISBN: 978-3-658-46190-4
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-658-46190-4
- Official record: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-658-46190-4
- KIT library catalogue: https://katalog.bibliothek.kit.edu/bib/1455512

The 2022 *Dirty Hands* chapter by Lintzen documents a 1.5-year ethnographic project in 2017–2018 focused on the Dortmund scene, combining field participation, literature and artefact analysis, observation and problem-centred interviews. The 2025 book is broader in scope and should not be treated as a Dortmund-only monograph. Its full chapter contents have not been inspected in this pass.

## 4. Consequences for NIGHTSHIFT's Dortmund grammar

The available evidence now supports a more precise, still provisional framing:

### Dortmund Block / Piece hypothesis
- sharp contour definition;
- relatively consistent stem weight;
- opaque, solid fill application;
- clear, compact/block-oriented letter readability as a broader regional description.

The first three attributes are explicitly reported in Hinz's summary of Gottlieb's classification. The broader “blocky and clear” characterization is also reported by KWI. These should be testable visual tendencies, not hard-coded universal rules.

### Dortmund Tag / Handstyle hypothesis
- keep separate from the piece grammar;
- use *ALL SURFACE* as the dedicated visual source, but inspect actual pages before recording gesture, terminal, slant or rhythm observations;
- treat the Amsterdam influence narrative as attributed practitioner/editorial history, not a sole causal explanation.

### MASON longitudinal case
- useful for studying change over time and individual signature consistency;
- do not use as a generic style preset or copy signature forms;
- only abstract and non-attributive principles may enter NIGHTSHIFT after human review.

### Implementation caution
The generator should represent these as independent, source-backed dimensions (contour sharpness, stroke-weight consistency, fill opacity, handstyle rhythm, etc.) with maturity and provenance. Do not merge them into one opaque “Dortmund” slider. No numeric bounds are justified by the accessible sources.
