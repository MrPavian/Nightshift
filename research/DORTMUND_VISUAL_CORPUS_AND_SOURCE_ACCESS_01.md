# Dortmund Visual Corpus — Source Access and Analysis Plan 01

**Date:** 2026-11-20  
**Status:** source-access map; no historical image has been copied into NIGHTSHIFT. Five Dortmund photographs in the Pixelprojekt Ruhrgebiet series have now been visually inspected at page level; no individual glyphs have been transcribed.  
**Purpose:** identify the strongest available visual records for Dortmund writing and separate handstyle evidence from pieces, train productions and later retrospective documentation.

## 1. The corpus has two different layers

### A. Piece / wall / train evidence

**Da kukse wa — Dortmunder Graffiti** (Tapir Verlag, 1987)
- Texts: Marianne Brentzel; photographs: Hubert Moormann.
- Moormann followed the scene over approximately two years, according to the contemporary book description reproduced by ILOVEGRAFFITI and the author page.
- Interviews: CHINTZ, SHARK, CHANA and ZODIAK.
- The author page provides an excerpt and confirms the publication is out of print. ILOVEGRAFFITI links a digital flipbook. The Dortmund U's *I WAS HERE* 360° exhibition page also says the brochure can be browsed online.
- **Access:** author excerpt and linked flipbook; exhibition page is currently protected by a browser verification layer in this research session.
- **Use:** primary-period visual and oral-history source; page-level captions and image dates must be checked in the publication itself.
- **Rights:** reference-only / unresolved. A public flipbook is not permission to extract or reuse photographs.
- Links:
  - https://www.mariannebrentzel.de/da-kukse-wa-leseprobe.html
  - https://ilovegraffiti.de/blog/2015/03/02/buch-da-kukse-wa-graffiti-in-dortmund-1987/
  - https://ilovegraffiti.viewbook.com/album/da-kukse-wa
  - https://dortmunder-u.de/i-was-here-360/

A 2020 second edition is described by a specialist bookseller as essentially identical to the 1987 original except for its preface. This is a retailer description, not a collation performed here. It may provide a practical acquisition route for study; compare physical copies before treating pagination or contents as identical.
- https://www.mrgraffiti.nl/product/da-kukse-wa-dortmunder-graffiti-2nd-edition/

### B. Handstyle / tag evidence

**ALL SURFACE — Tagging in Dortmund** (Holger Wiese and Tobias Sawitzki, November 2022)
- 244 pages, German, edition of 500, open-spine binding.
- Dedicated to marker and aerosol tags from Dortmund in the 1980s and 1990s.
- Publisher / specialist shop description reports hundreds of mostly previously unknown photographs and short recollections by scene participants.
- It explicitly connects SHARK's mid-1980s Amsterdam visit to the influence of Amsterdam's bombing mentality and style on SHARK, ZODIAK, CHINTZ, CHANA and other named writers.
- **Value:** unusually strong visual resource for handstyle variation, tag construction, marker vs aerosol, repeated letterforms and citywide circulation. It should not be used as direct source art for generated tags.
- **Rights:** reference-only / permission required for all photographs and quoted material.
- Links:
  - https://hitzerot.com/shop/all-surface/
  - https://www.mrgraffiti.nl/product/all-surface-tagging-in-dortmund-in-the-1980s-and-1990s/

## 2. Independent historical cross-checks

- The KIT dissertation *Style Writing* (2017), pp. 94–95, places Dortmund writing in 1983, TUF's founding in 1984/85, early Amsterdam / CTK influence and a train-bombing high point around 1991. It cites older scene publications including *Da kukse wa*, Schwarzkopf (1994), Behforouzi (2006), Treeck (2001) and Wiese (1996). These references are a bibliography trail for further checking.
  https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000079409/6209138
- *DIE ZEIT* (1989) and *taz* (5 October 1989) independently document the public prosecution / media controversy around SHARK, CHINTZ and CHANA. They are evidence of contemporary public visibility and conflict, not neutral descriptions of artistic quality.
  https://www.zeit.de/1989/42/helden-der-szene/seite-2
  https://taz.de/Uns-sind-die-Staedte-viel-zu-gruen/%211796030/
- KWI's regional overview describes the Dortmund letters as exceptionally blocky and clear, and connects the early scene with hip-hop culture and the TUF network.
  https://blog.kulturwissenschaften.de/graffiti-im-ruhrgebiet/
- ATOM ONE's retrospective interview adds a later 1994–1998 compositional development narrative. Treat it as his own account, not as a universal timeline.
  https://www.montana-cans.blog/black-artist-edition-20-special-interview-with-atom-one/

## 3. Visual-analysis split for NIGHTSHIFT

Do not merge tags and pieces into one generic Dortmund grammar.

| Corpus | What to inspect | Candidate NIGHTSHIFT learning |
|---|---|---|
| ALL SURFACE | marker tags, aerosol tags, repeated signatures, hand movement, letter joins, terminal gestures | handstyle rhythm and tag-specific gesture vocabulary |
| Da kukse wa | pieces, wall/train context where captioned, colour, silhouette, letter mass, composition | block-letter construction and whole-word composition |
| Dated train photos | car seams, windows, doors, panel boundaries, wholecar scale | surface-aware layout constraints |
| 1994–1998 retrospective examples | denser letter combinations and composition | transition from simple block grammar toward more complex relationships |

### Analysis protocol

For each image selected from a source, record:
1. Item/page number and exact caption.
2. Photographer and credited writer/crew, if supplied.
3. Date and place with confidence.
4. Format: tag, throw-up, piece, panel, wholecar, wall or sketch.
5. Letter observations: skeleton, terminals, counters, proportions, stroke mass and rhythm.
6. Relationship observations: spacing, overlap, connections, negative-space channels.
7. Execution observations: marker/aerosol, outline, fill, highlights, surface and photographic uncertainty.
8. Rights and access status.
9. Abstract design inference, explicitly not a copied glyph or artist-specific recipe.

## 5. Product decision

NIGHTSHIFT should model at least two separate Dortmund-related research profiles:
- **Dortmund Block / Piece grammar study** — provisional, based on *Da kukse wa* and dated piece imagery.
- **Dortmund Handstyle / Tag study** — provisional, based on *ALL SURFACE* and dated tag examples.

These are research categories, not claims that all writers used one style. The two profiles should not be collapsed into one “Dortmund preset”. All source imagery remains reference-only until item-level permission is documented.

### Pixelprojekt Ruhrgebiet — Benito Barajas, *Lost Spaces Graffiti* (2010)

Official series page: https://www.pixelprojekt-ruhrgebiet.de/serie/lost-spaces-graffiti

The page documents seven photographs, each catalogued at 60 × 40 cm:
- **Dortmund:** Dortmunder Norden; Südbahnhof (three photographs); Hafen.
- **Comparative Ruhrgebiet locations:** RuhrUni, Bochum; Alte Fabrik, Hagen.
- The series describes graffiti and tags in abandoned stations, factories, houses and wall remains, with repeated visits by different writers and accumulated layers over time.
- The page explicitly states that all usage rights to the photographs belong to Benito Barajas. No image was copied, embedded or redistributed.

**Five Dortmund images opened and inspected at page level:**
1. **Dortmunder Norden (2010):** dense accumulation of different marks and larger pieces across an industrial/derelict surface. Useful for context and coexistence of formats; no individual letter can be safely isolated from the page view.
2. **Südbahnhof (2010), image 2:** a broad, highly legible block-like upper composition sits above a denser, more interlocking lower composition. This is a useful within-image contrast between simpler letter mass and more complex overlap, not evidence that one is the canonical Dortmund style.
3. **Südbahnhof (2010), image 5:** repeated dark handstyle/tag marks across a wall; the page is useful for rhythm and accumulation, not for a clean alphabet sample.
4. **Hafen (2010):** rounded, inflated letter masses with strong outlines and colour fields, surrounded by smaller secondary marks. Useful as a bubble/throw-up composition reference.
5. **Südbahnhof (2010), image 7:** a large interlocking composition with layered outlines, internal lines, directional extensions and a strong green fill. Useful for whole-piece relationship analysis, not isolated glyph extraction.

These are page-level observations from a curated 2010 series of abandoned-space locations. They do **not** establish the frequency of any style in Dortmund, and they do not document the early 1980s/1990s period directly. The image file paths use the series' large-image directory, but exact pixel dimensions were not independently verified. Keep all five items link-only and rights-restricted.
