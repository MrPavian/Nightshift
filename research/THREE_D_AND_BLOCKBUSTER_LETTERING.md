# 3D LETTERING AND BLOCKBUSTER LETTERING

**Scope:** distinguish dimensional construction from large-scale squared lettering.

## Essential distinction

**3D** describes a way of representing depth or volume. **Blockbuster** describes a broad, squared, forceful letter approach associated with large surfaces and distance visibility. They are not synonyms. A blockbuster can be flat; a letter can have 3D depth without being a blockbuster.

## Blockbuster lettering

Joe Austin’s Taking the Train describes blockbuster letters as squarish and sharp-edged, contrasting them with rounded softie/bubble letters. He notes their resemblance to urban advertising sign lettering. This is a historical comparison, not proof of one source or direct borrowing. [A] https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7312/aust11142

Observable tendencies include broad angular letter masses, simple forceful silhouettes, high visibility and efficient use of a large surface. These are descriptive tendencies, not a universal template. Do not assert that every large block letter is historically a “blockbuster”.

## 3D lettering and attribution

PISTOL’s artist-controlled biography identifies 3D and U.S.-flag pieces from 1972 and 1973 among his early subway works. [B] https://gopistolart.com/bio

A thesis excerpt quoting BLADE and PISTOL in Yes Yes Y’all: Oral History of Hip-Hop’s First Decade preserves PISTOL’s claim that he developed a 3D approach around 1973 because earlier letters were flat and one-dimensional. The same recollection acknowledges that anyone could have done it. This is a valuable practitioner account, not conclusive proof of priority. [A/B] https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file?accession=ucin1338905792&disposition=inline

Anna Waclawek’s thesis also attributes early 3D lettering to PISTOL I. This is a secondary synthesis; precise origin should remain qualified until dated works and the original oral-history passage are examined directly. [A] https://central.bac-lac.gc.ca/.item?app=Library&id=NR63383&oclc_number=768533755&op=pdf

## What 3D means in the generator

Depth is a geometric relationship between a front face and offset/extruded planes, not itself a letter skeleton or lettering family.

- Extrusion: consistent vector offset from the face contour.
- Perspective depth: direction and scale vary toward a chosen vanishing point.
- Block shadow: flat offset silhouette, not necessarily a modeled volume.
- Inline/bevel/highlight: surface details that may suggest depth but are not depth geometry themselves.

These are present-day implementation terms, not necessarily historical writers’ vocabulary.

## Archive records

**Blockbuster:** lettering style/composition approach; squared, angular, broad, high-impact; evidence as descriptive historical category supported by scholarship; generator controls for width/height, corner treatment, stroke mass and surface fit.

**3D lettering:** spatial/rendering treatment; early practitioner claim associated with PISTOL; early 1970s, with a 1973 claim in retrospective oral history; early development documented but exclusive invention unsettled; generator controls for depth vector, extrusion, perspective and face/side separation.

## Generator inference

Keep lettering grammar, depth treatment, surface format, fill treatment and outline treatment independent. This permits a squared blockbuster skeleton with flat rendering or 3D depth without mislabelling the historical style. Calculate depth from the final vector contour rather than hard-coding separate 3D glyphs.
