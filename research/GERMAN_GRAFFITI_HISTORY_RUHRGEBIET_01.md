# German Style Writing — History, Regional Scenes and the Ruhrgebiet 01

**Status:** research synthesis; regional chronology remains open to practitioner and archival verification.  
**Scope:** West Germany and selected German scenes from the late 1970s through the 2000s, with emphasis on Dortmund/Ruhrgebiet, Berlin, Hamburg, Munich, Cologne/Düsseldorf and eastern Germany.

## 1. Do not write one German graffiti history

German writing developed through connected but locally distinct scenes. The available research points to early activity in the Cologne–Düsseldorf area, Munich and Dortmund, followed by stronger inter-city connections and distinct regional developments. Films, books, travel, visiting writers, jams, crew networks, transport systems and local surfaces all mattered.

The KIT dissertation by Heidi Pfeiffenberger, *Style Writing: Skripturale Ästhetik zwischen Text und Bild am Beispiel von Karlsruhe und Mannheim* (2017), is a central scholarly source. Its historical chapter describes München and Dortmund as early German centres and records how writers and scenes connected from the mid-1980s onward. Its regional case-study scope must be respected; it is not a complete national census. [DE-01]

Ulrich Blanché's 2024 article summary in *Wallraf-Richartz-Jahrbuch* identifies style writing in the Cologne–Düsseldorf area from 1979 and traces early impulses through Harald Naegeli, punk, the Neue Wilde, New York's East Village art scene and visits by New York writers including LEE and DAZE. This is an important regional account, not proof that every German scene began at the same time. [DE-02]

## 2. Dortmund and the Ruhrgebiet: a distinct lettering language

The Dortmund scene deserves its own substantial chapter. The KIT dissertation describes Dortmund as a major early centre and discusses the development of the so-called Dortmund Style: comparatively simple, readable, angular block letters, often presented in black and silver. It also records the role of Dortmund crews and writers in making the city a European destination for train writing in the 1990s. [DE-01]

The Kulturwissenschaftliches Institut Essen's Ruhrgebiet overview similarly identifies Dortmund as an early hip-hop environment in which rap, dance, DJ culture and graffiti developed together. It names CHINTZ, ZODIAK and SHARK and connects the blocky Dortmund Style to wider recognition in the Ruhrgebiet and internationally. [DE-03]

The research must not flatten the Dortmund Style into a single preset. It should compare dated examples and document changes in:
- letter skeleton and block construction;
- proportion, width and height rhythm;
- corner and terminal treatment;
- internal spacing and negative space;
- outline/fill conventions;
- train, wall and other surface contexts;
- individual variation within shared regional tendencies.

### Early Dortmund network — research leads
Sources name ZODIAK, CHINTZ and SHARK in the early Dortmund scene; later accounts and research mention ROC, COLE TWS, ZHIT, MASON, IZM, RIO, SKE3, DNK and others. [DE-04] The KIT dissertation also discusses the TUF crew and writers including CHINTZ, ATOM, SKE, SOB, VOLA, SAK, RIO, SMOKE and SHARK in the context of 1990s train writing. [DE-01]

These names are research leads, not a complete or definitive crew roster. Crew membership, spelling, dates and aliases must be confirmed per person and period.

## 3. Too Strong, ATOM / Der Lange, Pure Doze and Silo Nation

Too Strong belongs in a history of Dortmund hip-hop and writing networks, not only in a music sidebar. Interviews connect the group with graffiti writers, jams, local identity and the wider Ruhrgebiet scene. [DE-05; DE-06]

Important identity distinction:
- **ATOM ONE / Der Lange** is the writer and rapper discussed in the ILOVEGRAFFITI Podcast #040; the episode connects him with the TRD Crew and Too Strong.
- **Pure Doze / DOZE** is a separate Too Strong member and interview voice. Do not merge their identities.
- The podcast describes Too Strong as founded in 1989 by DJ Zonic and Pure Doze, with DJ Brocke and Der Lange joining shortly afterwards. [DE-05]
- An interview with Too Strong members DOZE and ATOM describes Silo Nation's origin in 1993 at Der Lange's birthday gathering in Dortmund: a wordplay on Zulu Nation/Silo, a large scene gathering and a sense of belonging. [DE-06]
- Theater Dortmund's production description presents Silo Nation as a lifestyle/community associated with Too Strong and as a cultural umbrella bringing together rap, DJing, breakdance, graffiti/street art and beatboxing. [DE-07]

**Editorial classification:** Silo Nation should be modelled as a hip-hop community/network/identity and event culture, not automatically as a graffiti crew. Too Strong is a music group with deep ties to the Dortmund hip-hop and graffiti environment. ATOM ONE and Pure Doze need separate person records and cross-links.

The local oral-history dimension is essential: record how participants describe the relationship between music, crews, graffiti, jams and the Dortmund scene. Do not reduce it to a neat marketing narrative.

## 4. Berlin: crews, wall, reunification and network formation

The Jugendkulturen / Pop- und Subkulturarchiv documents the emergence of West Berlin crews in the 1980s, including TDC (The Denots Crew) and TCF (The Comic Freezers), and names writers such as AMOK, KAOS, SHEK and MAXIM. It also notes the particular material conditions in the GDR, where aerosol scarcity led some young people to use wall paint and shoe-polish markers. [DE-08]

A BerlinWriters interview describes the growth of West Berlin crews, writing on the Wall, early train painting toward the end of the 1980s and the importance of travel to Amsterdam and meeting points where writers exchanged sketches and information after the Wall came down. This is a retrospective scene account and should be attributed accordingly. [DE-09]

ODem's *On the Run* is a first-person/biographical scene narrative about a young Berlin writer in the early 1990s. The book is a useful source for lived experience and the darker sides of scene life, but its publisher synopsis is not independent verification of every episode. [DE-10]

Later, 1UP (One United Power), founded in Kreuzberg in 2003 according to The Writers Bench Collection, became internationally visible through collective use of one crew name, actions and extensive documentation. Its collaboration with Martha Cooper produced *One Week with 1UP*. [DE-11] This is a later chapter, distinct from the 1980s/1990s origins.

## 5. Hamburg: DAIM and the archive as historical infrastructure

The University of Hamburg's Centre for the Study of Manuscript Cultures identifies Mirko Reisser / DAIM as a key figure in Hamburg's scene from 1989. It describes his distinctive exploration of the four-letter name DAIM, with form changing over decades, and his work as an archivist of Hamburg writing history. [DE-12]

The CSMC research project studies Reisser's private archive, described as containing around 50,000 photographs, thousands of books and magazines, sketchbooks and artist correspondence. The project's purpose is to investigate production, transformation, preservation and destruction of ephemeral style writing in social, spatial, historical and cultural contexts. [DE-13]

This archive should be treated as a major research infrastructure and a potential source of interviews, periodicals, dated photographs and local scene networks—not merely as a source of DAIM artwork.

## 6. Ma'Claim and photorealistic aerosol practice

AKUT's first-person account says Ma'Claim was founded in 2001 after earlier collaboration among AKUT, CASE and RUSK, with TASSO inviting CASE and AKUT to form the crew and RUSK joining the developing constellation. TASSO's interview also identifies him as founder in 2001 and explains that the crew focused on photorealistic character painting, while RUSK contributed style-writing. [DE-14; DE-15]

TASSO explicitly resists a simplistic invention claim: he points to earlier photorealistic aerosol work by Earl from Jena, Simo from Dresden/Hamburg and Chio from Chemnitz, saying Ma'Claim intensified and developed an existing field rather than inventing it. [DE-15]

The crew's importance for NIGHTSHIFT is not that it defines classic letter writing. It is a case study in:
- collaboration and specialization;
- photorealistic aerosol technique;
- digital planning and translation from photographic reference to large wall work;
- the relationship between characters and lettering;
- international circulation of a German crew's work.

Keep Ma'Claim's photorealistic character focus distinct from DAIM's three-dimensional letterform research. Do not treat either as a generic “German style.”

| Dortmund / Ruhrgebiet | TUF, TRD, Too Strong, ATOM ONE, DOZE, CHINTZ, SHARK, ZODIAK, Silo Nation | Dortmund Style, train culture, hip-hop networks, jams, cross-border travel |

This is a research coverage map, not a ranking or exhaustive roster.

## 9. Open research tasks

1. Verify the precise history, scope and membership of TUF, TRD and Silo Nation through more first-person interviews and dated material.
2. Distinguish ATOM ONE / Der Lange from Pure Doze / DOZE in all records and media.
3. Find primary and contemporaneous evidence for Dortmund Style examples and its circulation outside the Ruhrgebiet.
4. Research Silo Jams, local venues, labels, flyers, tapes, photographs and the social network around Too Strong.
5. Inspect the MCL catalogue for German scene magazines, books and fanzines.
6. Build individual records for CHINTZ, SHARK, ZODIAK, ATOM ONE, Pure Doze, ODEM, DAIM, LOOMIT, TASSO, AKUT, CASE and RUSK only when sources support the specific claims.
7. Expand beyond famous men: locate women writers, photographers, organizers and overlooked regional scenes.
8. Add east German and post-reunification city histories without treating the former GDR as one uniform scene.

## 10. NIGHTSHIFT design implications — not historical claims

- German regional scenes should be first-class archive paths, not a footnote under “European influence.”
- Dortmund Style deserves a dedicated comparative construction study, based on multiple dated examples and distinct writer variations.
- Silo Nation should link graffiti, rap, DJing, jams and community history without being reduced to a generator style.
- DAIM's work can support a study of spatial ambiguity, perspective and three-dimensional letter construction, but never a DAIM signature preset.
- Ma'Claim can support a separate study of aerosol photorealism, digital planning and collaborative production, not a classic graffiti lettering grammar.
- Regional timelines must permit parallel development and cross-border influence rather than a one-way New York-to-Germany arrow.
