# WOMEN WRITERS IN EARLY NEW YORK GRAFFITI

**Scope:** early women writers, visibility and the limitations of conventional histories. This is a corrective dossier, not an exhaustive roster.

## Why this matters

The absence of women from familiar histories is not evidence that women were absent from the scene. Scholarship and interviews point to a documentation problem: writers who were not later publicly visible, left the scene or whose work was not preserved can disappear from retrospective narratives. Women’s activity has also been framed through relationships to male writers rather than as independent practice.

## Early names and limits of the record

- An academic introduction on “graff grrlz” notes that Craig Castleman’s early ethnographic account included CHARMIN 65, BARBARA 62 and EVA 62, while canonical books often mentioned women briefly and without equivalent contextual treatment. It argues that women’s contributions were made illegible by the research lens, not simply absent. [A] https://s3.amazonaws.com/supadu-imgix/ingram-nyu/pdfs/introduction/9781479895939_intro.pdf
- Artsy’s 2019 feature, drawing on interviews and research by Jessica Pabón-Colón, names BARBARA 62, EVA 62 and MICHELLE 62 as active in Manhattan in the 1970s. It reports that CHARMIN 65 wrote on the Bronx Third Avenue elevated line in the early 1970s. Treat “first woman” formulations cautiously unless supported by dated primary documentation. [B] https://www.artsy.net/article/artsy-editorial-boundary-breaking-women-new-yorks-graffiti-scene
- A 2024 Nuart Journal discussion of the exhibition ILLEGAL emphasizes how writers such as BARBARA 62 and VAMPIRELLA disappeared from public memory. It reports research attributing important early work to BARBARA 62 and EVA 62, including movement between tags and pieces. This is a significant research lead, not settled consensus without review of underlying works. [A/B] https://nuartjournal.com/pdf/issue-8/04-NJ8-Blanche.pdf
- The Smithsonian Archives of American Art holds a 2022 interview with LADY PINK in the In Colors Oral History Project. This is a primary oral-history source for her own experience and recollections. [A] https://www.aaa.si.edu/collections/items/detail/lady-pink-interview-27380
- The National Endowment for the Arts describes LADY PINK’s entry into subway writing in 1979, her participation in the Cool Five and Public Animals, and her inclusion in the 1980 Fashion MODA exhibition Graffiti Art Success for America. [A/B] https://www.arts.gov/stories/magazine/2013/2/ahead-their-time/pure-expression
- MASS MoCA records her later founding of the all-women crew Ladies of the Arts and includes her comments on gender and representation. [A/B] https://massmoca.org/event/lady-pink/

## LADY PINK

LADY PINK (Sandra Fabara) began writing in 1979 as a teenager. Her recollections describe both participation in a male-dominated scene and the support and hostility she encountered. She also describes girls who drew in books but did not necessarily go on train-painting missions. Participation in writing culture therefore cannot be measured only by who painted subway cars.

Her career crossed subway writing, gallery exhibitions, film and later mural practice. The 1980 Fashion MODA exhibition is an important point in graffiti’s institutional presentation, but not the beginning of her writing practice.

## Research cautions

1. Do not create a token “women’s corner”. Integrate women writers into the main timeline, crew network, style history and visual reference system.
2. Do not infer gender from a name or photograph. Use self-identification and reliable biography.
3. Do not use “first woman” casually. “First” depends on what counts: first tag, subway writing, piece, surviving photograph or public identification.
4. Do not reduce women’s activity to partners, sisters or girlfriends of male writers.
5. Do not infer absence from canonical books. A gap in documentation is not proof of absence.

## Archive records

Create separate, source-led records for CHARMIN 65, BARBARA 62, EVA 62, MICHELLE 62, LADY PINK and CLAW. Record only supported details: alias, dates, area, crews, documented works, oral histories, source provenance and uncertainty. Do not fill missing biography with assumptions.

## Design inference

Make under-documentation visible. Include source coverage separately from historical importance; allow entries with limited biography but strong evidence of activity; show oral histories with provenance and permissions; do not rank writers by surviving photographs or later fame. Gender is not a visual style parameter in the generator. Relevant learning concerns are visibility, networks, access, authorship and documentation.
