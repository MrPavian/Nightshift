# AEROSOL TECHNOLOGY, CAPS AND PAINT PRACTICE

**Scope:** the material system behind aerosol graffiti: can, valve, actuator/cap, paint and surface.  
**Status:** introductory research synthesis. Manufacturer histories are treated as interested sources; ethnographic and scholarly sources are used for practice context.

## 1. The can is a system

Aerosol output is not controlled by the paint colour alone. The can combines:
- container and internal pressure;
- propellant;
- paint formulation and pigment;
- valve;
- actuator/nozzle;
- external cap or adapter;
- distance, angle, speed and movement of the person spraying;
- surface texture and environmental conditions.

Ursula K. Frederick’s peer-reviewed archaeological study, published in 2014 and based on fieldwork undertaken in 2012, describes aerosol-can components and accessories designed specifically for graffiti writers. It notes that writers historically adapted caps from other aerosol products to change spray width and pattern, while specialized caps later became commercially available. [A: archaeological/ethnographic research]

## 2. Caps and spray patterns

Janice Rahn’s ethnographic study records writers discussing fat and skinny caps, stock caps, fan width and modified nozzles. It describes the practice of taking caps from other aerosol products and adapting them to paint cans, as well as later commercial availability of specialist caps. [A: ethnographic research]

The important distinction for the archive:
- **Can/valve compatibility** is a physical interface question.
- **Cap/nozzle geometry** influences the emitted spray pattern.
- **Technique** is the writer’s control of distance, angle, speed, pressure and movement.
- **Visual result** is the combined outcome, not the cap alone.

Terms such as “fat cap” and “skinny cap” are common, but their exact spray widths vary with cap, can pressure, formulation and technique. Avoid assigning a universal millimetre width to a cap name without a specific manufacturer test and setup.

## 3. Material development and historical caution

Montana Colors’ own retrospective describes its 1996 work on high-pressure aerosols with a female valve compatible with caps popular in Northern Europe, including pink dot fat caps, grey banana caps and New York fat caps. This is a manufacturer’s account of its own product development; it is useful for the company’s history but not a neutral history of all aerosol innovation. [C: manufacturer source]

A separate academic conference abstract on sprayed paint places graffiti’s widespread adoption of aerosol in the late 1960s onward, while tracing earlier histories of sprayed pigment and industrial aerosol technology. The timeline of aerosol invention, commercial paint cans and graffiti-specific products must be kept separate: they are not one event. [B]

Surface, distance and gesture

Spray paint creates a relationship between body movement and surface. The CAA conference abstract notes that spraying allows the artist to work at a distance from the surface and that body movement—approaching and retreating, making small and large gestures—becomes part of the image-making process. [B]

For NIGHTSHIFT, this means a spray simulation should not be a uniform “airbrush” stamp. A credible visual model should consider:
- distance-dependent spread;
- movement speed and dwell time;
- pressure/flow consistency;
- cap pattern;
- paint opacity and layer build-up;
- surface roughness and absorbency;
- overlap and drying between passes.

These are technical design variables; the cited historical sources do not provide a complete physical paint simulation model.

## 6. Archive record proposal

- **Can:** pressurized container holding paint and propellant.
- **Valve:** controls release from the container.
- **Actuator/cap:** directs and shapes the output.
- **Spray pattern:** resulting distribution of paint droplets.
- **Fat/skinny cap:** colloquial cap categories; exact output is setup-dependent.
- **Pressure:** a property of the can/valve system, not simply a synonym for spray width.
- **Technique:** distance, angle, speed, motion and pass overlap.

## 7. NIGHTSHIFT design inference

Separate material simulation from lettering construction:
- can_profile: pressure/flow behavior and paint load
- valve_interface: compatibility class
- cap_profile: spray fan, edge softness and output rate
- paint_profile: opacity, hue, coverage and drying behavior
- surface_profile: roughness, absorption and curvature
- spray_motion: distance, angle, speed and path
- layer_stack: pass order and overlap

Do not encode brand names as historical style presets. Avoid using real product labels or packaging in fictional game assets. Generic cap profiles are sufficient for gameplay and protect the project from confusing a contemporary product catalog with historical truth.
