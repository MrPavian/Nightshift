# Dortmund Writing Vocabulary — 1987 Local Glossary and Taxonomy Notes (W02.27)

**Date:** 2026-11-14  
**Source anchor:** Marianne Brentzel, *Da kukse wa — Dortmunder Graffiti* (1987), author's official excerpt.  
**Purpose:** preserve a period-specific vocabulary snapshot for the BLACKBOOK ARCHIVE without turning one local glossary into universal definitions.

## 1. Directly documented period glossary

Brentzel's official excerpt includes a short “Writer-ABC” with the following entries:

| Term as printed | 1987 local explanation | Taxonomic role | NIGHTSHIFT treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| black-book | Book for sketches and photographs of painted and not-yet-painted works | Practice / archive object | Blackbook is a workspace/archive concept, not a lettering style |
| charakter | A comic-like sprayed figure | Figurative artwork element | Optional character layer; not required in a piece |
| getting fame | The writer's goal: becoming famous | Motivation / cultural context | Historical glossary content only; not a geometry parameter |
| piece | A large graffiti picture | Artwork form | Broad artwork category; do not impose a rigid complexity threshold |
| tag | The writer's name lettering | Signature / handstyle form | Separate handstyle authoring from developed piece lettering |
| whole car | A completely painted train | Vehicle-scale surface format | Train coverage format, not a font or style |

Source: https://www.mariannebrentzel.de/da-kukse-wa-leseprobe.html (excerpt headed “Writer-ABC”).

The definitions are useful because they are embedded in a Dortmund publication from the period rather than reconstructed from contemporary terminology. They should still be presented as **one 1987 local glossary**, not as the sole correct definitions across Germany, Europe or all graffiti eras.

## 2. Taxonomy consequences

1. **Tag vs piece:** the 1987 source distinguishes the writer's name lettering from a larger graffiti picture. A tag can be a standalone work or appear alongside pieces, but the two should not collapse into one authoring mode.
2. **Character vs lettering:** a comic-like sprayed figure is figurative content. It can accompany a piece, but is not part of the letter anatomy.
3. **Whole car vs style:** “whole car” is coverage of a vehicle surface, not a letterform grammar. Model car geometry and coverage separately from lettering style.
4. **Blackbook vs artwork:** the blackbook is the sketch/photo archive and practice environment. In NIGHTSHIFT this maps to the creative workspace and saved-project archive, not to a style preset.
5. **Getting fame vs visual output:** this is a cultural motivation, not a measurable property of a glyph or a guaranteed game outcome.

## 3. Historical context in the same excerpt

The author's excerpt reproduces a Dortmund newspaper controversy around the turn of 1986/87, including headlines about “Schmierer”, cleaning costs, train graffiti and public concern. It also frames the debate against unemployment, coal/steel employment decline, environmental conflict in Dorstfeld, apprenticeship scarcity and youth unemployment.

This is a source for how the author framed the period's public debate. It is **not** independent verification of each statistic, headline, police response or social condition. Any specific claim must be checked against the original newspaper issue, official data or another contemporaneous source before publication.
