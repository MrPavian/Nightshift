# Ruhrgebiet Writing — Dortmund, TUF and regional circulation (W02.36)

**Date:** 2026-11-14  
**Priority:** P1 — regional foundation  
**Status:** Source-led historical synthesis; Dortmund is strongly documented, other Ruhr cities remain comparison targets pending city-specific primary sources.

## 1. Research question

How did Writing develop in the Ruhrgebiet from the early 1980s, what made Dortmund an influential centre, how did the Dortmund block-letter approach circulate, and what can be translated into a neutral, cross-writer editor model?

This dossier deliberately does **not** assign a single uniform “Ruhrgebiet style”. It separates:
- Dortmund's early scene and TUF history;
- the locally described Dortmund Style;
- trainwriting / bombing as practice and infrastructure;
- influence and circulation into other Ruhr cities;
- present-day legal walls and public-facing graffiti culture.

## 2. Dortmund — chronology with source attribution

### Early scene and TUF
- A 2017 KIT dissertation dates the appearance of Writing in Dortmund to **1983**.
- It dates the formation of the first TUF crew (The United Force) to **1984/85** and reports that the crew included two women.
- The dissertation identifies ZODIAK (previously BIMBO) as an early influential Dortmund writer, active from 1983 and involved in the first pieces and TUF's formation.
- The 1987 book *Da kukse wa — Dortmunder Graffiti*, by Marianne Brentzel with photographs by Hubert Moormann, documents the early scene through images and interviews with CHINTZ, SHARK, CHANA and ZODIAK. Moormann followed the writers for more than two years. This is an unusually valuable contemporary source, rather than a later retrospective.

### Dutch / Amsterdam connection
The KIT dissertation links Dortmund's early visual influences to Amsterdam and the Crime Time Kings (CTK), citing the geographical proximity to the Netherlands. It also describes CHINTZ and SHARK as early Dortmund pioneers who adopted New York graffiti writing. These are not mutually exclusive: the international source culture and later European circulation need to be considered together.

### Trainwriting and the 1990–1991 peak
- The dissertation dates Dortmund trainbombing to **1984**, with a high point around **1991**, when wholecars were numerous and graffiti-free trains were reportedly rare.
- It describes Dortmund's train activity as sustained and unusually visible; a 1994 source quoted in the dissertation claims the local transit system had been continuously bombed for over 1½ years.
- The dissertation attributes Dortmund's 1990s European reputation and the arrival of visiting writers to the TUF network and a broader group including ATOM, SKE, SOB, VOLA, SAK, RIO, SMOKE and SHARK.
- The same account identifies a yard with 14 sidings as a major production context. This is historical source material only; this research does not map or publish operational rail access, security details or current infrastructure.

### Dortmund Style — the strongest documented visual description
The KIT dissertation characterises the late-1980s Dortmund Style as:
- simple rather than structurally intricate;
- often black-and-silver;
- legible, angular block letters;
- thick, blocky letter bodies;
- minimal or no Wildstyle interlocking;
- suited to spontaneous execution without preparatory sketches.

It quotes the scene description “dick, blockig, voll erkennbar” and says a related Ruhrgebiet tendency was described as “simpel-blockig-lesbar”. The KWI article independently describes the Dortmund Style as extremely blocky and clear, and says it attracted followers in the Ruhrgebiet and internationally.

**Caution:** these descriptions are historical generalisations, not a complete census of every Dortmund writer or every decade. They should be tested against dated photographs and the 1987 book before being treated as a visual rule set.

## 3. The Ruhrgebiet beyond Dortmund

The KWI article describes Writing as established in the Ruhrgebiet by the mid-1980s and names Dortmund as the region's first city with a hip-hop context in which dance, rap, music production and graffiti developed together. It also notes legal walls at Ruhr-Universität Bochum, Landschaftspark Duisburg, Essen and elsewhere.

This establishes regional spread and present-day legal infrastructure, but it does **not** establish equivalent 1980s/1990s histories or city-specific styles for Bochum, Essen or Duisburg.

## 4. Cross-source triangulation and contradictions

### Contemporary and retrospective sources
- *Da kukse wa* (1987) is close to the early scene and contains interviews and photographs. It is the first priority for direct visual and oral-history extraction.
- Contemporary 1989 reporting in *taz* and *Die Zeit* records the prosecution of SHARK, CHINTZ and CHANA. These sources are useful for public reception, legal conflict and the contemporary notoriety of the writers, but should not be treated as neutral style criticism.
- The KIT dissertation (2017) synthesises earlier publications and scene accounts. It is strong for an attributed chronology and visual terminology, but the references behind individual claims must be retained.
- Florian Deckers' KWI article (2022) provides an academic regional framing and connects Dortmund's history with the wider Ruhrgebiet, urban space and post-industrial sites.

### Important nuance
The early Dortmund scene was not only a style laboratory. It was also a high-volume tagging and bombing culture, a trainwriting network, and a social scene embedded in local hip-hop. A generator that extracts only the clean block-letter aesthetic would misrepresent the historical practice.

## 5. Editor implications — neutral, non-attributive mechanics

The evidence supports investigating the following independent controls. They are design hypotheses, not a “Dortmund preset” and not replicas of any named writer.

### Letter geometry
- body width and stroke mass;
- angularity / corner radius;
- counter size and openness;
- vertical compression / horizontal expansion;
- baseline alignment and cap-height consistency;
- inter-letter gap and overlap;
- outline weight relative to fill;
- optional 3D extrusion as a separate layer, not an assumed feature.

### Composition and execution conditions
- high legibility at a glance;
- low structural complexity as a deliberate choice;
- rapid construction with limited pre-planning;
- strong silhouette and stable negative spaces;
- separate controls for wall piece, panel, wholecar and wholetrain;
- black/silver as a historically observed palette option, never a mandatory regional identity.

### Practice taxonomy
Keep these distinct in the data model:
- tag / handstyle;
- throw-up;
- piece;
- bombing (activity / distribution practice);
- panel;
- wholecar;
- wholetrain;
- legal wall / hall-of-fame work.

A tag is not a simplified piece; a wholecar is not merely a wider canvas; bombing is not a lettering style.

## 9. Witten — Bunkerwelt as a regional hip-hop anchor

Witten must be treated as a priority Ruhrgebiet research node, not merely as a small neighbouring town. The **Bunkerwelt** matters because it was a local meeting, rehearsal, party, jam and recording environment through which Witten's hip-hop culture gained wider reach. It is an important cultural context for graffiti writing even where a direct graffiti production history has not yet been documented.

### What the sources currently support

- A local-history account published by *Mapping Hip Hop Culture in the Ruhr Area* describes the Bunkerwelt as originating in the 1990s around Dike Uchegbu (DIKE), who rented a rehearsal room in an old bunker in Witten-Annen. It became a place for hip-hop parties and jam sessions, bringing together rappers, musicians and other artists. The account says the bunker was no longer in use after the turn of the millennium and describes rent increases around 2000 as a factor in leaving the premises. This is a secondary account and should be cross-checked with interviews and contemporary material.
- A 2026 announcement for a talk at the Märkisches Museum says historian Dr. Thomas Throckmorton researched the emergence and national significance of Witten's Bunkerwelt scene using varied sources and background conversations with key participants. This is a promising lead for a more robust local history, not itself a transcript of the talk.
- A Bunkerwelt profile lists a network of participants and associated acts including Terence Chill, OnAnOn, Lakmann One, Flipstar, Till Tomorrow / Creutzfeld & Jakob, Dike D, El9Six, Pahel and Das TagTeam. Treat this as a scene-network lead; verify individual roles and dates against first-person sources.
- Witten's 2021 *Bring the Beat Back* documentation explicitly describes the Ruhrgebiet and Witten as nationally known for their flourishing 1990s hip-hop scene and jams. The event brought together DJing, graffiti writing, rap and breakdance, with participants from Witten and other cities. This supports a broad, interconnected hip-hop ecology, but does not by itself prove that the Bunkerwelt hosted every graffiti event or that all listed artists were graffiti writers.

### Keep three Witten histories distinct

1. **Bunkerwelt / hip-hop network:** a social and cultural infrastructure for rap, music, parties, jams and cross-disciplinary exchange.
2. **Witten graffiti / writing practice:** requires its own dated evidence: writers, crews, pieces, photos, jams, walls, magazines and oral histories. Do not infer a local graffiti style from the Bunkerwelt's musical reputation.
3. **Contemporary legal walls and urban art:** UrbanArtWitten documents the Crengeldanz Hall of Fame, opened in 2019, with a long public wall and supported youth / community activity. This is a contemporary legal infrastructure, not evidence of 1980s/1990s writing history.

### Why this matters to NIGHTSHIFT

The Bunkerwelt is a useful case study in how a scene is built by more than lettering alone:
- physical meeting and rehearsal spaces;
- jam sessions and mixed-discipline events;
- peer learning and informal skill exchange;
- local identity and regional / national collaboration;
- the transition from local infrastructure to wider cultural networks.

For the research model, add a **scene infrastructure** layer alongside writers, crews, cities, styles and works. Record venues, events, collectives, media and their dated relationships. For the editor, this is contextual and educational content; it must not be converted into a visual “Bunkerwelt style” preset without independent visual evidence.

### Witten research backlog

- Obtain / inspect the 2026 Throckmorton talk materials or recording, if published.
- Identify the bunker building and establish its dates and use from reliable local-history sources; avoid publishing sensitive access details for disused premises.
- Find first-person interviews with DIKE, Terence Chill, Lakmann, Flipstar, El9Six and other participants, preserving exact role descriptions.
- Search contemporary flyers, jam posters, local press, photographs and recordings from the 1990s.
- Build a separate Witten graffiti chronology; identify writers and dated works rather than assuming the Bunkerwelt music network equals the writing network.
- Compare the Bunkerwelt network with Dortmund's TUF network and wider Ruhrgebiet exchange.

## 10. New city evidence — Essen and Witten (source pass 2026-11-14)

### 10.1 Essen — early origins are contested; the 1990s leave traceable evidence

A TU Dortmund seminar project, *Mapping Hip Hop Culture in the Ruhr Area*, interviewed local artists and organisers about Essen's graffiti history. Its most useful result is that it **does not pretend the origin story is settled**:
- The writer / graphic designer The Top Notch identifies Essen-Steele as the starting point and says the first local writers came from there.
- Bud MH offers a different account, locating the beginnings more broadly along the S-Bahn route.
- The researchers explicitly say they could not verify either claim at the time of the seminar.
- A surviving piece at the Hünninghausener Weg youth centre is described as dating from **1992**. This is a promising dated object lead; the original photograph, current survival and exact inscription/date attribution need independent confirmation.
- The account describes The Top Notch's later network in Steele and the initiative that led to Essen's legal-surface programme around **2010**.
- The same article dates the start of the Hafendampf festival to **2013**, with invited writers from inside and outside the Ruhrgebiet.

**Research implication:** Essen should not be represented as a simple extension of Dortmund Style. Its earliest local geography is disputed, and the available evidence points to a Steele / S-Bahn corridor question that needs oral histories and dated images. The 1992 youth-centre piece may become an anchor record if verified.

### 10.2 Witten — first-person evidence connects tagging, graffiti and Bunkerwelt's social network

A 2005 *Hip Hop Wont Stop* interview with Terence Chill is useful because it is first-person and specific:
- He says he began as a skater, tagged and painted a few pieces before turning to the microphone.
- He explicitly says he would not call himself a writer. Preserve that self-description; do not promote him to a graffiti-writer record.
- He describes meeting Dike around 1994 and forming Reimwärtz with Paul; after Dike went solo, Terence and Paul continued as OnAnOn.
- He says the Bunkerwelt jams were full, but visitors did not all come from Witten; he names Dike, OnAnOn, Creutzfeld & Jakob, Os and Mess as Witten-associated acts, and says part of RAG had lived in Witten for a time.
- His account of how he found the Bunkerwelt is comic / deliberately absurd (including the line that he “invented” it). It must not be used as a literal factual account of the venue's founding.

This is a valuable reminder that the Bunkerwelt was not a closed local clique: it was a point in a wider Ruhrgebiet network. But the interview also reinforces our evidence boundary: a musician's past tagging and pieces are not proof of professional or sustained writer activity.

The official WERK°STADT documentation of *Bring the Beat Back* (2021) names Witten-associated graffiti participants including SKOR82, Akzent One, Lucky Lou and EI9six, alongside artists from Bochum, Cologne, Bremen, Berlin and New York. It explicitly frames the event as reconnecting the four hip-hop elements and recalls Witten / Ruhrgebiet's 1990s jam culture. This is evidence of contemporary scene continuity and cross-city participation, not a substitute for 1990s event documentation.

### 10.3 Bochum — an early visual lead, but not yet a scene chronology

A digitised academic publication contains a captioned photograph titled **“Graffiti in Bochum 1982”**, credited to Grajetzky et al. (2004). This is an important lead because it may push the regional visual record earlier than the mid-1980s general chronology. However, a caption alone does not establish whether the image depicts US-style Writing, political graffiti, a mural, or another form of wall inscription. The original publication and photograph context must be inspected before classifying it as Style Writing.

A Bochum municipal youth-services report documents the legal-wall programme's policy origin in 2001 and reports 26 available free surfaces in the report's 2020 context. This belongs in the contemporary institutional history, not the early-scene chronology.
