# NYC Graffiti–Hardcore Crossover: People, Bands and Evidence 02

**Scope:** evidence-led follow-up to the 1980s–1990s New York graffiti / hardcore crossover dossier. This document prioritizes named individuals, band participation, crew affiliations, artwork and publication credits. It is not a complete scene roster.

**Editorial rule:** distinguish (1) writing practice, (2) band membership, (3) crew affiliation, (4) artwork/graphic credit, and (5) shared social/venue networks. One does not automatically prove another.

## 1. Strongly documented individual links

### Mackie Jayson — HYPE / HYPER; Frontline and later bands
- i-D's 2017 interview-based feature identifies Mackie Jayson as HYPE/HYPER, affiliated with RTW (Rolling Thunder Writers), and a drummer in Frontline, described as a 1981 NYHC band made up of graffiti writers. It also lists later musical work with Cro-Mags, Bad Brains, The Icemen, Shelter and Hazen St.
- Freddy Alva's 2017 No Echo companion mixtape article lists Frontline members as HYPE, ME 62, RATE 5, RAGE and NOAH, and notes later musical paths for some members.
- **Assessment:** strong secondary/practitioner-source support for a direct writer-to-band-member bridge. The exact band lineup should be treated as a dated snapshot, not a permanent roster.

### REVS — Adam 12
- Alva's band-by-band listing identifies REVS, FRIPP and ENO in early-1980s NYHC band Adam 12, and describes REVS as subsequently becoming a prominent NYC graffiti writer.
- **Assessment:** useful direct crossover lead, but the article does not provide a full chronology of when each member wrote or played. Preserve the band spelling and alias as presented by the source; verify against period flyers/demos before assigning exact dates.

### TMR writers — Terminal Confusion and Second 2 None
- Alva's listing says Terminal Confusion formed from the remains of STD (Skinheads Til Death), released a first demo in 1986, and featured VOYER, NIM, MAR and BUNK, all affiliated with northern Queens crew TMR (The Mob Rules).
- It separately identifies Second 2 None as TMR-related, naming BUNK, ZZ, ACID and TRETAL.
- The same account credits BUNK with publishing two early NYHC fanzines containing graffiti: *The Village Noize* and *Yes Zista*.
- **Assessment:** a particularly valuable example of the crossover as an infrastructure: band participation, crew ties and print culture overlap. Exact issue dates and original zine scans remain a verification task.

### SANE / SMITH — DIE 116 and shared visual production
- Alva's listing describes DIE 116 as named after an alternate tagging name associated with SANE SMITH and names NATZ and CREEP in the band.
- The article also reproduces a DIE 116 piece by SANE SMITH and credits the *Urban Styles* cover art to STAK TFP and its handstyle to JERE DMS.
- i-D's 2017 feature describes SANE and SMITH as brothers, notes collaborations with HUSH, JERE and NATZ, and discusses their graffiti activity.
- **Assessment:** supports overlapping people, band naming and visual authorship. It does not establish that every person named was a band member, crew member or co-author of every image.

### Dmize — JERE, MQ and the Queens social network
- In a 2014 first-person interview, Dmize guitarist Rob “Beto” Rosario says he and Hoya met Richie, Jere and Chiqui through graffiti writer MQ around late 1987 or 1988. He says Richie and Jere joined in 1988–89, and Jere left after the first demo, with Chiqui taking his place.
- The same interview describes Dmize as a Corona/Jackson Heights band and acknowledges a connection to the neighborhood's DMS scene while declining to overstate the band's involvement.
- A separate first-person interview with Chiqui Rodriguez confirms he knew MQ and describes his own limited-area writing activity in Jackson Heights.
- Powerhouse Arena's 2017 event page identifies JERE as a DMS co-founder, a writer since 1984 and the original singer for Dmize.
- **Assessment:** high-value oral-history evidence for interpersonal routes into a band. Keep the timeline attributed to Beto and distinguish JERE's first-demo-era role from Chiqui's later role. Do not infer a complete DMS membership list from these interviews.

### SMOG — Crumbsuckers imagery, RIS and ROT
- i-D's 2017 feature says SMOG began bombing trains in 1986, attended early CBGB hardcore matinees, used a character from NYHC band Crumbsuckers in his pieces, belonged to RIS (Rocking It Suckers), and founded ROT (Ratz On Tracks). It says both crews included a number of people from the NYHC scene.
- Powerhouse Arena independently identifies SMOG as a writer who goes back to early CBGB hardcore matinees and includes him in the *Urban Styles* discussion.
- **Assessment:** strong evidence for a visual borrowing and scene overlap as reported by a specialist feature and book event. The specific Crumbsuckers character should be identified from the reproduced artwork before any in-game visual reference is considered.

### FCEE / Chaka Malik — Stagedive, Burn, Skarhead and Dmize
- i-D identifies FCEE as a Queens native and original member of Chaka Malik's Stagedive skateboarding team in the late 1980s. It describes the skate / graffiti / hardcore overlap and says his *Urban Styles* pieces represent Burn, Skarhead and Dmize.
- Alva's mixtape article identifies Chaka Malik from Burn as EXPO in the book's spoken-word introduction.
- **Assessment:** direct evidence for a three-way skate, graffiti and hardcore network, plus later band-related artwork. A band representation artwork is not proof that FCEE played in that band.

## 2. Visual culture and print evidence

- i-D identifies demo covers, show flyers and band logos for Crumbsuckers, Outburst and Pagan Babies as examples of graffiti-derived visual language entering NYHC's visual identity by the mid-to-late 1980s.
- Alva's *Urban Styles* companion article gives explicit production credits: STAK TFP (cover art), Orlando Arce (layout) and JERE DMS (handstyle).
- The same article reproduces a DIE 116 piece by SANE SMITH and a 1982 CBGB image of Frontline members.
- Andrew Monserrate's first-person No Echo interview documents a separate pathway: he made artwork for his band Stand Proud and describes producing flyers, zine covers, band T-shirts, posters, tattoos and stencils for people in the scene.
- **Caution:** Monserrate's account supports the existence of a broader DIY graphics ecology, but does not by itself prove a graffiti crew affiliation. Likewise, a graffiti-influenced flyer is not evidence that its designer was a writer unless separately credited.

## 3. SEEN / Richard “Richie” Mirando — separate profile, crossover not established here

- Gallery biographies and artist profiles identify SEEN as Richard Mirando, born in 1961 in the Bronx, associated with United Artists (UA), and active on New York subway trains from 1973. One profile identifies UA associates including Pjay, Duster, Sin and his brother Mad.
- An Independent interview from 2014 describes his subway painting period as spanning approximately sixteen years and his later move toward tattooing and gallery work. A 2016 interview also identifies his UA affiliation and early subway work.
- **What this research does not establish:** the sources inspected for this dossier do not demonstrate SEEN as a member of the NYHC bands or crews discussed above, nor a direct collaboration with the named crossover actors. This is an evidence boundary, not a claim that no social contact or undocumented connection existed.
- **Archive treatment:** SEEN belongs in the core NYC writer/artist archive as a major Bronx / UA figure. Keep his entry distinct from the NYHC crossover graph unless a specific primary or first-person source establishes a link.

## 5. NIGHTSHIFT design implications — explicitly inference

1. **Model crossover as a network, not a single crew flag.** A person may have separate edges for writing, band membership, crew affiliation, artwork, skate team, zine editing and venue participation.
2. **Time-bound every relationship.** “Played in Frontline in 1981” and “later played in Cro-Mags” are different edges with different periods; never flatten them into a timeless membership list.
3. **Keep visual credits separate from musical roles.** “Designed a flyer for” or “painted a band tribute” must not be rendered as “member of band.”
4. **Use scene infrastructure as environmental storytelling.** Rehearsal rooms, matinees, small venues, photocopied zines, demo tapes, skate teams and neighborhood friendships can connect systems without inventing a single unified subculture.
5. **Treat alias uncertainty as first-class data.** Preserve source spelling and attribution; do not silently normalize aliases, crew names or band lineups.
6. **SEEN should be represented as a core historical writer profile, not forced into the NYHC crossover storyline without evidence.**
