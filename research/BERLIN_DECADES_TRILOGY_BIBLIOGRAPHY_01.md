# DECADES — Berlin Graffiti Writing 1990–2020: Trilogy Bibliography 01

**Research date:** 14 November 2026  
**Status:** publisher-level bibliography; listed names are attributed portrait subjects, not a complete census or proof of crew membership.

## Series overview

Peter Stelzig's *DECADES* trilogy documents Berlin graffiti writing in three ten-year periods. The publisher frames the books as a combination of large-scale photographic documentation and personal texts by writers, with train writing a major but not exclusive focus. Across the three volumes, the series offers a rare chronological source for changing Berlin conditions, participants and visual practice. [BD-01; BD-02; BD-03]

## Volume 1 — 1990–2000

- Published: 2021
- Publisher: Racoon Books
- Author: Peter Stelzig
- Extent: 324 pages; over 860 photographs
- Languages: German and English
- Scope: post-Wall Berlin scene, train writing, walls and private photographs.
- Portrait subjects listed by publisher/catalogue: BAS2, BOY, CATS, CHILL, CHIO, DEON, DISNE, DMB, DRUM, FOK, INKA, KEL, KMER, KAMEL, LYTE, ODEM, POET62, REW, RUZD, DSYER, SEKD, SHEK, SOME, STEAK, TFB, TOK, TORCH, TOWN, VDS and WEX, among others. [BD-01]

**Historical reading:** the publisher describes the fall of the Wall as a catalyst for a new phase of Berlin graffiti. Treat this as the volume's framing, not as a claim that Berlin graffiti began in 1989/90.

## Volume 2 — 2000–2010

- Published: November 2022
- Publisher: Racoon Books
- Author: Peter Stelzig
- Extent: 328 pages; over 760 photographs
- Languages: German and English
- Portrait subjects listed by publisher: CHIKA, CO2, DIKE, DROW, EHSONE, EXOT, FLS, GATE, HK, KAMEL43, KAZE, METRO, MR.IX, MRN, NERZ, PACK, PAST2, RELAX, RINO, ROGER, SABE, SMASH, SKAR, SKEY, SPAIR, TFZ and TIER; plus a P. BERG special with a text by MC BOMBER. [BD-02]

**Historical reading:** the publisher describes the 2000s as a period of transition and pluralization after the “Wildstyle” dominance of the 1990s, with new participants particularly from eastern Berlin, more train writing, stronger repression and the disappearance of many street pieces amid urban redevelopment. These are the publisher's synthesis and should be compared with individual writer testimony and other urban histories.

- Languages: German and English

**Historical reading:** the publisher emphasizes international migration into Berlin, the city's status as a destination for train writers, increased visibility and documentation, and changing technical and competitive conditions. Keep these claims attributed to the publisher until independently compared with other sources.

## Cross-volume observations

1. The three volumes provide a structured decade-by-decade entry point, but each is a curated portrait selection, not an exhaustive roster.
2. Train writing is prominent throughout; walls and other forms remain present and should not be erased from the scene history.
3. Personal texts are primary/first-person testimony for the writer's own experiences, not automatically independent corroboration of every date or crew relation.
4. The publisher's narrative about style pluralization in the 2000s is a useful research hypothesis, not a universal law or a generator preset.
5. A person appearing in one volume does not establish formal membership in a crew named elsewhere in the same book.
6. Visual material is copyrighted. A bibliographic description or book preview does not grant reproduction rights.
