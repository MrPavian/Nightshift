# Writers, Crews & Cultural Influences — Initial Research Register

> This is not a ranking or a list of "inventors". Entries are selected because institutional archives and documented oral histories provide a starting point. Individual attribution must remain tied to the source and its specific claim.

## Early New York writing and Bronx oral histories

### TAKI 183
- **Context:** New York writer whose name became widely known after a 1971 New York Times article.
- **Source-backed note:** The SCHIRN overview says the article helped trigger a wider wave of pseudonyms and imitation across the city. It does not establish TAKI 183 as the first writer.
- **Archive use:** early name-writing, visibility, public attention and the difference between a famous example and an origin myth.
- **Source:** Katrin Görler, SCHIRN Kunsthalle, 2013: https://www.schirn.de/schirnmag/eine-kurze-geschichte-des-stylewriting/. [B]

### ROCKY 184
- **Context:** Early writer associated in the Bronx County Historical Society collection description with the Writer's Corner in Washington Heights in the late 1960s.
- **Archive use:** oral-history testimony and early scene networks.
- **Source:** Bronx County Historical Society oral history, presented by Google Arts & Culture: https://artsandculture.google.com/story/-AVhMvHuARRJQA. [A]

### STAFF 161 and the Ebony Dukes
- **Context:** The same collection describes STAFF 161 as founder of the Ebony Dukes, a crew of writers from Hewitt Place who began in the late 1960s/early 1970s.
- **Archive use:** crew formation and neighborhood context; attribute the claim to the collection description.
- **Source:** Bronx County Historical Society oral-history collection: https://artsandculture.google.com/story/-AVhMvHuARRJQA. [A]

### RIFF 170 and INDS
- **Context:** The collection describes RIFF 170 as a member of INDS, one of the early Bronx crews, active especially in the early and mid-1970s, and influential in lettering development.
- **Archive use:** early crew networks and the social circulation of lettering approaches.
- **Source:** Bronx County Historical Society oral-history collection: https://artsandculture.google.com/story/-AVhMvHuARRJQA. [A]

### BUTCH 2 / The Fantastic Partners
- **Context:** The Bronx County Historical Society describes BUTCH 2 as a member of The Fantastic Partners and a highly regarded early Bronx style pioneer.
- **Archive use:** practitioner testimony and style development; avoid unsupported "first" or "greatest" labels.
- **Source:** Bronx County Historical Society oral-history collection: https://artsandculture.google.com/story/-AVhMvHuARRJQA. [A]

## Documented subway-era artists and image-makers

The Bronx Museum's Henry Chalfant exhibition archive names Dondi, Futura, Lady Pink, Lee Quiñones, Zephyr, Blade, Crash, DAZE, Dez, Kel, Mare, SEEN, Skeme and T-Kid among writers represented in Chalfant's documentation of subway work. This establishes them as subjects in the photographic record, not a complete biography or a ranking of influence. [A]

The Museum of the City of New York's Martin Wong collection highlights Rammellzee, Lee Quiñones, Lady Pink and Futura 2000 among artists whose work documents the transition from street/subway practice into canvases, paper and institutional exhibition contexts. [A]

## Research protocol for future profiles

Every expanded person/crew profile should include:
- preferred name / alias and known variants;
- dates only where sourced;
- borough/neighborhood/crew affiliations only where sourced;
- first-person testimony or archival references where available;
- specific, attributable contribution rather than vague praise;
- dated visual examples with image rights/provenance;
- a note distinguishing influence claims from the curator's interpretation.

Avoid copying recognizable signatures or recreating a living artist's exact style. Use profiles to understand broad historical developments and design problems, not to turn a person into a preset.
