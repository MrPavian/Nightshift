# The Clean Train campaign and 1989

> New York's transit authorities fought painted trains for years; in spring 1989 the subway fleet was declared graffiti-free. Writing culture did not end there.

## Introduction

The Museum of the City of New York reports a spring 1989 declaration that the subway fleet was free of graffiti [c1]. The statement concerns the transit fleet, not the end of writing on walls, in books, galleries, photography or abroad [c2].

## Why it matters

Writing and the city's campaign to suppress it are related but analytically distinct histories [NYC-008]. The decline of painted subway trains did not end writing culture [NYC-012].

## Context and development

After the subway era, writing continued across walls, freight, legal sites, paper, galleries, print media and later digital networks [c3].

## Not the same as

- **The end of graffiti** — 1989 marks a transit-era change, not the end of a culture [NYC-012].

## Evidence notes

The 1989 milestone is attributed to the museum's account; independent primary MTA corroboration is still open in the research library.
