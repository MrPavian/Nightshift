# Paris and Seine-Saint-Denis

> Hip-hop arrived through radio and television in the 1980s; Paris writing then grew from the relationship between the city and its suburbs.

## Introduction

A City of Paris / Plaine Commune publication describes the 1980s spread of hip-hop culture in France through independent radio and the television programme *H.I.P H.O.P.*, early crew activity in north-east Paris and the wastelands of La Plaine Saint-Denis, and 1984 block parties at Stalingrad [c1].

## Why it matters

Scholarship frames Paris and its suburbs as a major centre of graffiti and street art from the early 1980s and stresses the geography of Paris and its banlieues, including populations shaped by postcolonial migration [c2]. The scene is not a copy of New York; its geography and social context made it distinct.

## Context and development

Paris also has much older traditions of public writing: a peer-reviewed study documents street writing in late nineteenth-century Paris [c3] (see [[concept.public-inscription|public inscription]]).

More primary oral histories are needed; current evidence is a city publication and a scholarly chapter.
