# Lady Pink

> A New York writer of the subway era whose lettering, film work and later murals are documented in her own words and in scholarship.

## Introduction

In a 2013 interview published by the National Endowment for the Arts, Lady Pink (Sandra Fabara) recalls beginning graffiti in 1979 and first painting subway cars [c1]. She was sixteen when included in the 1980 Fashion MODA exhibition *Graffiti Art Success for America* [c1]. The same interview identifies her with the crews The Cool Five (TC5) and The Public Animals (TPA) [c2].

## Why it matters

Scholarship on the film *Wild Style* analyses her lettering in the opening sequence and records her role in directing other writers on set [c3]. This treats her as a writer with technical expertise and artistic judgement, not only as a visible woman in the scene.

## Context and development

Research notes that even influential writers such as Lady Pink have received comparatively little academic attention [c4] — one reason this archive places her in the main history ([[history.nyc-emergence|New York]], [[history.institutional-recognition|exhibitions]]) rather than in a separate corner.

## Not the same as

- **“The first woman writer”** (→ concept.visibility-and-archival-gaps) — women took part from the early period; the sources support her importance as one of the earliest women with major visibility in New York, not a “first” title [c5].

## Evidence notes

Dates and crew memberships come from her own retrospective testimony and should be read as her recollection. No images of her work are embedded; rights would need item-level clearance.
