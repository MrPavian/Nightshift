# Tag

> A writer's name written quickly by hand — a practice with its own gesture, rhythm and social function, not just a small version of a piece.

## Introduction

Tags, throw-ups and pieces are described in ethnographic research as common forms of New York writing [c1]. They coexist; a tag is not merely an early stage that a writer leaves behind [c2].

## How to recognize it

A single line of letters, written in one continuous rhythm, repeated in many places. Its quality lies in the hand: proportion, flow and consistency (see [[practice.handstyle|handstyle]]).

## Context and development

Press attention to tags in the early 1970s made the practice publicly visible [NYC-005], and competition for visibility helped make individual style important [NYC-006]. In Philadelphia, local tag traditions such as the Wicked developed their own forms ([[scene.philadelphia|Philadelphia]]).

## Not the same as

- **A small piece** — a tag is its own name-writing practice with distinct gesture and purpose [c2].
- **Gang markings** (→ scene.philadelphia) — they shared urban space, but research treats them as different practices.

## In NIGHTSHIFT

The game does not reproduce real tags or signatures. Original, synthetic lettering may study rhythm and flow [c3].
