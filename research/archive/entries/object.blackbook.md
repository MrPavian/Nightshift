# Blackbook

> A book for drawing, collecting and keeping letters — a working space and, later, an archive.

## Introduction

Surviving books show how important paper became for writing. NYU's Martin Wong Papers hold more than 30 sketchbooks together with graffiti tag-books, drawings and photographs [c1]. The Museum of the City of New York describes how Martin Wong collected writers' work, letters and periodicals [c2].

## Why it matters

A blackbook can record process: variants, revisions, exchanges and other writers' contributions. Uses differ between writers and settings [c3].

## Not the same as

- **A finished wall or train piece** — a book may hold sketches, tests and exchanges rather than final work.
- **A museum catalogue** — an archive holding proves that books exist, not that every statement inside them is accurate [c4].

## In NIGHTSHIFT

Saving and comparing versions of a letter in the editor is a design analogy to working in a book, not a historical claim [c5].
