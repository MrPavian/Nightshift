# São Paulo and pixação

> Pixação is São Paulo's own system of public name writing, with tall, angular letters and its own rules — not a variant of New York style.

## Introduction

Research describes pixação as a locally developed form of public name writing that became visible around 1980 and spread widely in the 1990s and 2000s. A peer-reviewed article describes its graphic language as vertically elongated letters built from straight lines and points, often called *tag reto* [c1].

## Why it matters

Explanations for the look of tag reto differ — the city's tall buildings and lettering from heavy metal and punk records are both proposed — and these remain interpretations, not settled origins [c1]. A University of São Paulo dissertation argues that the city's groups have their own roots, visual examples, rules and systems of internal valuation [c2].

## Terminology and variants

*Pixação* and *grafite* are often contrasted in Brazil, but research challenges treating them as sealed-off practices; processes and forms can overlap [c3].
