# Public inscription before and beside modern writing

> People wrote in public space long before the 1960s. Modern writing is one chapter, not the beginning.

## Introduction

Political writing, gang markings, neighbourhood name writing and mural traditions all predate New York subway writing, and they must not be merged into one practice [NYC-001]. In late nineteenth-century Paris, for example, a peer-reviewed study documents a culture of public writing in the streets [c1].

## Why it matters

Saying “graffiti began in New York” erases these histories. The archive therefore speaks of the *emergence of modern New York hip-hop writing*, not of the invention of graffiti [c2].

## Context and development

Several local traditions had their own paths: Philadelphia's name writing of the 1960s ([[scene.philadelphia|Philadelphia]]) and, in Amsterdam, a punk-linked name-writing culture before hip-hop writing became widely known there [c3].

## Not the same as

- **Modern writing** (→ concept.writing-and-graffiti) — a specific, name-centred culture with its own codes.
