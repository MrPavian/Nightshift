# Amsterdam

> A punk- and squatter-linked name-writing culture existed in Amsterdam before New York-style hip-hop writing became widely known there.

## Introduction

Graduation research at the Reinwardt Academie describes an Amsterdam name-writing culture emerging around 1977, largely among young people connected to punk; it is based on literature and ten in-depth interviews [c1]. The study reports that films and books about US graffiti later inspired a new local style, and that Amsterdam hip-hop graffiti developed as a recognisably different phase [c2].

## Why it matters

Amsterdam shows that a local writing phase could precede the wide adoption of New York-style writing [c3]. The Dutch Graffiti Library's punk archive project describes late-1970s punk and squatter tagging followed by a generation seeking city-wide visibility [c4].

## Context and development

The same local research records a scene concern that the history of graffiti should be preserved by writers and their communities, not only by formal institutions [c2].

## Evidence notes

Based on one heritage thesis, interviews and a specialist archive; it is not a complete census of the city.
