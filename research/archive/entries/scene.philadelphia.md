# Philadelphia

> One of the earliest documented centres of modern name writing in the United States, with its own vocabulary and hand traditions — not a footnote to New York.

## Introduction

Research places the emergence of name writing in Philadelphia in the 1960s and discusses CORNBREAD's activity around 1965 in North Philadelphia, with COOL EARL and TOPCAT 126 active from 1966 [c1]. A public-history account describes Black teenagers who called themselves wall writers and situates the movement between about 1965 and 1985 [c2]. The evidence does not support naming one first writer or an uncontested date [c3].

## Why it matters

Philadelphia and New York developed related but distinct writing cultures in the 1960s [NYC-003]. New York's subway and media later made its visual language dominant, which hid the distinct Philadelphia tradition [c4].

## Context and development

Ethnographic research challenges the idea that Philadelphia writing simply grew out of gangs. It describes writing as a culture with its own rules and practices [c5]. Local hand traditions include long, tall forms and the Wicked, which researchers describe as exaggerated, elongated and partly meant for insiders to read [c6]. Fieldwork records *whip* as a local word for the energy and continuity of a flowing hand [c7].

## Terminology and variants

*Wicked* / *Wicket*, *tall hand* and *whip* are local terms with their own scope. They are not universal graffiti vocabulary.

## Not the same as

- **The place of “the first tag”** — Philadelphia is a scene with its own history, not a single origin event [c3].
- **Gang marking** — overlapping urban context, but a different practice [c5].
