# Sequential train photography

> Photographing a moving train section by section so that a whole painted car survives as a connected record.

## Introduction

Henry Chalfant developed a sequential technique for photographing subway trains; the Bronx Museum describes these photographs as documenting the emergence of the form and its transformation into an international phenomenon [c1]. The Smithsonian archive records his move to New York in 1973, the method and his collaboration with writers [c2].

## Why it matters

Painted cars rarely survived. Photography preserved evidence of temporary work and helped knowledge travel [NYC-009].

## Not the same as

- **The work itself** — sequential photographs keep a car's composition together, but cannot reproduce its scale, movement, colour conditions or audience [c3].

## Evidence notes

Individual photographs need item-level metadata and rights review before any reuse. This archive embeds none of them.
