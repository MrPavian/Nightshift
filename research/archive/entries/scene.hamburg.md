# Hamburg and its archive

> Hamburg's writing history from the 1980s survives partly because a writer became its archivist — and a university made that archive a research subject.

## Introduction

The University of Hamburg's Centre for the Study of Manuscript Cultures (CSMC) describes Mirko Reisser, known as DAIM, as part of Hamburg's graffiti scene from its tentative beginnings in the 1980s and as an archivist of the city's graffiti history [c1]. Its research project *Graffiti: Ephemeral Inscriptions in Urban Space — Style Writing in Hamburg in the 1980s and 1990s* (2019–2023) studies his private archive, describing him as a key figure in the Hamburg scene since 1989 [c2].

## Why it matters

Writing is ephemeral: walls are buffed and trains cleaned. The project studies how style writing is produced, transformed, preserved and destroyed, and it treats the archive itself — including a digital database — as a research object [c2]. This connects to [[object.blackbook|blackbooks]] and to [[history.institutional-recognition|collections]] elsewhere.

## Not the same as

- **One writer's story** — the archive documents a city's scene, and what was collected shapes what is remembered ([[concept.visibility-and-archival-gaps|gaps in the record]]).
