# Dortmund Style

> A regional lettering tendency known for extremely blocky, clear letters — readable at a distance, built from strong, simple masses.

## Introduction

The KWI Essen article describes the Dortmund Style as notable for its extremely blocky and clear letters, and says it found many fans and imitators in the rest of the Ruhrgebiet and internationally [c1].

## How to recognize it

As a construction tendency, “blocky and clear” means broad, solid letter bodies, legibility kept intact and little decoration that hides the letter [c1]. These are tendencies, not rules; individual writers vary within them [c2].

## Why it matters

The style shows that regional identity can live in construction — skeleton, mass and spacing — rather than in effects. That makes it a useful case for studying [[construction.letter-anatomy|letter anatomy]] and [[construction.negative-space|negative space]].

## Not the same as

- **A preset** — the style is a shared tendency with many individual hands; NIGHTSHIFT will not reduce it to one button or copy any writer's letters [c2].
- **Blockbuster lettering in general** — large block letters exist in many scenes; the Dortmund Style is a specific regional history.

## Evidence notes

Published descriptions are short. A proper construction study needs several dated examples from different writers; the research library lists this as open work.
