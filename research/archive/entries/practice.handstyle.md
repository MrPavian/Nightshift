# Handstyle

> A writer's personal way of forming letters by hand: rhythm, proportion, joins and the habits that make one hand recognisable.

## Introduction

During the 1970s, New York writers increasingly developed differentiated handstyles alongside more elaborate compositions [NYC-006]. In Philadelphia, fieldwork describes *whip* — continuity, speed, joints and the trace of a flowing hand — as a local way of talking about the energy in letter strokes [c1].

## Why it matters

Handstyle is where personal style begins. It also shows why a closed outline cannot tell the whole story: the same silhouette can come from very different movements [c2].

## Not the same as

- **A letterform style** — handstyle is a gestural practice; named letterform styles describe construction (→ construction.letter-anatomy).
- **Handwriting in general** — writers deliberately shape and repeat their hand as a public identity.

## In NIGHTSHIFT

NIGHTSHIFT treats handstyle as gesture and movement as well as visible shape, and does not infer stroke order from a closed contour [c2].
