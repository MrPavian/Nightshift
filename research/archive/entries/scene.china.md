# China: Beijing, Shanghai, Chengdu

> Not one national scene but several city histories, each shaped by its own context and by artists looking for a Chinese visual language.

## Introduction

*Graffiti in China* (University of Bologna / 1088press, 2024) is based on research and interviews in Beijing, Shanghai and Chengdu, and its editors state that these cases cannot represent the whole Chinese scene [c1].

## Context and development

The book describes three different urban contexts: Beijing, a political and administrative centre; Shanghai, an international and commercial city; and Chengdu, a regional centre showing that development cannot be reduced to the two largest cities [c2]. It documents artists seeking a Chinese visual language through references to characters, calligraphy, traditional painting and literature, as well as travel and exchange with foreign writers [c3]. Among the Shanghai cases it includes Tin.G, associated with the China Graffiti Girls [c4].

## Why it matters

China shows circulation as reinterpretation: international contact combined with local scripts and art traditions ([[concept.circulation-and-adaptation|circulation]]).
