# Da kukse wa (1987) — 2020 Reprint, Access and Bibliographic Verification (W02.26)

**Date:** 2026-11-14  
**Purpose:** improve the access and edition record for Dortmund's early graffiti publication. This dossier records publication metadata and access routes; it does not claim page-level visual inspection.

## 1. Original publication and editorial provenance

The author Marianne Brentzel's own website identifies *Da kukse wa — Dortmunder Graffiti* as published in 1987, with photographs by Hubert Moormann, interviews with CHINTZ, SHARK, CHANA and ZODIAK, and texts by Brentzel. Her sample excerpt includes a contemporary newspaper controversy around the turn of 1986/87 and a small period glossary defining blackbook, character, getting fame, piece, tag and whole car.

- Author's official page: https://www.mariannebrentzel.de/da-kukse-wa.html
- Author's excerpt: https://www.mariannebrentzel.de/da-kukse-wa-leseprobe.html
- ILOVEGRAFFITI retrospective (2015): https://ilovegraffiti.de/blog/2015/03/02/buch-da-kukse-wa-graffiti-in-dortmund-1987/

The author's page confirms the book is out of print in its original edition. It does not provide a page count or ISBN in the accessible text.

Institutional page: https://dortmunder-u.de/i-was-here-360/

**Evidence boundary:** 36 pages and “identical except for the preface” are seller-supplied metadata, not yet verified by collation. The institutional page supports the exhibition/reprint provenance, not every seller claim. A current shop stock label can change.

## 3. What this unlocks for NIGHTSHIFT research

This is the strongest practical access route so far for the early Dortmund scene corpus because it avoids reliance on an unverified third-party scan. A legitimate 2020 reprint can support a page-by-page research ledger, subject to applicable copyright and permissions.

When inspected, record:
- page number and whether the image is a tag, throw-up, piece, character, wall or train;
- visible support and composition, without inferring location/date from appearance alone;
- caption, credited writer and photographer only when printed;
- whether an image is a contemporary photograph, later reproduction or editorial illustration;
- rights holder and permitted use, separately from the right to view or purchase the book.

The author's excerpt is text, not a substitute for the full visual corpus. Do not treat the period glossary as a complete or universal taxonomy.

## 4. Historical context, carefully bounded

The author excerpt documents a contemporary newspaper debate around 1986/87 and quotes the police president's response to reports of graffiti. It also references unemployment, coal/steel job losses, an environmental scandal in Dorstfeld and youth apprenticeship/unemployment issues as part of the contemporary social setting. This is valuable primary-author text, but the specific social statistics and newspaper statements require their own original sources before being repeated as independently verified facts.

The book's period glossary gives one local printed vocabulary snapshot. It can inform the BLACKBOOK ARCHIVE's historical terminology notes, but should not be converted into hard-coded universal definitions.
