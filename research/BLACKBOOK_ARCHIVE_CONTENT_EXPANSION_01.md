## Entry 07 — Writing and graffiti
- ID: practice.writing
- Type: Practice / cultural activity
- Short definition: In New York writing culture, “writing” is a practitioner term for putting one's name or chosen mark into public circulation through stylized lettering. “Graffiti” is a wider external label whose meaning changes by context.
- Context: Joe Austin describes the development of New York writing in the 1960s and early 1970s as a complex practice with multiple influences, not a single invention. [SR-NYC-001]
- Distinguish from: graffiti as a broad category that can include many unrelated inscriptions and practices.
- Visual learning: compare a simple name inscription, a developed handstyle and a composed piece without presenting them as a universal mandatory progression.
- Related: handstyle, tagging, piece, history of New York writing.
- Generator: vocabulary/context only; not a style preset.
- Evidence: strong for scholarly framing; local origin stories require attribution.
- Rights: original diagrams only. Status: draft.

## Entry 08 — Blackbook / piece book
- ID: object.blackbook
- Type: Object / practice / archive
- Short definition: A sketchbook or book used to develop, collect or preserve lettering and related visual work. Specific use varies between writers and social settings.
- Context: NYU Fales documents more than 30 sketchbooks and graffiti tag-books in the Martin Wong Papers. MCNY describes Wong acquiring writers' personal sketchbooks and preserving correspondence and publications. These sources show the historical importance of books as surviving records, not a single universal use. [SR-NYC-002; SR-NYC-004]
- Recognize by: pages, revisions, name studies, compositions, annotations and accumulated work; not every book contains all these elements.
- Distinguish from: a finished wall/train piece and a museum catalogue. A blackbook may document process and exchange rather than only final work.
- Learning angle: show a page as a time-based record of variants, revisions, peer marks and developing ideas.
- Generator: save concept branches and compare iterations; this is a NIGHTSHIFT workflow analogy, not a historical claim.
- Rights: archival page images are reference-only unless item-level reproduction rights are cleared. Status: draft.

## Entry 09 — Clean Train campaign / 1989 milestone
- ID: history.clean-train-campaign
- Type: Historical policy / transit context
- Short definition: A sustained New York transit/city effort to prevent and remove writing from subway cars, culminating in an institutional declaration that the subway fleet was graffiti-free in spring 1989.
- Context: the MCNY City as Canvas guide describes intensified MTA measures and the 1989 declaration. Joe Austin separates the campaign against subway writing from the cultural history of writing itself. [SR-NYC-003; SR-NYC-001]
- Distinguish from: the end of graffiti culture. The milestone refers to the subway fleet, not every surface or form of writing.
- Learning angle: two synchronized timelines—transit policy and cultural afterlives.
- Generator: historical surface/context only; no mechanical “end of graffiti” event.
- Evidence: strong as an attributed museum account; independent primary MTA corroboration remains open. Status: draft.

## Entry 10 — Sequential train photography
- ID: practice.sequential-train-photography
- Type: Documentation practice
- Short definition: Photographing train cars in sequence to preserve a work's relationship across multiple cars and reconstruct a larger composition from separate frames.
- Context: Smithsonian's collection description for Henry Chalfant's subway photographs reports a sequential method and contact with writers. Direct access to the record failed during this pass; details remain provisional pending repository confirmation. [SR-NYC-005]
- Distinguish from: a single isolated detail photograph; a sequence preserves continuity but cannot reproduce the original moving train or viewing conditions.
- Learning angle: assemble car-by-car images into a whole-train view, labelled as an interpretive composite.
- Generator: future train-surface layout tests; never derive historical glyph geometry by tracing photographs.
- Rights: no Smithsonian images in-game without item-level clearance. Status: draft / source recheck required.

## Entry 11 — From blackbook to archive
- ID: history.blackbook-preservation-network
- Type: Historical process / preservation
- Short definition: The survival of writing culture through sketchbooks, correspondence, publications, photography, collecting and exhibitions, alongside the disappearance or cleaning of public works.
- Context: NYU Fales documents the breadth of the Martin Wong Papers; MCNY describes acquisitions, correspondence, underground publications and the Museum of American Graffiti. [SR-NYC-002; SR-NYC-004]
- Distinguish from: a simple claim that graffiti “moved into galleries.” Preservation was distributed across personal books, photographs, publications, relationships and institutions.
- Learning angle: follow an object through creator, exchange, collector, archive and exhibition only where provenance is documented.
- Generator: project history/version comparison is a product analogy. Status: draft.

## Entry 12 — Institutional recognition
- ID: history.institutional-recognition
- Type: Historical process / exhibition context
- Short definition: The growing presence of writing-related works in galleries, museums and collections, alongside continued practice outside those institutions.
- Context: MCNY records Wong's collecting and the opening of the Museum of American Graffiti in 1989; its Above Ground release later frames the collection's path into institutional recognition. [SR-NYC-004; SR-NYC-006]
- Distinguish from: a universal or inevitable career path. Gallery recognition did not represent every writer or replace street/subway practice.
- Learning angle: compare public surfaces, personal books, canvas, exhibitions and archives without implying one is more authentic.
- Rights: collection metadata and links only; reproduction requires item-level clearance. Status: draft.
