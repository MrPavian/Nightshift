# Graffiti Practices, Formats & Surfaces

> This file deliberately separates what writers do from the visual form of lettering and from the surface on which work appears. Terminology varies; entries are working definitions, not universal legal or cultural classifications.

## 1. Practice and activity

### Writing
A practitioner term for the activity and culture of making one's name/lettering visible. Joe Austin notes the distinction between practitioners' use of "writing" and outsiders' use of "graffiti". [A]

### Tag / tagging
A tag is a writer's stylized name or signature. In early New York accounts, tags were often aliases combined with street numbers; that pattern is historically important but not a mandatory template for all tags. Tags can vary in speed, complexity, scale, tool and placement. [B]

**Generator implication:** handstyle is not a typed font. It needs stroke order/rhythm, pressure/weight variation where relevant, slant, spacing, joins and individualized terminals.

### Bombing
A term for intensive or widespread writing activity, often associated with quantity and visibility. It describes an activity/context rather than one fixed lettering style. Avoid defining bombing as a particular bubble alphabet or as synonymous with every illegal act.

**Generator implication:** do not create a "Bombing font". Model the visual forms separately from the in-game activity and its context.

## 2. Lettering / artwork forms

### Throw-up / throwie
A quickly executed, relatively simple, often rounded or inflated letter piece, commonly with fill and outline. The exact visual conventions vary by writer and period. It is a form of lettering/artwork, not a synonym for bombing.

### Piece
Short for "masterpiece" in common writing terminology: a more elaborate, composed work built around letters and often accompanied by fills, outlines, highlights, background or characters. Usage varies; do not enforce one fixed complexity threshold.

### Burner
An evaluative term for a particularly strong, striking or accomplished piece in some practitioner contexts. It is not a stable technical style category and should not be encoded as a single preset.

### Character
A figurative element that may accompany lettering or appear independently. Characters can function as mascots, narrative figures, signatures or compositional counterweights. Do not assume every character is derived from comic art.

## 3. Surface and composition formats

### Panel
A piece occupying a section or panel of a subway car. The car's doors, windows, seams, handles and other physical divisions affect the composition.

### Wholecar
A composition that covers most or all of a subway car's exterior. Treat it as a vehicle-scale format, not a lettering style. Train architecture and the relation between multiple panels matter.

### End-to-end
A composition extending along the length of a car, often across multiple door/window sections. Exact usage can vary by context; verify against period-specific sources and practitioner accounts before using as a strict taxonomy.

### Top-to-bottom
A format description emphasizing coverage from the upper to lower limits of a car side. It can overlap with other format descriptions; do not assume the terms are mutually exclusive.

### Wall piece
A piece made on a wall surface. Wall dimensions, texture, architectural boundaries, visibility and surrounding context affect composition.

## 4. Related media and street-art forms

### Stencil
An image-making method using a cut or prepared mask to control where paint reaches a surface. It is a technique/medium, not a lettering style. Stencil work may include lettering, figurative imagery or both.

### Sticker
A portable adhesive surface carrying a tag, graphic or message. The format affects scale, material and placement; it is not synonymous with a tag, although tags can be placed on stickers.

### Paste-up
A paper-based image or text applied to a surface with adhesive. It differs materially from direct painting and can include hand-drawn, printed or mixed-media content.

### Mural
A broad term for a wall-based artwork, often commissioned or community-based, but not exclusively so. A mural is defined by its wall-scale context, not by a single style or medium.

## 5. Taxonomy cautions

- "Bombing" is activity/context, not a font or style.
- "Burner" is praise/evaluation, not a consistent geometry recipe.
- "Wholecar", "panel", "end-to-end" and "top-to-bottom" describe format/surface coverage; they are not lettering grammars.
- "Stencil", "sticker" and "paste-up" describe technique or support/medium.
- "Character" describes figurative content, not a required component of a piece.
- These categories can overlap in one artwork or action.

## Dortmund period glossary — *Da kukse wa* (1987)

Marianne Brentzel's official excerpt from *Da kukse wa* includes a period “Writer-ABC”: **black-book** (book for sketches and photographs of painted / unpainted works), **charakter** (comic-like sprayed figure), **getting fame** (writer's goal of becoming famous), **piece** (large graffiti picture), **tag** (writer's name lettering), and **whole car** (completely painted train). These are documented as one Dortmund glossary snapshot from 1987, not universal definitions. The terms reinforce the separation of archive/practice, figurative content, motivation, lettering/artwork form and vehicle-scale format. See [Dortmund Writing Vocabulary — 1987 Local Glossary](DORTMUND_WRITING_VOCABULARY_1987_01.md).

## Dortmund tag corpus — *ALL SURFACE* (2022)

*ALL SURFACE – Tagging in Dortmund* is a distinct 1980s/1990s marker and aerosol tag / handstyle corpus. Keep this material separate from piece, panel and wholecar composition. A tag is a writer's name/signature practice; it is not merely a small piece or a preset style. Formal comparisons should be based on multiple inspected examples and described neutrally (e.g. rhythm, slant, compression, continuity and terminal behavior), without copying identifiable writers' signatures. Publisher descriptions do not substitute for page-level inspection. See [Dortmund ALL SURFACE — Tagging and Handstyle Corpus](DORTMUND_ALL_SURFACE_TAGGING_CORPUS_01.md).
