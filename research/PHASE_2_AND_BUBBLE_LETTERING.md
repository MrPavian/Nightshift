# PHASE 2, “SOFTIES” AND BUBBLE LETTERING

**Scope:** rounded lettering in early New York subway writing.  
**Status:** researched dossier; origin claims qualified.

## Finding

PHASE 2 (Michael Lawrence Marrow) is strongly associated with the development and naming of “softies”/bubble lettering in the early 1970s. The safest formulation is pivotal developer and popularizer, not uncontested sole inventor. Practitioner recollections preserve competing claims involving PHASE 2, COMET and JESTER.

## Evidence

- Sotheby’s catalogue notes that PHASE 2 began using his name in late 1971 and “softie” bubble letters in late 1972. This is a specialist catalogue, not a contemporaneous 1972 record. [B] https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2023/hip-hop/phase-2
- An exhibition document reproduced by Contemporary Art Library says PHASE 2 began painting wider, soft-edged pieces in 1972, initially nicknamed “softies”, and began calling them “bubble letters” in 1973. It links his work to the 149th Street Writers Bench and the form’s circulation. [B] https://cdn.contemporaryartlibrary.org/store/doc/65122/docfile/767536fc2b5af77c5f2a7e5f126c3c63.pdf
- ACA Galleries describes him as a pioneer of softies and records recollections of his experimental, constantly changing lettering. Its attribution of loops and arrows should remain attributed pending corroboration. [B] https://acagalleries.com/news/92/
- Joe Austin’s scholarly history describes PHASE 2 as credited with the softie style, characterized by rounded, puffy, inflated forms, and contrasts it with squarer blockbuster letters. [A] https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7312/aust11142
- A thesis excerpt quoting Yes Yes Y’all: The Oral History of Hip-Hop’s First Decade records a recollection that bubble letters emerged around 1973 and that COMET, JESTER and PHASE 2 disputed/influenced the invention. This disagreement is important evidence, not noise. [A/B] https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file?accession=ucin1338905792&disposition=inline

## Description

Softie/bubble lettering is a rounded, inflated letter treatment. Common tendencies include broad swollen strokes, rounded terminals, compact counters and a soft silhouette rather than the hard squared geometry of a blockbuster. These are family traits, not mandatory rules. Later bubble pieces may include sharp joins, extensions, arrows and individualized structures.

## Context and unresolved questions

The 149th Street Writers Bench and other meeting points helped writers see work, exchange knowledge, compare styles and build reputations. Styles spread through observation, conversation, rivalry and adaptation.

Do not claim PHASE 2 alone invented bubble letters. The sources support an approximate 1972–73 development window, not a definitive first piece. The exact contributions of COMET and JESTER, and exclusive attribution of loops/arrows, need further primary evidence.

## Archive record

- Canonical term: Bubble lettering
- Aliases: softies; softie letters
- Category: lettering style / form family
- Period/place: early 1970s, New York City; Bronx networks relevant
- Associated writer: PHASE 2
- Related actors: COMET, JESTER, Writers Bench / 149th Street
- Evidence: strong association; sole-inventor claim disputed
- Generator: rounded stroke profiles, inflated mass, variable counters and soft terminals; preserve per-letter variation.

## Generator inference

Treat bubble lettering as a construction grammar, not a font filter: establish a recognizable skeleton, expand stroke mass with rounded contours, preserve counters, allow controlled compression/overlap, and keep outline/fill as independent render layers. Do not conflate the lettering grammar with chrome, gradients or extrusion.
