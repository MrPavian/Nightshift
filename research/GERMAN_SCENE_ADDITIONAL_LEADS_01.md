# German Scene — Additional Research Leads 01

**Status:** source-graded research addendum, 14 November 2026.

## 1. Stylefile — publication infrastructure, not a crew

Stylefile began in Aschaffenburg in 1999, founded by Markus Christl and Jörn Stiller, with Freshseb involved in the initial magazine idea and Siggi Schlee designing the logo according to the Grafflibrary record. Publikat describes the magazine as made from within the scene and focused particularly on European graffiti, with international coverage. Its first issue appeared in November 1999. The publisher states that the original business collapsed after its e-commerce division struggled; Markus Christl regained the STYLEFILE MAGAZINE and PUBLIKAT trademark rights in 2025 and announced a return. [SL-01; SL-02; SL-03]

**Archive classification:** publication / media platform / scene infrastructure. Not a graffiti crew and not a lettering style.

Why it matters:
- documents a broad European scene across multiple cities and countries;
- connects train, wall, blackbook and interview culture;
- provides dated issue-level evidence and a possible bibliography for German and international writers;
- helps reconstruct how images and ideas circulated before social media.

Research task: build an issue index with issue number, date, featured cities, writers, crews, photographers, interviews and rights status. Do not scrape/reproduce magazine pages or photos without permission.

## 2. 1UP — One United Power

The Writers Bench Collection describes 1UP as founded in Kreuzberg, Berlin, in 2003, growing from a small group of friends into a globally visible crew using one shared name. It documents coordinated collective actions, international travel, photography/film and the 2018 collaboration with Martha Cooper, *One Week with 1UP*. [SL-04]

**Archive classification:** graffiti crew / collective identity / documentation network.

Research questions:
- How did the shared-name identity change the relationship between individual writer and crew?
- What roles did film, photography, online circulation and international travel play?
- How should the archive distinguish public-facing crew identity from private member attribution?
- How does 1UP's work connect to, but differ from, Berlin Kidz and other Berlin networks?

Do not infer that every Berlin Kidz participant is a 1UP member. The available Berlin Graffiti film listing tags 1UP and describes the Berlin Kidz film, but that alone does not establish exact membership overlap. [SL-05]

## 3. Berlin Kidz

The 2014 Berlin Graffiti listing describes the film/DVD *Berlin Kidz* as documenting a group and a particular Berlin urban-action milieu, including train surfing, rooftop movement and large-scale tags. The source is a scene/media listing, not a scholarly history or reliable roster. [SL-05]

**Archive classification:** film / documentary representation / Berlin scene phenomenon. Do not assume the label denotes a stable formal crew with a verified membership list.

Editorial safeguards:
- discuss the film's representation and editing, not as a how-to;
- do not reproduce dangerous train-surfing or rooftop actions as playable instructions;
- separate what the film depicts from what is known about individuals and affiliations;
- seek filmmaker, participant and contemporary publication testimony.

## 4. MOSES & TAPS

URBAN NATION's artist record describes MOSES & TAPS as a collective founded by Moses and Taps in 2007, with roots in 1990s graffiti and public-space work dating to 1994. It describes a changing, open collective of train writers and other artists, with conceptual works such as the realistically painted train door (2010) and *The Wall* (2015). [SL-06]

**Archive classification:** artist collective / post-graffiti and conceptual practice / train-writing context.

Why it matters:
- interrogates the boundaries between graffiti, illusion, public transport and conceptual art;
- documents a transition from classic trainwriting roots to gallery/museum contexts without erasing those roots;
- offers a case study in visual deception and context, but not a generator preset.

Research task: compare first-person interviews by Moses and Taps with URBAN NATION's institutional framing; establish which works were collaborative and how collective membership changed.

## 5. LOOMIT

In a 2009 ILOVEGRAFFITI interview, LOOMIT says he encountered graffiti in June 1983 and began painting in July 1983. He identifies his crews as FBI, UA and FX, and describes seeing *Wild Style* on television later that year and becoming interested in writers including Lee, Zephyr and Dondi. [SL-07]

**Archive classification:** writer / Munich scene / crew-network history.

This is first-person retrospective testimony. Preserve it as his account, and corroborate exact dates and crew periods with dated photographs, publications and other interviews.

Research tasks:
- connect Munich's early scene with travel, train writing and international exchange;
- identify dated works and the evolution of LOOMIT's spatial/3D approaches;
- distinguish his own practice from later collective or institutional accounts.

## 6. OZ — Hamburg

The City of OZ project is a joint initiative by Gängeviertel e.V. and the Graffitiarchiv / Archiv der Jugendkulturen e.V. It preserves a biography, press record, chronology, publications and discussion of Walter Josef Fischer (1950–2014). The project's chronology says Fischer arrived in Hamburg in 1992 and developed the characteristic OZ mark, smileys and other symbols. [SL-08; SL-09]

The project frames OZ's work amid conflict over public space, criminalization, solidarity and the boundaries between art and damage. A scholarly chapter by N. Samutina analyses the smileys as a visual/social project and an exercise in seeing the city differently. [SL-10]

**Archive classification:** individual writer / urban visual intervention / Hamburg-specific cultural and legal history.

Editorial safeguards:
- do not reduce OZ to a “most prolific” statistic; reported counts are often attributed claims and should be qualified;
- do not repeat sensationalist tabloid framing as neutral fact;
- preserve the conflict between public recognition, damage claims, state response and solidarity;
- do not romanticize unsafe railway activity or the circumstances of his death.

## 7. CAN2

A 2010 interview records CAN2 saying he made his first piece in December 1983 and describing an early path from B-boying and music videos into graffiti, sketching and later airbrush work. [SL-11]

**Archive classification:** writer / German scene / first-person oral history.

Research tasks: establish city/region and crew chronology with stronger sources; compare dated work and interviews; identify how airbrush and sketch practice related to his later mural and character work. Do not fill in missing biography from name recognition alone.

## 8. DARKO, VANDALZ and THE FÖNS — unresolved leads

The names were supplied as important research leads. This pass did not locate sufficiently strong, unambiguous primary or institutional sources to establish which specific DARKO, VANDALZ or THE FÖNS are intended, their membership, dates, cities or historical contributions.

**Do not publish profile claims yet.** Next verification should establish:
- exact spelling, alias variants and whether the term names a person, crew, magazine or project;
- city/region and active period;
- first-person interview, dated publication, archive record or contemporary photograph;
- members and membership periods, where relevant;
- specific documented contribution;
- rights and provenance for any proposed visual example.

The plural “The Föns” may be a crew name or a spelling variant; do not silently normalize it to another name.
