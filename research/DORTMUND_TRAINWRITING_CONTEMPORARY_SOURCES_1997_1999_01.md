# Dortmund Trainwriting — Contemporary 1997–1999 Evidence and Archive Access 01

**Date:** 2026-11-14  
**Status:** contemporary publication texts inspected; embedded photographs are not reused. Statements about operations and enforcement remain attributed to the contemporary reporter or magazine.  
**Purpose:** supplement retrospective 2002/2020/2022 accounts with near-period sources describing Dortmund trainwriting in 1997–1999.

## 1. *BOMBER* magazine, issue 14 (October–December 1997)

**Inspected source:** https://nzdr.ru/data/media/biblio/graffiti/Bomber.Graffiti.Magazine.Issue.14.-.1997.pdf

The issue is a period publication from the European graffiti-writing network. Its pages include an editorial review of two MASmedia products associated with Dortmund writer MASON:
- **King of the Line** — a double CD-ROM with mostly new/unpublished material, primarily from Dortmund writers, presented through an interactive station / timetable interface with video clips and audio.
- **Rolling Thunder 001** — described as 40 minutes of Dortmund train action, with trains, wholecars and whole-trains. The review names WUT, RIO, DNK, SAK, MAS, VOLA, SOB, DAR, PEST, ATOM, POEM and BASS, and mentions a three-car colour whole-train and an eight-car silver whole-train.

The same issue includes a “Silver slammers 1997” photographic collection with dated pieces from Copenhagen, New York, Holland, Amsterdam, Stockholm, Utrecht, The Hague and England. These are not Dortmund items, so do not accidentally import their captions into the Dortmund corpus.

**Value:** this is an unusually strong contemporary cross-check that Dortmund train footage and interactive digital archiving existed by 1997, and that a named set of writers was associated by the magazine review with the Dortmund video. It is also evidence of early digital archive presentation, not only a graffiti publication.

**Evidence boundary:** the review is written by a magazine editor/reviewer, not an independent audit of the footage. Names in the review are not a crew roster. The PDF is hosted on a third-party archive mirror; original publication provenance is clear from the scanned issue, but hosting rights are not established. No images are reproduced here.

## 2. *Endstation* issue 14 — “Dortmund - Haupt Bahn Hof” (8 March 1999)

**Inspected source:** https://www.graffiti.org/endstation/magazine/mag14/mag14.htm

This near-period report is explicitly dated 8 March 1999 and says it was made by an *Endstation* reporter in Dortmund. It provides a first-person outsider / visiting-reporter account of the state of the rail scene:
- The author says the photographs online are only a small fraction of the activity documented in 1996–1999.
- The report describes many visiting European writers and some visitors from overseas.
- It says wholecars, panels, throw-ups and tags remained numerous in 1996–1997.
- It reports changes to the timetable and a new anti-graffiti programme in early 1998, including yard / backjump closures, security staff, cameras and faster cleaning.
- It says the local scene responded with more silver pieces because of the reduced time available.
- It describes a Christmas / New Year 1998–1999 action called “Operation Silver Storm” and estimates 150–200 panels, several wholecars and one whole-train.

These are **the reporter's claims and estimates**, not official Deutsche Bahn or police statistics. In particular, the security staffing, camera deployment and operation totals require independent corroboration before being treated as established quantitative facts.

**Value:** the article provides a tightly dated snapshot of perceived changes between 1996 and early 1999, including the shift from a highly visible train scene to a faster-cleaning environment. It also documents how the writer community understood and narrated those changes at the time.

**Rights:** the article carries an explicit copyright notice. Photographs are not copied, traced or imported into NIGHTSHIFT. The report is used for textual historical analysis only.

## 5. NIGHTSHIFT implications

1. Prioritize *IRON CITY* acquisition / legitimate inspection; the MCL catalogue gives a concrete institutional search route.
2. Locate a legitimate copy of *BOMBER* #14 and verify whether the online PDF matches the printed issue. Do not assume mirror hosting grants reuse rights.
3. Treat *Rolling Thunder 001* as a separate moving-image archive lead; search for a rights-cleared copy or production record, not a pirated download.
4. Keep the *Endstation* 1999 article as a contemporary visitor account. Do not convert its estimated activity totals or security measures into database facts without corroboration.
5. Model historical scene changes as a sequence of context shifts: activity density, transit operations, security / cleaning, writer adaptation and media circulation. Do not collapse these into one “Dortmund style” attribute.
6. No named writer's tag, signature or piece may be copied into generator training data or presets.
