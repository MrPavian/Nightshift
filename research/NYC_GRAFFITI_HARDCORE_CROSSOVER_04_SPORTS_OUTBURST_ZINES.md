# NYC Graffiti–Hardcore Crossover: SPORTS, Outburst and the DIY Visual Layer 04

**Scope:** a focused supplement on the SPORTS crew, band-related visual production, and Queens fanzine infrastructure. It builds on the earlier crossover dossiers and distinguishes first-person recollection, author-curated historical reporting, artifact captions and later retrospectives.

## 1. SPORTS: an explicitly mixed hardcore / writing network

Freddy Alva's feature “The Graffiti and Hardcore Connection” describes SPORTS as an “ever shifting collective” started by Ed “HUSH,” an Absolution roadie. It gives two alternative expansions of the name:
- “Skins & Punks Out Rocking The System”
- “Sick People Out Ruling The Subways”

The article names Gavin “NATZ” Van Vlack, Chaka “EXPO” Malik and SANE/SMITH among the people associated with the collective. It also shows or captions:
- a Burn piece by SANE, dated 1990;
- an unfinished GIS–SPORTS crew piece by HUSH and SCAR 1, dated 1987, with photo credit to SCAR 1 GIS;
- a Chaka sticker dated 1989;
- a NATZ piece dated 1987, also photo-credited to SCAR 1 GIS.

**Evidence assessment:** this is unusually specific secondary reporting with named people, dates and artifact captions. The article supports SPORTS as a shifting mixed-scene collective, not a stable formal crew with a complete roster. The two expansions are both reported; do not silently choose one as the definitive official expansion.

## 2. Jay “JAY R” Rufino and Outburst: a clear artist / band / design link

The same Alva feature identifies Jay “JAY R” Rufino as a Woodside, Queens native who grew up tagging and used his graffiti skills in artwork for Outburst, the band in which he played guitar.

The article captions several relevant artifacts:
- *Where the Wild Things Are* compilation booklet, 1989;
- Stand Proud flyer, 1988;
- Outburst, *Miles to Go* 7-inch cover, 1989;
- Outburst sticker, 1989.

This is a stronger case than a band simply using graffiti-like lettering: the source explicitly connects a named guitarist and writer to the band's visual material. The article's image captions provide dates and artifact types, but this dossier has not independently inspected the physical booklet, flyer, sleeve or sticker. Treat the dates and attribution as reported by Alva until the originals are examined.

## 3. The Village Noize: a dated Queens DIY publishing network

A No Echo retrospective on Queens hardcore fanzines reports:
- *The Village Noize* was started by Eric Weilander of Bayside with Chris Bunkley of Queens Village helping on the first five issues.
- The first issue appeared in 1987; it began as a stapled, photocopied zine focused on NYHC bands.
- Weilander recalls an interview with Prong from the CBGB stage, a Murphy's Law interview layout using Uncle Al's characters from the first album cover, and spray-painted covers for issues before the publication moved to glossy production around issue nine.
- The zine ran for 17 issues, with the last issue in 1995.

This is a retrospective based on the creator's recollection. It is useful for the material production history and named editorial collaboration. The original issues have not been inspected here; no specific graffiti writer should be credited with a cover without an issue-level byline or direct testimony.

## 4. Yes Zista: Bunkley's own publication and later band roles

The same retrospective says Chris Bunkley went on to publish two issues of *Yes Zista* in 1988 and 1989, focused primarily on NYHC. Bunkley recalls the “for the kids by the kids” ethos and hands-on cut-and-paste production using scissors and rubber cement. The second issue included an interview with Raw Deal (later Killing Time), according to the retrospective.

The article also says Bunkley later sang for Second 2 None and Terminal Confusion. Freddy Alva's band-by-band list independently identifies BUNK in those two bands and associates the relevant musicians with TMR.

**Interpretation:** Bunkley's path connects local fanzine publishing to band participation. It demonstrates the DIY media network's overlap with writer/band identities without proving that every zine contributor was a graffiti writer.

## 5. In Effect and the wider Queens fanzine ecology

The No Echo retrospective also records *In Effect*:
- Chris Wynne began it in College Point in 1988.
- It lasted 12 printed issues.
- Wynne recalls a group of friends drawn to heavier music as the starting point.
- An anthology of the printed issues was announced for 2021 by Shining Life Press.

This is important as scene infrastructure and as a potential source corpus. The source does not establish *In Effect* as a graffiti zine or its editor as a writer. Do not classify it as such without artifact-level evidence.

## 6. Original artifact trail: what can and cannot be claimed

### Directly described or captioned in published reporting
- SPORTS / HUSH / SCAR 1 GIS: unfinished crew piece, dated 1987 in the caption.
- NATZ: piece dated 1987 in the caption.
- SANE: Burn piece, dated 1990 in the caption.
- Chaka / EXPO: sticker, dated 1989 in the caption.
- JAY R / Outburst: 1988 Stand Proud flyer, 1989 compilation booklet, 1989 *Miles to Go* 7-inch cover and sticker.
- *The Village Noize*: first issue 1987; 17-issue run ending 1995, according to the editor's retrospective.
- *Yes Zista*: two issues, 1988 and 1989, according to the publisher's retrospective.

### Not yet verified by inspection
- Physical originals, reverse sides, printer marks, complete credits or exact publication dates of the flyers, booklet, sleeves and stickers.
- Original *The Village Noize* and *Yes Zista* issue scans.
- Whether every image reproduced in the secondary article was photographed from an original artifact or a later reproduction.

### Rights
The images are evidence and research leads, not assets cleared for reuse. NIGHTSHIFT should create original fictionalized graphics informed by broad period production methods, not reproduce protected logos, album covers, flyers, lettering signatures or identifiable artwork.

## 7. NIGHTSHIFT design implications — inference, not history

1. Model SPORTS as a time-varying social collective with people connected by distinct edge types; do not force it into a fixed roster.
2. Separate a musician's role from their graphic contribution. JAY R's case can have independent edges for guitarist, writer and credited band artwork.
3. Treat zines as production systems: editor, contributors, issue number, date, print method, distribution context, interviews, reviews and image credits.
4. Let zines, flyers, demo covers, stickers and show bills form a media layer connecting writers, bands, venues and neighborhoods.
5. Preserve uncertainty in artifact dates and credits until original scans or first-person confirmation are available.
6. Use fictional in-world bands, zines and graphics in gameplay. Historical names and artifacts belong in the archive/reference layer, not as copied commercial assets.
