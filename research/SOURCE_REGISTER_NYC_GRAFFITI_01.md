# NYC Graffiti Research — Source Register 01

Research pass: 14 November 2026. Scope: early New York writing, subway culture, archival preservation, blackbooks and institutional transition.

## SR-NYC-001 — Joe Austin, Taking the Train
- Creator: Joe Austin. Publisher: Columbia University Press, 2001.
- Scholarly monograph. https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7312/aust11142
- Inspected: book description, contents, prologue preview and selected chapter previews.
- Supports: early writing culture; distinction between writing history and the public construction of a “graffiti problem”; city/MTA policy; cultural survival after subway removal.
- Caution: Austin emphasizes multiple originating influences, not one clean root. Preview access is partial; do not attribute details from inaccessible chapters without consulting the book.
- Accessed 2026-11-14.

## Claim-level cautions
1. Origins: no single inventor, borough or newspaper article should be presented as uncontested origin. Austin explicitly emphasizes a web of influences.
2. 1989: the MCNY guide reports a spring 1989 declaration about the transit fleet. It does not establish the end of wall writing, blackbooks, crews, galleries, photography or international scenes.
3. Archive vs event: a finding aid proves the presence and arrangement of records, not automatically the truth of every statement in those records.
4. Sequential photography preserves continuity across cars but cannot reproduce original scale, movement, colour conditions or audience.
5. Access, citation, study, reproduction and commercial game use are separate permissions.

## Research gaps
- Direct item-level inspection of the Smithsonian Chalfant collection.
- Primary documentation for specific early women writers and dated works; avoid unsupported “first” labels.
- Independent MTA/municipal corroboration for Clean Train milestones.
- First-person evidence on blackbook exchange, critique and peer learning.
- Dated, region-specific evidence for panel, end-to-end, wholecar and blockbuster terminology.

A source record can support multiple claims, but each archive entry must cite the specific claim it supports. Authority alone is not evidence; state what the source actually documents.
