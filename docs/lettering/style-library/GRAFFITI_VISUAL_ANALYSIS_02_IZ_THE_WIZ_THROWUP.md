## Source evidence

The Letterform Archive object record identifies the photograph as *Iz the Wiz Throw-up*, ca. 1983, New York, and credits Atco / Lou Romain. It describes the form as:

> Throw-up, Throw, Fill-in, or Fill: Overlapping bubble letters with an outline and fill.

The image is directly viewable from the object page. This is the first specimen in the current project corpus for which both an explicit style label and an individually opened image are available.

## Direct visual observations

- The main forms are very large relative to the train-car panels.
- The visible outer contour is broad, rounded and continuous in character.
- Several letter-like masses overlap or approach one another closely.
- The interior areas are pale / visually low-contrast in this reproduction; a precise fill colour should not be inferred.
- The main forms cross the panel divisions, while window frames, seams and lower train structure remain visible.
- Additional tags and smaller marks sit around the dominant throw-up and should be treated as separate secondary marks.
- The exact letter sequence is not confidently legible in this reproduction. Do not use it as a glyph transcription source.

## Provisional construction grammar

This is an abstraction for an original generator, not a reconstruction of the photographed piece.

1. **Mass-first silhouette:** create a small sequence of large rounded letter masses before applying outlines or colour.
2. **Overlap as a word-level rule:** allow adjacent glyph masses to overlap or nearly touch; do not solve spacing as independent typographic kerning alone.
3. **Continuous outer contour:** generate a coherent outer boundary around the combined word mass, while preserving enough internal separation to keep the letters readable.
4. **Low internal complexity:** keep internal cuts and counters simple compared with a wildstyle construction.
5. **Outline as a distinct layer:** the contour should be generated after the base letter masses and remain independently adjustable.
6. **Fill as a separate layer:** the archive definition names both outline and fill, but this particular displayed reproduction does not justify a precise fill recipe.
7. **Train-surface interaction:** panel seams and windows are substrate constraints, not letter anatomy.

## What this specimen can and cannot teach

**Useful evidence**
- A confirmed throw-up classification.
- Rounded / bubble-like massing.
- Overlap between adjacent letters.
- Strong outer contour.
- Relationship between the dominant word and secondary tags.

**Not established**
- Exact letter skeletons or alphabet-wide glyph rules.
- Exact letter sequence.
- Exact fill colour or paint process.
- A universal throw-up recipe; one object is not a complete style corpus.

## Generator test derived from this specimen

Create an original 4–6 character throw-up word with:
- inflated rounded masses;
- controlled overlap;
- a unified outer outline;
- simple internal counters;
- optional independent fill and highlight layers;
- no copied letter sequence, contour path, tags or train-car layout.

Acceptance checks:
- silhouette reads as a compact throw-up rather than a generic rounded font;
- neighbouring letters behave as a connected word mass;
- outline remains coherent at different word lengths;
- no decorative 3D, arrows or wildstyle extensions are required for the style to read.
