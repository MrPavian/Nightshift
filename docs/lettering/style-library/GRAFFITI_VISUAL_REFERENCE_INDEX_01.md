## 2. High-value artist studies (not style labels)

These are not templates and must not be treated as universal representatives of a family. Study them to understand different solutions to lettering.

### DONDI WHITE — structure, flow, connections
- Entry: https://www.graffiti.org/dondi/zeph.html
- Photo archive: https://www.graffiti.org/dondi/subway.html
- The archive biography explicitly discusses Dondi's redefinition of Wildstyle, emphasizing letter dynamics, form and juxtaposition; it describes his forms as acrobatic and aerodynamic.
- Study question: how does the underlying letter remain traceable while connections and extensions increase complexity?
- Rights note: the linked photo archive explicitly credits Henry Chalfant and Martha Cooper; do not copy the photos into the repo.

### SEEN — bubble forms and large-scale name presence
- Artist index: https://www.redhouseoriginals.com/artists/seen
- Artwork example: https://www.redhouseoriginals.com/shop/artwork/jumbo-blue-bubble
- The gallery identifies the work as spraypaint on canvas and dates the example to 2024. The artist index lists additional bubble-related works.
- Study question: compare multiple works and identify which properties belong to letterform anatomy versus finish, colour and scale.
- Rights note: gallery artwork images are not automatically reusable.

### IZ THE WIZ — throw-up practice and repetition
- Archive: https://www.at149st.com/iz.html
- The archive documents his well-known throw-ups in the mid-1970s and later whole-car work.
- Study question: how do repetition, economical structure and quick execution affect letter proportions?
- Rights note: archive prohibits reproduction without permission.

### LADY PINK — lettering within a wider mural practice
- Official site: https://www.ladypinknyc.com/about
- Study question: distinguish letter construction from surrounding narrative/mural imagery; do not assume all graffiti work is a lettering style.
- Rights note: official portfolio imagery remains copyrighted unless explicitly licensed.

## 3. Reference annotation sheet

For each individual source image, create one record with these fields:

- `reference_id`
- `style_hypothesis` (not assumed truth)
- `artist_or_writer` (if reliably attributed)
- `source_page_url`
- `image_url_or_page_anchor` (only if publicly accessible and stable)
- `photographer`
- `date_or_period` (known / estimated / unknown)
- `place_or_scene` (known / estimated / unknown)
- `medium_or_context` (train, wall, canvas, blackbook, digital, unknown)
- `rights_status` (permission, explicit licence, link-only, unknown)
- `visible_glyphs`
- `letterform_notes` (skeleton, mass, bowls, stems, counters, terminals, cuts)
- `composition_notes` (baseline, rhythm, spacing, joins, overlap, flow)
- `treatment_notes` (fill, outline, inline, 3D, highlights, background)
- `what_not_to_copy`
- `confidence` (high / medium / low)

Unknown metadata must stay unknown. Do not infer artist, date, region or rights from visual appearance.

## 4. Research findings from the first pass

1. **Bubble is not just an inflation filter.** The SEEN gallery gives a concrete multi-work comparison route. Inspect bowl tension, mass, terminals and counter design separately from colour and outline.
2. **Throw-up and Bubble overlap but are not synonyms.** Historical sources describe throw-up as a speed/format/practice context; a throw-up may use rounded or block-derived lettering.
3. **Blockbuster must be designed for scale and coverage.** Museum and conservation references emphasize large, clear, robust, square/block-like letters and distance legibility. The current Straight + fit-box implementation does not prove that grammar.
4. **Simple/Straight is not generic sans typography.** Dürst's examples include angular and rectangular additions; museum framing emphasizes individual style and form.
5. **Wildstyle is a relationship and flow problem.** Dondi's work is useful for studying how dynamic letterforms and juxtaposition create complexity. Arrows alone are not Wildstyle.
6. **Effects are separate.** 3D, outline, fill and highlights must not be used to disguise weak letter construction.
