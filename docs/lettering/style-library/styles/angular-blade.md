# Style Card — Angular / Blade Lettering

**ID:** angular-blade | **Version:** 1.0.0  
**Primary axis:** Letterform grammar  
**Maturity:** Draft. Angular lettering is documented as a broad construction tendency; “Blade” is treated here as a NIGHTSHIFT operational subfamily, not a universal historical category.

## Definition and intent
Angular lettering emphasizes sharp direction changes, clipped corners, pointed terminals and faceted transitions. It may remain simple and readable or become more complex. “Blade” is a working NIGHTSHIFT label for a sharper, tapered and directional variant; do not claim it is a universally accepted graffiti taxonomy term.

## Shared visual signature
- Directional strokes and deliberate angle families.
- Clipped, pointed or tapered terminals.
- Faceted curves where appropriate.
- Strong negative-space channels.
- Sharpness is structural, not a collection of arbitrary spikes.

## Templates

### T1 — Faceted Angular
**Intent:** straight-segment construction with controlled bevels.  
**Construction:** define skeleton; select an angle family; replace selected curves with facets; preserve bowls and counters as deliberate polygonal negative shapes.  
**Parameters:** facet count, primary angle, secondary angle, corner clip, stem mass.  
**Guardrails:** do not polygonize every curve equally; maintain glyph-specific anatomy.  
**Tests:** A, B, G, O, S, R; words ZERO, KRAFT.

### T2 — Blade / Tapered
**Intent:** directional, tapered terminals and wedge-like stroke endings.  
**Construction:** keep the main body mass stable; taper only selected terminals or transitions; anchor every point to a stroke direction; inspect thin necks and joins.  
**Parameters:** taper length, taper width, tip angle, directional bias, minimum neck.  
**Guardrails:** no unanchored spikes; no needle-thin structural bridges; taper is not equivalent to Wildstyle extension.  
**Tests:** A, K, M, R, V, W; word VANTA.

### T3 — Cut / Shard
**Intent:** sharp notches, cuts and interrupted contours within a readable glyph.  
**Construction:** author the base glyph; mark cuts as contour events; classify each as terminal cut, notch, inset or separation; protect counters.  
**Parameters:** cut depth, cut angle, cut frequency, notch clearance, fragmentation budget.  
**Guardrails:** cuts must not sever essential stems or destroy the skeleton.  
**Tests:** E, F, H, N, Z; word SHARD.

## Composition
A consistent directional bias can unify a word, but not every letter needs the same number of points. Use a small family of angles and preserve negative-space rhythm. Extensions that connect multiple letters belong to Wildstyle relationship grammar, not this card.

## Failure modes
- SPIKE_NOISE — random points with no directional anchor.
- OVER_FACETING — every curve becomes a generic polygon.
- NEEDLE_NECK — tapered join becomes structurally fragile.
- WILDSTYLE_LEAK — interlocks/extensions introduced without relationship modelling.
- COUNTER_SHARD — cuts damage counter topology.

## Acceptance tests
- Compare T1–T3 against Classic Straight on the same word.
- All sharp features must map to a terminal, transition or contour event.
- Inspect at thumbnail size for noise and full size for join integrity.
- Test curved glyphs (B, G, O, S) as well as angular glyphs (A, K, M, V, W).

## Evidence and inference
**Source-supported:** angular/block and sharp-edged lettering are recognized descriptive tendencies in graffiti style accounts.  
**NIGHTSHIFT inference:** Blade and Cut/Shard as operational subfamilies; all parameters, angle families and tests.  
**Open:** practitioner terminology for “blade” and regional variants.
