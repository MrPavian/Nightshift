# Style Card — Traditional / Old-School Writing

**ID:** traditional-old-school | **Version:** 1.0.0  
**Primary axis:** Letterform grammar + historical/contextual reference  
**Maturity:** Draft. “Old-school” is a broad retrospective label, not one fixed alphabet. Templates below are NIGHTSHIFT study families, not reconstructions of a specific writer or crew.

## Definition and scope
Traditional / Old-School is used here as an umbrella for earlier and foundational writing approaches: simpler letter skeletons, handstyle-derived forms, early bubble/softie forms, outlined pieces and classic straight/block constructions. These are not one coherent style and must not be collapsed into a single preset. The label is useful as a historical lens only when a place, period and source are named.

## Templates

### T1 — Early Simple Outline
**Intent:** economical, readable letterforms with limited internal detail.  
**Construction:** begin with a clear skeleton; use restrained stroke mass; add a clean outline; keep counters generous; avoid modern complex extensions.  
**Parameters:** stem mass, outline weight, slant, spacing, detail budget.  
**Guardrails:** do not present this as a universal 1970s alphabet; historical variation is substantial.  
**Tests:** A, E, M, R, S; words TRAIN, SOUL.

### T2 — Classic Softie / Rounded
**Intent:** rounded, inflated massing associated with early softie/bubble traditions.  
**Construction:** expand recognizable skeletons into rounded bodies; preserve counters and bridges; vary glyph width and bowl shape.  
**Parameters:** inflation, roundness, body mass, counter clearance, width variation.  
**Guardrails:** distinguish letterform grammar from Throw-up format; do not reproduce a documented historical piece.  
**Tests:** B, O, R, S, U; words LOVE, SOUL.

### T3 — Classic Straight / Block
**Intent:** broad, direct letter construction with restrained embellishment.  
**Construction:** establish robust stems and bars; keep counters open; use a consistent but not mechanical rhythm; apply outline only after silhouette approval.  
**Parameters:** stem mass, corner treatment, bar alignment, counter scale, word density.  
**Guardrails:** do not confuse this with every use of “Blockbuster”.  
**Tests:** A, B, G, M, R; words TRAIN, POWER.

### T4 — Classic Piece Treatment
**Intent:** a developed piece presentation layered over a separately chosen letter grammar.  
**Construction:** choose T1–T3 or another documented base grammar; build word silhouette; add outline, fill and background as separate layers; keep finish independent from glyph structure.  
**Parameters:** outline hierarchy, fill coverage, background density, palette count, depth toggle.  
**Guardrails:** “piece” is a work format, not a letterform family. Do not label finish as historical without source evidence.  
**Tests:** same word in flat and finished views.

## Historical handling
Every historical study must record geography, approximate period, source and what is directly visible. Do not infer tool, stroke order or exact chronology from a still image. Do not imitate a named writer's signature or a specific piece.

## Failure modes
- OLD_SCHOOL_MONOLITH — unrelated historical forms collapsed into one style.
- PERIOD_WASH — no geography/period/source attached to historical claims.
- PIECE_AS_FONT — work format mistaken for letterform grammar.
- NOSTALGIA_FILTER — retro palette/effects substitute for historical form.
- RECONSTRUCTION_OVERCLAIM — generated form presented as authentic archival reconstruction.

## Acceptance tests
- Compare T1–T3 in monochrome; differences must be structural.
- T4 must leave the underlying front-face geometry unchanged.
- Each historical reference carries source, geography and period metadata.
- Practitioner/editor review required before “historically representative” labels.

## Evidence and inference
**Source-supported:** sources document changing and diverse early forms, including tags, softies, straight/block letters and developed pieces; historical accounts are geographically and temporally bounded.  
**NIGHTSHIFT inference:** T1–T4 and parameterization.  
**Open:** the label “old-school” varies widely across scenes and generations.
