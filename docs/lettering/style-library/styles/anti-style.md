# Style Card — Anti-Style / Experimental Lettering

**ID:** anti-style | **Version:** 1.0.0  
**Primary axis:** Experimental letterform and composition grammar  
**Maturity:** Draft. “Anti-style” is a contested, scene-dependent term. This card defines safe operational study modes, not a universal taxonomy or a license to imitate named writers.

## Definition and intent
Anti-Style is used in some graffiti conversations for work that resists polished, conventional or easily categorized letter construction. It is not simply “bad lettering”, random distortion or a preset for illegibility. The deliberate disruption of expected form, consistency and finish can itself be a coherent visual decision.

NIGHTSHIFT uses “Experimental Lettering” as the neutral internal label and retains “Anti-Style” as a searchable community term with a caution flag.

## Templates

### T1 — Raw / Unpolished
**Intent:** retain visible irregularity, asymmetry and rough contour decisions without defaulting to generic mess.  
**Construction:** establish a minimal glyph skeleton; permit controlled deviations in width, alignment, terminals and contour; retain enough anatomy to identify authored decisions.  
**Parameters:** irregularity budget, baseline drift, width variance, contour roughness, glyph identity floor.  
**Guardrails:** roughness must be authored and repeatable; do not add random noise as a substitute for design.  
**Tests:** A, E, M, R, S; word STATIC.

### T2 — Deconstructed / Fragmented
**Intent:** break, offset or separate selected components while retaining a recoverable relationship to the base glyph.  
**Construction:** define a conventional base; choose components to split or displace; document each transformation; preserve negative-space and reading cues.  
**Parameters:** fragment count, displacement, component omission, overlap, reconstruction cues.  
**Guardrails:** no arbitrary fragmentation; if a glyph becomes non-identifiable, label the output abstract rather than typographic.  
**Tests:** K, R, X, Z; word FRACTURE.

### T3 — Abstract / Illegible-by-Design
**Intent:** deliberately move from readable lettering toward visual composition, while preserving an authored relationship to text.  
**Construction:** record the source word; select which letter features remain; compose the visual mass; document what is legible, ambiguous or absent.  
**Parameters:** abstraction level, retained feature count, glyph traceability, negative-space structure, composition balance.  
**Guardrails:** do not claim legibility where none exists; distinguish abstract lettering from a readable alphabet template.  
**Tests:** compare the same word at open, partial and abstract levels.

## Evidence protocol
- Mark which features are intentional disruptions.
- Preserve the source word and transformation notes for evaluation.
- Do not infer authorial intent from a reference image without testimony.
- Do not reproduce a recognizable artist's anti-style, signature or characteristic piece.

## Failure modes
- RANDOM_NOISE — accidental distortion presented as anti-style.
- BAD_FONT — “ugly” default font used as a shortcut.
- INTENT_UNDOCUMENTED — no record of what was deliberately disrupted.
- TRACEABILITY_OVERCLAIM — abstract output falsely labelled readable.
- STYLE_CLONE — recognizable living or historical writer's signature approach copied.

## Acceptance tests
- T1 must show controlled irregularity across at least six glyphs.
- T2 must document each altered component.
- T3 must declare traceability and ambiguity honestly.
- All variants must remain distinguishable from technical errors or broken geometry.

## Evidence and inference
**Source-supported:** graffiti classification is fluid and contested; graffiti lettering may deliberately modify, extend, loop, shorten, thicken or abstract letters for composition.  
**NIGHTSHIFT inference:** T1–T3 and operational definition.  
**Open:** practitioner terminology and the boundary between anti-style, experimental writing and abstraction require direct testimony.
