# Graffiti Zine Composition Analysis 01 — IGTimes

**Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Source:** Letterform Archive, *Subscription to Mischief* — IGTimes walkthrough  
**Status:** source-based editorial / composition analysis; not a glyph specimen and not a style-family classification.

## Source facts

The Letterform Archive walkthrough states:
- David Schmidlapp began IGTimes in 1983.
- Artist, writer and Bronx native Phase 2 joined on volume 8 in 1986.
- The magazine ran for eleven years and used several names, including International Graffiti Times, The International Get Hip Times, T.I.G.H.T. and The Intergalactic Times.
- Early issues were usually folded like a New York City subway map; volume 7 appeared in newspaper format with the masthead *The Subway Sun*.
- Early issues used limited palettes of two or three colours, with duotoned or black-and-white piece photographs anchoring handlettering, tags, illustration, collage and rough-rag typed text blocks.
- After Phase 2 joined, the publication moved toward more sophisticated collage and later multipage, full-colour releases. Thick black rules and dynamic framing devices bordered text; imagery and text could cross margins and extend off the page.

Source: https://exhibitions.letterformarchive.org/mischief/walkthroughs/igtimes

## What this does and does not tell us

This is strong evidence about a historically grounded graffiti editorial language. It is **not** evidence for the construction of a specific alphabet or a particular Bubble, Throw-up, Blockbuster, Simple/Straight or Wildstyle grammar. The walkthrough's text describes the publication and layout; it does not identify individual glyphs or assign style labels to every pictured piece.

## Composition grammar hypotheses for NIGHTSHIFT

These are original system hypotheses inspired by the documented editorial methods, not reproductions of a specific spread.

### A. Zine page as layered collage

Model a page as independently movable layers:
1. primary piece photograph / artwork panel;
2. handlettered title or masthead;
3. tag / signature strip;
4. typed editorial text;
5. illustration or icon;
6. crop marks, rules, frames and borders;
7. paper / photocopy texture.

### B. Controlled palette mode

Provide a dedicated two- or three-colour print mode. Colour reduction should be a page-level constraint and should not modify the underlying graffiti glyph construction.

### C. Dynamic frame mode

Allow text panels and image panels to cross or break the nominal page margins. Use black rules and framing devices as compositional elements rather than default decoration.

### D. Folded-format constraints

Support non-standard page geometries, including folded map-like layouts and newspaper formats. Page composition should be responsive to the physical fold and trim, not only to a rectangular screen canvas.

### E. Cohesive chaos

Use a constrained set of layer types, palette limits and hierarchy rules, then permit asymmetrical placement, overlaps and inconsistent module sizes. Randomness alone is not a substitute for editorial judgement.

## Generator implications

- Keep graffiti letterform grammar separate from zine layout grammar.
- Add a future `zine_composition` mode only after the lettering engine can create credible original letters.
- Support duotone / limited-palette rendering as a print treatment, not as a style preset.
- Allow collage layers to crop and overlap independently.
- Treat handlettering, tags, typed text and piece photography as distinct content types.
- Preserve source credits and rights for every archival image used as research material.

This source expands the project beyond the train-car piece. It supports a second research track: how writers circulated and framed their work in print, and how tags, handlettering, pieces, illustrations and text interacted in zine culture.

It should feed a future editorial composition module, not be counted toward the five core graffiti lettering specimen quotas.
