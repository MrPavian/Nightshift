# Visual Analysis 05 — Letterform Archive Magazine and Zine Gallery

**Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Source:** Letterform Archive, “An Undercover Look at Letterform Archive’s Graffiti Collection” (published 2026-10-19)  
**Review status:** five hi-fi gallery images opened and visually inspected; six item-level records added because the Bomb gallery image shows two separately captioned covers. No image binary copied into NIGHTSHIFT. Rights remain link-only.

## Access and evidence boundary

The Letterform Archive article identifies each gallery item and states that its gallery images are hi-fi captures that can be enlarged. The five images below were opened directly from that gallery. The Online Archive catalogue itself currently displays a scheduled-maintenance / temporary-offline message, so its item pages could not be independently checked during this pass.

The article is strong for caption-level attribution and access provenance. The observations below are limited to visible features in the opened gallery images. They do not establish universal style definitions, writer intent, exact stroke order, or reuse rights.

## 1. IGTimes 6 — interior spread (1993)

- **Caption credit:** David Schmidlapp (coeditor/publisher) and PHASE 2 (coeditor/art director).
- **Gallery image:** [Open hi-fi spread](https://letterformarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/lfa_graffiti_0001_063-web.jpg)
- **Catalogue lead:** [IGTimes 6 record](https://oa.letterformarchive.org/item?workID=lfa_graffiti_0001)

### Direct observations
- The spread is a dense editorial montage of many separate train photographs and graffiti pieces, framed by thick dark rules and a large masthead.
- Multiple letter systems coexist across the photographs; several are cropped by the small panels or page edges.
- The masthead and editorial framing are visually distinct from the photographed graffiti works.
- At this reproduction scale, individual glyph anatomy in most flicks is too small to measure reliably.

### NIGHTSHIFT relevance
This is **not one alphabet specimen**. It is a valuable index page for selecting individual photographed pieces in a later pass. Preserve the separation between editorial masthead, photo documentation, and the actual letterforms inside each photograph. Do not count the spread as a reviewed Bubble, Blockbuster or Wildstyle example.

## 2. Just Hangin’ Out — Greg Lamarche (Sp.One), 2021

- **Caption credit:** Greg Lamarche (Sp.One).
- **Gallery image:** [Open hi-fi artwork](https://letterformarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/lfa_skillscollections_0160_001-web.jpg)
- **Catalogue lead:** [Just Hangin’ Out record](https://oa.letterformarchive.org/item?workID=lfa_skillscollections_0160)

### Direct observations
- The sheet contains two distinct lettering compositions, not one continuous alphabet.
- The upper composition is a dense, multicolour arrangement of rounded and angular letter-like masses, with strong overlaps, dark internal divisions, repeated small circular motifs and several protruding terminals.
- The lower composition reads “JUST HANGIN’ OUT” and uses compact, heavy, irregular block-like letters. Dark offset massing is visible behind the yellow front faces.
- A hanging spider and small ground marks are separate illustration / composition elements, not letter anatomy.
- The page does not provide a neutral monochrome view of either piece.

### NIGHTSHIFT hypotheses
- The upper and lower pieces should be treated as separate specimens and separate construction problems.
- The lower lettering suggests a useful study of compact block mass, irregular cap-height rhythm, counter retention and a shared dark depth layer. These are observations to test, not a named style classification.
- The upper piece is a useful example of dense multi-letter composition, but its overlaps must not be reduced to a global overlap percentage. Pair-specific relationships and front/behind order matter.

### Limits
The exact text of the upper composition is not confidently transcribed. No family label is assigned to either piece. Do not reproduce its exact word, glyph paths, colour arrangement, spider or page composition.

## 3. The Bomb Hip-Hop Magazine no. 44 — cover by Cokes (1995)

- **Article caption:** David Paul (creator) and Cokes (cover artist).
- **Gallery image:** [Open combined hi-fi scan](https://letterformarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Graf_BombMag-web.jpg) — left cover.

### Direct observations
- The cover combines an illustrated central scene with a large, elongated, outlined masthead / title treatment across the upper area.
- The title lettering uses irregular directional contours and is integrated with the illustration and smaller handwritten marks.
- The image is a magazine cover composition, not an isolated graffiti piece or alphabet sheet.

### NIGHTSHIFT relevance and limits
Treat the title as editorial display lettering and the rest as illustration / cover composition. The scan does not isolate a clean set of glyphs or establish a style family. It is useful as evidence that lettering, illustration and editorial hierarchy can be deliberately integrated, but not as a ready-made generator grammar.

## 4. The Bomb Hip-Hop Magazine no. 46 — cover by Serkit (1996)

- **Article caption:** David Paul (creator) and Serkit (cover artist).
- **Gallery image:** [Open combined hi-fi scan](https://letterformarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Graf_BombMag-web.jpg) — right cover.

### Direct observations
- The large “BOMB” title is constructed as broad, heavy display lettering with strong block-like mass and high contrast against the densely illustrated cover.
- The letter bodies appear deliberately compact and close-set; the O is treated as a dominant rounded form.
- Secondary illustration and smaller lettering surround the title and compete for attention, but the title retains the strongest hierarchy.

### NIGHTSHIFT hypotheses
- Useful for studying the relationship between a compact four-letter word, large glyph mass, counter scale and editorial hierarchy.
- Any future reconstruction should begin with a clean front-face silhouette and test the B/O/M/B rhythm before adding illustration or depth.
- The cover is not sufficient evidence for a general Blockbuster grammar. “Block-like” here is a visible shape description, not a source-confirmed style label.

### Limits
The combined scan is one gallery image containing two separately captioned covers. No claim is made that the cover title is a graffiti piece or that the visible forms represent Serkit’s entire lettering practice.

## 5. Skills 6 — cover by Greg Lamarche (Sp.One), 1994

- **Caption credit:** Greg Lamarche (Sp.One).
- **Gallery image:** [Open hi-fi cover](https://letterformarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/lfa_skillscollections_0006_001-web.jpg)
- **Catalogue lead:** [Skills 6 record](https://oa.letterformarchive.org/item?workID=lfa_skillscollections_0006)

### Direct observations
- The cover is dominated by a photograph of a graffiti-covered subway train in perspective.
- The “SKILLS” masthead at upper left is a custom outlined display construction with broad geometric strokes, clipped / stepped internal joins and a strong horizontal rhythm.
- A vertical “SKILLS MAGAZINE #6” spine element uses a separate condensed sans-like treatment.
- The photographed train writing is numerous and visually important, but too small in this reproduction to support reliable individual glyph measurements.

### NIGHTSHIFT relevance
Keep the cover masthead, spine typography and graffiti in the train photograph as three separate evidence layers. The masthead is a useful custom-display-lettering reference, but should not be mistaken for the graffiti photographed on the train. The train image is an index for future item-level flick selection, not a counted individual piece analysis.

## 6. TCI Crew — interior spread from Graffito (circa 1990s)

- **Article caption:** TCI Crew, interior spread from *Graffito*, circa 1990s.
- **Gallery image:** [Open hi-fi spread](https://letterformarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/lfa_graffiti_0031_002-web.jpg)

### Direct observations
- The left page contains a colour photograph of a train piece with angular, overlapping letter masses, dark outlines and separate dimensional / shadow regions.
- The lower half of the spread shows a monochrome outline sketch on ruled paper. The sketch uses large angular contours, overlapping forms and several sharp internal cuts.
- Dice illustrations sit around the sketch as separate page decorations.
- The right page is mostly dark negative space; the spread uses a strong left/right editorial contrast.
- The exact word is not confidently legible from this view.

### NIGHTSHIFT hypotheses
- The monochrome sketch is particularly useful for a later close study of front-face contour and overlap without colour fill.
- Compare the upper painted piece and lower sketch as two different representations; do not assume they are the same piece or the same construction stage.
- The dice are decorative page elements and should not be absorbed into the letter grammar.
- Pair relationships, overlap order and negative-space channels should be recorded separately from the glyph silhouettes.

### Limits
The caption credits the spread to TCI Crew, but does not identify the individual writer of the pictured piece in the article. No individual author or style-family label is inferred.

## Cross-object findings

1. **Magazine mastheads are not graffiti pieces.** Skills and The Bomb provide useful display-lettering studies, but their editorial role must remain explicit.
2. **A page can contain several different evidence types.** IGTimes 6 and Skills 6 mix editorial typography with photographs of actual pieces; the photo subjects are separate specimens.
3. **Monochrome sketches are high-value construction evidence.** The lower TCI sketch removes fill and depth distractions while retaining contour relationships. It deserves a dedicated glyph-level follow-up if a higher-resolution crop can be inspected.
4. **Dense overlaps are relational.** Just Hangin’ Out and the TCI sketch show why word-level overlap cannot be represented by one global scalar alone.
5. **Composition must not inflate style counts.** Editorial spreads and multi-piece covers are recorded as inspected objects, but are not counted as individually reviewed examples for Bubble, Straight, Blockbuster, Throw-up or Wildstyle.
