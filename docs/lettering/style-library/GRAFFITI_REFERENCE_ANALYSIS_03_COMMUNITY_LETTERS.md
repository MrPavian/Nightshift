# Graffiti Reference Analysis 03 — Community Letters and Flick Trading

**Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Source section:** Letterform Archive, *Subscription to Mischief*, “Community Service: Letters and Flicks”  
**Status:** five item-level records added as metadata leads; no new image has been counted as visually inspected.

## Curatorial context

The Letterform Archive explains that before online platforms such as Art Crimes, Flickr and Instagram, writers exchanged photographs and correspondence by mail. Letters were often illustrated and included stickers, sketches and notes. The Archive says these longhand letters were usually signed with tags or throw-ups and document a range of handstyles. Writers also photocopied blackbook sketches for submissions, correspondence and personal letterheads.

Source: https://exhibitions.letterformarchive.org/mischief/exhibit/community-service

This is meaningful contextual evidence for handstyle research. It does not mean every letter page is itself a clean or legible tag specimen.

## What we can infer safely

- The Archive treats mailed letters and flick exchanges as part of graffiti's documentation and community infrastructure.
- The correspondence material can contain tags, throw-ups, sketches and personal letterheads.
- A paired letter-and-sketch object such as Spel's may be particularly useful for studying the relationship between ordinary handwriting, personal handstyle and drawn letter construction.
- Different writers and places make this a potentially useful comparative corpus, but only after individual image review.

## What we cannot infer yet

- We cannot describe the actual handstyles in these five objects without inspecting their images.
- We cannot classify any of these five records as tag, throw-up, bubble, straight letter or wildstyle based on the writer's name, date, place or medium.
- We cannot use the catalogue section's general statement about letters being signed with tags or throw-ups as proof that each specific item visibly contains one.
- We cannot infer glyph anatomy from the existence of a sketch alone.

## Generator implication

This suggests a separate **handstyle / correspondence research track**, distinct from production-letter grammar:

1. preserve natural pen stroke and line rhythm as a separate drawing system;
2. distinguish normal handwriting from a graffiti handstyle and from a drawn piece;
3. allow a correspondence page to contain several content classes (letter text, tag, sketch, sticker, note);
4. keep the paper substrate and photocopy reproduction effects separate from the original strokes;
5. do not merge handstyle rules into Bubble / Blockbuster / Throw-up construction.

## Next visual review

Open each image from the object page at the largest available size. Record:
- whether the page contains a tag or throw-up, and exactly where;
- whether there is a separate sketch;
- pen / marker stroke weight and line quality only where visually evident;
- baseline, slant, spacing and letter connections;
- whether the page is a photocopy or original;
- any uncertainty caused by reproduction quality.

Only then can an item be counted as a visually reviewed handstyle specimen. These five records currently remain metadata-only and do not increase the inspected-style totals.
