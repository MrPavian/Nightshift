# Graffiti Reference Acquisition 03 — Letterform Archive Permanent Collection

**Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Status:** source/access strategy plus first hi-fi gallery inspection pass; six item-level records added in the follow-up analysis.

## Why this source matters

Letterform Archive's October 19, 2026 article describes a permanent graffiti collection built from 2021–2024. The Archive reports holding **700 books, zines and other ephemera** spanning the 1980s through today. Its focus is printed material rather than a large standalone photographic archive. The article also states that the gallery below the interview contains hi-fi captures that can be opened full-screen and enlarged.

The article is particularly valuable because it was developed with graffiti writer, type designer and educator Kel Troughton, who acted as curatorial consultant. The article emphasizes community terminology, provenance, and the importance of writers themselves in identifying historically undocumented material.

Source: https://letterformarchive.org/news/an-undercover-look-at-letterform-archives-graffiti-collection/

## Evidence available from the article

- Collection scale: 700 books, zines and ephemera.
- Chronological scope: 1980s through today.
- Material types: mostly printed works, rare zines and books; some posters, prints, correspondence and original drawings.
- Hi-fi gallery captures are explicitly identified as enlargeable.
- Research visits can be scheduled free of charge; visitors can request specific material.
- The Archive's curatorial consultant stresses the importance of using terms that are correct within graffiti-writing culture.
- Provenance and community identification are treated as important parts of the collection record.

These points establish a strong research route. They do **not** yet establish individual letterform specimens or their style labels.

Do not assign these to Bubble, Throw-up, Blockbuster, Simple/Straight or Wildstyle based on title, creator name or period alone. Each image must be opened and its visible letterforms assessed independently.

## Practical acquisition plan

### Pass A — inspect online gallery

Open each hi-fi gallery image from the October 2026 article. For every image actually opened, record:
- stable object URL or gallery caption;
- creator / writer credit exactly as printed;
- photographer / publisher / zine credit if supplied;
- publication date and page number if visible;
- visible glyphs and exact readable text;
- letter construction observations separate from composition and printing artifacts;
- whether the image is sufficiently clear for structural analysis.

### Pass B — request targeted research visit

The Archive states that free research visits can be scheduled and specific material can be requested. Ask for:
- Skills 1–7, especially original spreads and any tracing-paper construction studies;
- IGTimes 6 and other PHASE 2 editorial material;
- issues of 12oz Prophet, Graphotism, Girlz Bomb and Can Control;
- original sketches, stickers and correspondence with visible handstyles;
- any material catalogued under throw-ups, silvers, burners, handstyles or freight writing.

Request item-level terminology and provenance from staff rather than imposing our own style classification.

### Pass C — select specimens

Only count an item as individually reviewed after its actual image/page has been opened at sufficient size. Keep the following evidence levels distinct:
- `catalogue_metadata_only`
- `image_preview_seen`
- `hi_fi_image_opened_and_inspected`
- `physical_item_inspected`

A specimen may be assigned a candidate style family only when the image and/or an authoritative source supports that label. If the label is our own inference, mark it explicitly as such and include the visible evidence.

## Rights and repository policy

Hi-fi viewing access and research-visit access do not grant reproduction rights. Do not copy image binaries into NIGHTSHIFT. Keep external links, catalogue metadata and original written analysis. Seek permission before publishing or redistributing any archival image.

## Generator relevance

The Archive's emphasis on letterform invention, community vocabulary and provenance supports a data model where:
- the source terminology is preserved verbatim;
- our internal grammar labels are stored separately;
- each observation is linked to a specific object and page;
- construction rules are not inferred from a writer's name or a magazine's era;
- editorial layout, reproduction artifacts and actual painted letter anatomy are kept distinct.
