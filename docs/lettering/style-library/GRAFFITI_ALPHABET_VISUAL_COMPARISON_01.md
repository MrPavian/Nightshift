# Graffiti Alphabet Visual Comparison 01 — Controlled A–Z Sheets and Letter Families

**Status:** first visual comparison pass; educational / generator sheets only, not a practitioner-reviewed corpus  
**Date:** 19 November 2026  
**Scope:** visual inspection of published A–Z sheets and same-letter comparisons across Bubble, Bomb/Throw-up, Tag, Wild/Semi-Wild, 3D, shadow, Gothic/Old English, Block and Angular examples. No source artwork is copied into the repository.

## 2. Visual findings by family

### A. Tag / handstyle — V03

**Direct visual observations**
- The alphabet is drawn as narrow, mostly single-weight marker-like strokes rather than closed, inflated block contours.
- Several terminals extend into directional tails or arrow-like endings.
- The glyphs are individually displayed; the sheet does not demonstrate a continuous one-stroke name or a verified stroke order.
- Width, slant and terminal treatment vary from letter to letter.

**Design inference for NIGHTSHIFT**
- A tag should not be generated by taking a filled uppercase glyph and making its outline thinner.
- It needs a centreline / stroke-path representation, editable stroke order, joins, lifts, speed/pressure hypotheses and terminal gestures.
- A static alphabet can inform shape vocabulary, but it cannot prove ductus or motion. Keep Tag / Handstyle separate from the current contour-master pipeline until HST-01 exists.

**Do not infer:** that all tags are monoline, arrow-ended, uppercase or written without lifts. V03 is one educational alphabet, not a universal handstyle rule.

### B. Wild / Semi-Wild — V01

**Direct visual observations**
- The sheet has a strong angular and directional character: pointed terminals, clipped corners, triangular cuts and short extensions.
- Several glyphs contain internal directional marks or arrow-like features.
- Despite the source label, the letters are presented as separate alphabet specimens. The sheet does not show actual pair-specific shared contours or a word-level relationship graph.
- Heavy outline, orange fill and black depth/shadow contribute substantially to the visual impact.

**Design inference**
- This is useful as a study of *glyph-level angular moves*, not proof of true interlocking Wildstyle.
- Extensions should be attached to named structural terminals or axes, not sprinkled around the glyph as decoration.
- Real interlocking must be evaluated in pairs and words: attachment, crossing, overlap, continuation, shared contour and negative-space channels need explicit relationships.
- A style specimen should be re-rendered as a plain monochrome front-face before any geometry is accepted.

**Important distinction:** GraffSpace itself describes this particular sheet as Semi-Wildstyle and says the letters in a real piece change with their neighbours. That distinction supports NIGHTSHIFT's separation of individual glyph grammar from composition grammar.

### C. Bomb / throw-up-like — V02

**Direct visual observations**
- The letters use large rounded masses, soft lobes and irregular outer contours.
- The forms are not simply identical ovals: bowl sizes, widths, shoulders and terminals vary by glyph.
- Some letters retain small counters; others have open or near-solid interior structures.
- The O contains a cartoon face, which is a character/decoration layer rather than a reusable letter-construction rule.

**Design inference**
- Preserve glyph-specific anatomy while inflating. A, B, P, R, O and G require different counter/aperture handling.
- A compact throw-up can use a Bubble-like letterform, but “throw-up” remains a practice / work-format context, not a synonym for Bubble.
- The sheet is a good counterexample to a perfectly uniform geometric bubble font: controlled asymmetry is part of its visual character.

### D. Bubble — V04

**Direct visual observations**
- The alphabet uses rounded, inflated bodies with smooth external contours and compact internal openings.
- The letter family is visually unified by a consistent soft mass, but widths and internal structures still vary.
- Gloss highlights, multiple outlines and colour gradients are prominent.

**Design inference**
- The front-face silhouette must carry the Bubble identity in monochrome; gloss and outlines are not evidence of successful construction.
- Do not apply the same rounding radius blindly to every corner. Bowls, necks, terminals and junctions need role-specific handling.
- Counter area, counter opening, neck strength and open apertures must be measured independently. The current C03 negative-space gate is therefore a necessary safety layer, not a style-quality score.

### E. 3D and drop shadow — V05 / V06

**Direct visual observations**
- V05 shows a consistent extrusion direction behind the letter faces. The face geometry is visually simpler than the overall rendered result.
- V06 uses a black offset silhouette behind white marker-like letters. The shadow is a displaced copy / exposed offset region, not a new letter skeleton.
- Both examples achieve much of their depth and weight through the secondary layer.

**Design inference**
- 3D extrusion and drop shadow belong to rendering / presentation, not to the letterform grammar.
- Keep a canonical front-face path. Derive extrusion and shadow from it, with a shared direction and explicit depth.
- Never count a thicker outline, shadow or extrusion as a stronger glyph stem or as a structural connection.

### F. Gothic / Old English — V07 and V09

**Direct visual observations**
- V07 has pronounced thick/thin contrast, broken or angular stem transitions, pointed terminals and calligraphic curves.
- The Y example in V09 shows a distinct blackletter-derived construction with contrasting strokes and a long, curling terminal.
- These forms are structurally unlike the current neutral A–Z contour masters.

**Design inference**
- Blackletter needs a separate script-derived glyph system, including stroke contrast, pen-angle logic, arches, joins and terminal families.
- Do not produce it by adding spikes, serifs or curls to the current Straight masters.
- Old English / Blackletter and Chicano lettering traditions should not be collapsed into one generic “Gothic” toggle without source-specific distinctions.

## 3. Controlled comparison lessons

### Same word, different family

Graffiti Art presents the same word, “PIECE”, in Bubble, Block and Angular forms. Its published examples specify:
- Bubble: 36 units of stroke weight per 100 units of letter height; upright; rounded ends.
- Block: 30/100; upright; square, flat ends.
- Angular: 24/100; approximately 11° forward slant; pointed blade-like free terminals.

These are the site's own contemporary generator settings, not street-wide measurements. Their value is methodological: hold the word and artboard constant, then compare the actual front-face geometry. The three variants also differ in default overlap and finish, so NIGHTSHIFT should strip those treatments out before comparing construction.

### Same glyph, different family

Graffiti Letters' Y page describes the glyph as a V joined to a descending stem, then shows family-specific outcomes: a rounded short-stem Bubble Y, a squared Blockbuster Y, a Wild-style curling extension, a Throw-up form and an Old English form. This is a useful model for a per-glyph variant matrix.

The page's tutorial vocabulary is useful, but its alphabet is an educational / generator product. It should not be treated as a primary record of how writers universally construct Y.

### Construction teaching

Zumola's lesson separates block letters (outer boundary, then bars / internal gaps) from Bubble letters (rounded shapes, then smoothed outline) and explicitly warns against collapsing inter-letter gaps to solve wide glyphs. This is a useful beginner-level construction baseline. It is deliberately simplified and should not become NIGHTSHIFT's full style grammar.

### Taxonomy method

MoMA's record for Evan Roth's *Graffiti Taxonomy: New York* describes a Paris study that selected ten frequent letters and compared eighteen tags in upper- and lowercase, from simple to ornate. The key lesson is to compare the *same letter across many independent writers*, rather than infer a style from one finished word. This method is more valuable to NIGHTSHIFT than importing any single alphabet as a master.

## 4. Cross-family comparison matrix

| Family / treatment | What changes in the front-face geometry? | What belongs outside the glyph? | Separate NIGHTSHIFT system |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tag / Handstyle | Gesture path, stroke direction, joins, terminal movement | Marker texture, ink bleed, wall surface | Centreline / motor-plan engine |
| Bubble | Rounded mass, bowl tension, counter and neck geometry | Gloss, colour, outline stack, drips | Glyph construction + negative-space gates |
| Bomb / Throw-up | Simplification, compact mass, glyph-specific inflation | Speed context, fill/outline convention, palette | Letterform + practice/format context |
| Straight / Block | Stem/bar geometry, width class, corner and terminal logic | 3D block, shadow, fill | Letterform grammar |
| Angular / Blade | Facets, cuts, tapers, pointed terminals | Split fill, highlights, extrusion | Separate angular operation family |
| Wild / Semi-Wild | Glyph extensions may alter contours | Actual joins, crossings and word-level flow | C04 relationship / composition solver |
| Gothic / Blackletter | Script-derived skeleton, contrast, arches, terminals | Texture, outline, colour, depth | Separate script glyph library |
| 3D / Shadow | Normally no new front-face anatomy | Extrusion, offset copy, lighting | Rendering pipeline |

## 5. Concrete NIGHTSHIFT decisions

1. **Do not add more cosmetic presets to C03.** The next design work should be original, annotated glyph studies that establish how the letter is built.
2. **Retain the Creator 0.2 skeleton → bars → style-moves model as a separate experimental track.** It is a better fit for repeatable stylistic moves than pretending the existing contour resolver can express every family. It still needs glyph-master integration and visual review.
3. **Tag is not a thin font.** HST-01 needs gesture data and should remain unavailable as a production generator feature until that exists.
4. **Wildstyle is not an alphabet-only feature.** A sheet of isolated glyphs can show angular moves, but it cannot prove interlocking.
5. **Blackletter is a separate script system.** Do not derive it from the sans-like master alphabet.
6. **Rendering is orthogonal.** 3D, shadow, chrome, gloss and outline must be switchable without changing the canonical front-face geometry.
7. **Use letter-family drills.** Start with straight stems (E/F/H/I/L/T), round forms (C/G/O/Q/S), bowls (B/D/P/R), diagonals (A/K/M/N/V/W/X/Y/Z) and hooks (J/U). This grouping is useful for test coverage, not a claim that writers all learn in this order.
8. **Do not calibrate from these sheets alone.** The examples are too few, some are synthetic/educational, and their rights/authorial provenance is not sufficient for production assets.

## 8. Limitations / next evidence pass

- This pass inspected published teaching and generator alphabets, not a representative set of original blackbook pages by independently identified writers.
- Several sheets are deliberately simplified and may contain generated, commercial or editorially normalised forms.
- The visual examples do not establish regional chronology or a universal writer practice.
- No stroke order, speed, pressure, cap choice or execution sequence is inferred from static outlines.
- The next pass must inspect individual blackbook / ink-outline pages with traceable authorship and compare repeated letters across writers. MoMA's taxonomy method is the model; a single writer's alphabet must remain a single-writer grammar, not become a universal preset.
