# Source Register — Graffiti Lettering Styles

Research pass: 19 November 2026. Tutorial resources, scholarly sources, museum interpretation and practitioner testimony are not interchangeable.

## Core graffiti / lettering sources

### S1 — Kunstmuseum Stuttgart / StadtPalais Stuttgart
Wände | Walls — Graffiti im Kessel, exhibition materials and glossary (2020–2021).
- Exhibition: https://www.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/ausstellungen/graffiti-im-kessel
- Brochure: https://www.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/sites/default/files/2021-04/waendewalls_graffiti_im_kessel_broschuere_webversion.pdf
Supports German contextual terminology including Tag, Throw-up, Simple Style, Semi-Wildstyle and Wildstyle. Museum interpretation, not a technical construction manual.

### S2 — GraffSpace
Graffiti Alphabet A–Z: https://www.graffspace.com/de/graffiti-alphabet
Visual discovery of A–Z sheets in several approaches. Educational web content, not scholarly authority; image reuse rights must be checked.

### S3 — Graffiti-Letters.com
Alphabet A–Z in twelve styles: https://graffiti-letters.com/letters/
Supports same-letter comparison across styles and tutorial-linked alphabet references. Site material, not historical authority; no blanket commercial/repository reuse permission.

### S4 — Graffiti Art
- Letter structure: https://www.graffitiart.co/learn/letter-structure/
- Styles: https://www.graffitiart.co/styles/
- Lessons: https://www.graffitiart.co/learn/
Supports skeleton → stroke weight → finished letter distinction, counters as negative spaces and contemporary design profiles for Bubble, Block and Angular. Not historical scholarship.

### S5 — Lisa Gottlieb (2006)
Applying Panofsky’s Theories of Iconographical Analysis to Graffiti Art: Implications for Access to Images of Non-Representational/Abstract Art, PhD thesis, University of Toronto.
https://www.collectionscanada.gc.ca/obj/thesescanada/vol2/002/NR21858.PDF
Supports classification complexity and disagreement among experts around Blockbuster, Straight letters, Wild and Dimensional. Does not settle universal definitions.

### S6 — Nancy Macdonald (2001)
The Graffiti Subculture: Youth, Masculinity and Identity in London and New York, Palgrave Macmillan.
https://student.cc.uoc.gr/uploadFiles/181-%CE%A0%CE%9F%CE%93%CE%9A329/graffiti%20subculture.pdf
Ethnographic context for throw-ups, bubble lettering, simple outlines/fills and writer accounts. London/New York scope; do not flatten regional variation.

### S7 — Joe Austin (2001)
Taking the Train: How Graffiti Art Became an Urban Crisis in New York City, Columbia University Press.
https://doi.org/10.7312/aust11142
Historical contrast between rounded softie forms and squarer blockbuster lettering; New York historical scope.

### S8 — Sanja Ewald in conversation with Mirko Reisser (DAIM), 2023
The Spray Can as an Attitude to Life between Illegal Action and Commercial Art, De Gruyter.
https://doi.org/10.1515/9783111326306-009
Practitioner perspective on style writing, movement, complex letter structure and relation between name and composition. Attributed perspective, not universal rule.

### S9 — New-York Historical Society
TRACY 168, WILD STYLE (1992), collection record.
https://emuseum.nyhistory.org/objects/53361/wild-style-tracy-168
Dated catalogued object with inscription WILD STYLE; provenance anchor, not proof of universal grammar.

### S10 — Graffiti Archaeography, Western Sydney University repository
https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/files/94853086/uws_15396.pdf
Secondary discussion of Wildstyle / semi-Wildstyle, Blockbusters and Throw-ups. Consult exact page context before public-facing claims.

### S11 — Auer Verlag / Karin Mann
Street Art: Graffiti — Schritt für Schritt zum Style Writing, sample lesson:
https://www.auer-verlag.de/media/ntx/auer/sample/08808DA5_Musterseite.pdf
Educational sequence separating outline, rounding/inflating, spikes/extensions and matching effects to form. School-art teaching material, not historical taxonomy.

## Expanded sources added in this pass

### S12 — Thomas Dürst (2020; updated 2023)
Graffiti und Street Art: Style Writing — eine kleine Stilkunde.
https://duerst.eu/blog/20-01-07_Graffiti-Stilkunde.htm
German-language overview of terms including Tag, Throw-Up, Piece, Blockbuster, Schraffo, Semi-Wildstyle, Simple Style, Wildstyle, Hollow and Bubble. Useful for terminology comparison; not a scholarly authority.

### S13 — Aesthetics of Graffiti: Comparison to Text-Based and Pictorial Artforms (2020)
SAGE journal article.
https://doi.org/10.1177/0276237420951415
Discusses tags as signatures, abstraction and fluid drawing movement/handstyle. Research context; does not define every tag tradition.

### S14 — Drawing: Research, Theory, Practice
University of Aberdeen repository record:
https://research.aub.ac.uk/id/document/712
Academic discussion of tag, throw-up, piece and wildstyle as distinct writing practices. Consult full text and bibliographic metadata before quoting.

### S15 — Graffiti Art
Styles and letter structure resources, especially Straight and Angular profiles:
https://www.graffitiart.co/styles/
Contemporary design reference; use for visual vocabulary, not historical claims.

### S16 — Graffiti Archaeography
Western Sydney University repository PDF:
https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/files/94853086/uws_15396.pdf
Secondary source discussing distinctions among semi-wildstyle, wildstyle, blockbuster and throw-up. Page 97 contains a relevant discussion in the indexed PDF; verify pagination in the source PDF before formal citation.

### S17 — Claudia Walde (2018)
Street Fonts: Graffiti Alphabets from Around the World. Thames & Hudson.
https://books.google.com/books?id=hgqHswEACAAJ
Comparative collection of alphabets by 154 graffiti artists from 30 countries. Useful for diversity and alphabet comparison; individual artwork rights remain with their creators/publishers.

### S18 — Rob Roy Kelly American Wood Type Collection, University of Texas at Austin
Blackletter & Brush / Script:
https://rrk.finearts.utexas.edu/woodtype/script/
Defines Blackletter as a broad family and distinguishes Textura, Rotunda, Schwabacher and Fraktur. Typographic/historical source, not graffiti-specific.

### S19 — Robert Bringhurst
The Elements of Typographic Style, section 11.4 “Blackletters”.
Bibliographic record/edition details should be verified for the version used. Discusses major blackletter families including Textura, Fraktur, Bastarda and Rotunda. Do not use a pirated scan as a repository asset.

### S20 — Jürgen Spitzmüller (2011)
The ‘typographical manifesto’: Blackletter/roman typeface variation as a social practice in Germany.
https://www.spitzmueller.org/docs/handout-heidelberg-2011-09-19.pdf
Academic conference handout with terminology and chronology for Textura, Rotunda, Bastarda, Schwabacher and Fraktur. Cultural-political context matters; do not treat Blackletter as neutral decoration.

### S21 — Museum für Islamische Kunst, Staatliche Museen zu Berlin
Calligraffiti — teaching materials:
https://islamic-art.smb.museum/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Praesentation_Calligraffiti-1.pdf
https://islamic-art.smb.museum/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Handreichung_Arbeitsboegen_Calligraffiti-2.pdf
Defines Calligraffiti as a combination of calligraphy and graffiti and provides educational context. Cultural context and source traditions must be handled carefully.

### S22 — Museum für Islamische Kunst, Staatliche Museen zu Berlin
Present: How do you create something new from the old? — Calligraffiti info sheet.
https://islamic-art.smb.museum/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/201030_E_Arbeitsblaetter_Aktion_Gegenwart_Kalligrafie_EN.pdf
Educational framing of calligraphy and graffiti as related but distinct practices.

### S23 — Enrico Bonadio (2023)
Creativity and Originality of Graffiti Lettering, in Copyright in the Street, Cambridge University Press.
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/copyright-in-the-street/creativity-and-originality-of-graffiti-lettering/9BE0A6B09D7FCAF73249DE5DE4D24E02
Discusses creativity through reinterpretation, deconstruction and reshaping of alphabetic letters. Relevant to originality policy, not a style manual.

### S24 — Theresa Heyd & Alexandra Karnatz (2020)
Handstyle as social meaning: Graphic variation, graffiti and gender.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amper.2020.100063
Open-access research on graphic variation, handstyle and social meaning in a German urban context. Do not generalize the case study to all writers or regions.

## Evidence handling
- Cite claims in each card and distinguish source observation from NIGHTSHIFT inference.
- Record exact PDF page numbers after page-level inspection; indexed page labels can differ from printed pagination.
- Do not infer stroke order, speed, tool angle, cap choice or aerosol technique from still images.
- Online alphabets are not automatically reusable assets.
- If a source only supports a broad term, do not use it to validate detailed generator parameters.
