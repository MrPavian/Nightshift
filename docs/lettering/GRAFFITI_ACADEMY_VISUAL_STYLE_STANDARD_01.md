# NIGHTSHIFT — GRAFFITI ACADEMY VISUAL STYLE STANDARD 01

**Status:** Binding visual reference standard  
**Reference:** Owner-approved A-study reference image, 15 November 2026  
**Applies to:** Letter Construction Academy — uppercase, lowercase and numerals

## 1. Visual target

The approved reference is a graffiti blackbook / workshop infographic, not a clean typographic worksheet. It combines a compact technical teaching layout with the material character of a used blackbook page.

Required qualities:
- tactile warm-white / pale concrete paper;
- subtle paper grain, scuffs, photocopy wear and restrained concrete-like distress;
- strong black ink, hand-drawn marker headings and bold section bars;
- yellow as the dominant signal/highlighter colour;
- violet/purple for construction lines, arrows and selected paint accents;
- occasional original spray splashes, drips and aerosol marks at the edges and around the graffiti examples;
- clear nine-panel grid, heavy imperfect panel outlines and hand-marked numbering;
- a large, expressive NIGHTSHIFT masthead and a clear character-study title;
- energetic graffiti examples that visibly carry the visual weight of the page.

Distress is a framing/material layer. It must never obscure labels, construction lines, letter anatomy or learning content. No random grunge that reduces legibility.

## 2. Page format and composition

- DIN A4 landscape, 297 × 210 mm, ratio 1.414:1.
- Master artboard: 1680 × 1188 px or vector equivalent.
- Header band across the top: NIGHTSHIFT identity on the left, character study title in the centre/right, master-reference note on the right.
- Nine numbered learning panels in a three-column × three-row composition below the header.
- Footer banner across the page with one concise learning objective.
- Preserve generous internal margins. Do not allow titles, labels, arrows or graffiti art to collide with panel borders.
- The layout is a consistent system, not a rigid requirement that every panel has identical content density. Adapt diagrams to the actual glyph.

## 3. Palette and material

Use this palette as a starting point; keep contrast high:
- Ink / outline: near-black #111111.
- Paper: warm off-white #F2EEE6.
- Signal yellow: #F5D400 (highlighter / paint accent).
- Construction violet: #8756A7.
- Secondary graphite: #3A3836.
- White: #FFFFFF.

Use yellow for section numbers, key terms, marker swipes and selected graffiti highlights. Use violet for construction geometry, dimension lines, arrows and selective paint accents. Black remains the primary letter and text colour.

## 4. Typography and drawing language

- Main masthead: expressive hand-painted / marker-like lettering, original to NIGHTSHIFT.
- Section headings: bold condensed marker lettering, white on irregular black bars.
- Body copy: compact, highly legible hand-lettered or condensed sans-serif style.
- Use consistent German terminology; retain established English graffiti terms in parentheses where helpful.
- Technical diagrams: crisp black contour, violet construction geometry, yellow measurement/reference marks.
- Graffiti drawings: original custom letterforms with meaningful structural variation. Never use a stock font glyph as a substitute for a graffiti construction.
- Use hand-drawn arrows, circles, ticks and leader lines only when they point to the correct feature.

## 5. The nine fixed learning panels

Keep this order and core teaching purpose:

1. **Grundform** — neutral reference form with construction grid and core geometry.
2. **Buchstabenanatomie** — actual letter parts and negative spaces; only label parts that exist.
3. **Proportionen & Raster** — height, width, baseline, guides and character-specific proportions. Mark numerical ratios as example values unless they are explicitly part of a chosen construction.
4. **Konstruktionsanalyse** — visible step-by-step construction from skeleton to finished basic form. Every step must add a real structural layer.
5. **Formvariationen** — at least five structurally different variants, not five cosmetic treatments.
6. **Formcharakter** — concise comparison of the effect of geometry, weight, width, angle, rhythm and negative space.
7. **Kalligrafie & Duktus** — tool comparison and movement study, clearly separating observed process from inferred movement.
8. **Graffiti-Transfer** — prominent original graffiti lettering explorations. For the A reference, show distinct throw-up-oriented, block-letter and piece/Wildstyle-oriented directions. These are parallel design approaches, not a universal progression.
9. **Lernkontrolle** — practical tasks that culminate in a learner-created original graffiti letter.

## 6. Graffiti is the destination

Graffiti must not be a token panel or a decorative afterthought:
- Panels 5 and 8 must contain substantial, original lettering.
- At least three examples in panel 8 must be immediately distinguishable by silhouette and construction, not only by colour.
- Letterform decisions come before outline, fill, highlights, shadow or 3D effects.
- Show readable construction and intentional negative spaces.
- Make the A recognisably an A while allowing bold stylistic choices.
- Avoid copying identifiable writers, crews, signatures, tags or existing pieces.
- Never imply Tag → Throw-up → Piece is a mandatory learning ladder.

## 7. Accuracy and evidence safeguards

- Anatomy must be specific to the character. For uppercase A, possible labels include apex, left/right diagonal stems (Schenkel), crossbar (Querbalken), counter/negative space (Innenraum/Punze), feet and terminals. Label only what the diagram actually shows.
- A conventional uppercase A does not have an x-height in the same sense as lowercase letters. Do not label a generic horizontal guide as “X-Linie” without explaining the chosen system.
- Height, width, crossbar position and counter size are design variables. Any percentage shown is an example for that drawing, not a universal rule.
- A skeleton is a centreline/structural guide; it is not the same thing as a contour or filled glyph.
- Keep skeleton, stroke body, contour, fill and presentation effects distinct.
- A still image cannot establish stroke order, number of movements, pressure, speed, tool angle or aerosol process. Label movement diagrams as hypotheses unless process evidence exists.
- Broad nib, brush and marker behaviour can be compared, but do not claim that their mechanics transfer directly to aerosol.
- Distinguish calligraphy, handstyle and graffiti lettering; show their relationship without collapsing them into one category.

## 8. Production and print quality

- Work as vector-first artwork (SVG) wherever practical.
- Keep all text editable and proofread. Do not rely on AI-rendered lettering for final labels.
- Inspect at actual A4 landscape size and at 100% zoom.
- Check clipping, overlap, spelling, line contrast, leader-line targets and panel balance.
- Ensure a monochrome print remains understandable; yellow/violet must not carry meaning alone.
- Distress layers must be removable or reducible for a clean print variant.
- Export a print-ready PDF/PNG only after content and layout QA.
- Keep an editable master and a clean print/export variant.
- Do not mark a sheet approved until the owner has reviewed it.
