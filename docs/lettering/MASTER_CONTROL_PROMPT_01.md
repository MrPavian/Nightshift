# NIGHTSHIFT – LETTER CONSTRUCTION ACADEMY
## MASTER CONTROL PROMPT 01 – LETTER STUDY

**Version:** 1.2  
**Reference character:** A  
**Scope:** Uppercase A–Z, lowercase a–z and numerals 0–9  
**Binding visual standard:** docs/lettering/GRAFFITI_ACADEMY_VISUAL_STYLE_STANDARD_01.md

## 1. Mission

Create a systematic, technically accurate and visually consistent learning sheet for one character. **The destination is original graffiti lettering.** Typography and calligraphy provide foundations for understanding construction, rhythm, negative space and tool behaviour; they are not the end product.

The owner-approved reference image is the binding visual direction: tactile blackbook / graffiti-workshop infographic, warm distressed paper, black marker headings and outlines, yellow highlighter accents, violet construction marks, nine numbered panels, expressive original graffiti examples and a bold footer. Follow the separate visual standard for exact layout, palette, hierarchy and print safeguards.

Do not revert to a clean typographic worksheet. Graffiti must be visually substantial and central.

## 2. Fixed page system

- DIN A4 landscape, 297 × 210 mm; master artboard 1680 × 1188 px or equivalent.
- Header: NIGHTSHIFT masthead, character study title and master-reference note.
- Body: nine numbered panels in a three-column × three-row arrangement.
- Footer: concise learning objective.
- Material: warm off-white / concrete-like paper, restrained photocopy wear and scuffs.
- Palette: near-black ink, warm paper, signal yellow, violet construction lines, white.
- Section headings: bold white marker lettering on black bars; yellow numbered markers.
- Main diagrams: crisp and technically readable; graffiti examples: expressive and original.
- Texture and paint effects must never obscure text, anatomy, leader lines or construction.
- Keep all final labels editable, correctly spelled and proofread. Do not use image-generated text as final copy.

## 3. Mandatory nine-panel structure

Keep this order for every character. Adapt the content to the actual anatomy; do not force a universal glyph template.

### 01 — Grundform
Show the neutral reference form with construction grid and core geometry. Keep it free of style effects.

### 02 — Buchstabenanatomie
Label only components that actually occur. Show negative spaces as active shapes. For uppercase A, possible terms include apex, left/right Schenkel, Querbalken, Innenraum/Punze, Füße and Endungen.

### 03 — Proportionen & Raster
Show height, width, baseline, relevant guides, crossbar position and counter proportions. Mark numerical ratios as example values for this drawing, never as universal rules. A conventional uppercase letter does not use x-height in the same sense as lowercase; do not label a generic guide “X-Linie” without explaining it.

### 04 — Konstruktionsanalyse
Show a coherent sequence from skeleton/structural guide to stroke body, structural components, contour and finished basic form. Each step must visibly add a meaningful layer. Distinguish skeleton, contour and filled shape.

### 05 — Formvariationen
Show at least five structurally distinct original variants. Vary width, height, slant, weight, angles, curvature, terminals, joins, negative space or rhythm. Differences must be visible in silhouette and construction, not only in colour or effects.

### 06 — Formcharakter
Compare how geometry, weight, width, angle, rhythm, spacing and negative space affect the letter's character. Descriptors are analytical observations, not rigid style categories.

### 07 — Kalligrafie & Duktus
Show useful tool comparisons (e.g. broad nib, brush, marker) and explain how tool behaviour can affect stroke contrast, rhythm and movement. Distinguish observed process from inferred movement. Static drawings cannot prove stroke order, number of movements, pressure, speed, tool angle or aerosol process. Do not claim that calligraphic tool mechanics transfer directly to spray-can technique.

### 08 — Graffiti-Transfer
This is a major visual panel, not a token add-on. Show at least three substantial, original and clearly differentiated graffiti-letter explorations. For A, useful parallel directions include:
- throw-up-oriented: fast, compact, rounded or inflated massing;
- block-style: bold, structured, controlled geometry and clear negative space;
- piece / Wildstyle-oriented: more complex original structure, connections, directional movement and planned overlaps.

These are design directions, not a universal Tag → Throw-up → Piece progression. Strong letter construction comes first; outlines, fills, inline, highlights, shadows and 3D effects support rather than replace it. Never copy identifiable writers, crews, signatures or existing pieces.

### 09 — Lernkontrolle
Provide three to five practical tasks that move from reconstruction to variation and culminate in the learner's own original graffiti-letter design. Include a word application where appropriate.

## 4. Educational and editorial safeguards

- Anatomy, terminology and construction must be correct for the specific glyph.
- A skeleton is a centreline or structural guide, not an outline.
- Keep skeleton, stroke body, contour, fill and presentation effects distinct.
- Treat counters and negative spaces as active design components.
- Do not invent universal proportions, stroke counts or construction recipes.
- Do not infer movement from a static contour; label hypotheses as hypotheses.
- Distinguish typography, calligraphy, handstyle and graffiti lettering while explaining useful relationships.
- Do not impose historical claims or a universal development ladder.
- All demonstrations must be original fictional designs.

## 5. Quality gates

A sheet is not ready for owner review until:
1. all nine panels are present, ordered and balanced;
2. the neutral glyph and anatomy are correct;
3. proportions are presented as character-specific, not universal;
4. construction stages visibly build on one another;
5. at least five variations differ structurally;
6. graffiti panel 08 contains at least three substantial original designs;
7. graffiti is not a minor decorative afterthought;
8. labels, arrows and leaders are accurate, legible and collision-free;
9. text is proofread and remains editable;
10. print-size and 100% inspections show no clipping or overlap;
11. a clean, legible monochrome output remains possible.

If any gate fails, revise before delivery. Do not scale to the next character until the owner approves the A reference sheet.

## 6. Production sequence

1. Read CLAUDE.md and the binding visual style standard.
2. Inspect the current SVG, reference image and any owner feedback.
3. Establish character-specific anatomy and factual teaching copy.
4. Build the nine-panel vector artwork.
5. Add texture and graffiti paint accents as separate, controllable layers.
6. Render at actual A4 landscape dimensions.
7. Inspect at fit-to-page and 100%; check all labels, leaders, panel boundaries and print contrast.
8. Update the project handover with exact paths, commit, proofs and remaining blockers.

**Graffiti lettering is the destination. Construction is the foundation. Calligraphy is one influence—not the finish line.**
