# NIGHTSHIFT – LETTER CONSTRUCTION ACADEMY
## MASTER CONTROL PROMPT 01 – LETTER STUDY

**Version:** 1.0  
**Reference letter:** A  
**Scope:** Uppercase A–Z, lowercase a–z and numerals 0–9

---

## 1. Purpose

Create a systematic, technically sound and visually consistent letter study for the NIGHTSHIFT Letter Construction Academy.

Every letter is examined using the same educational and visual framework. The goal is to understand construction, anatomy, proportions, negative space and design possibilities—not merely to generate decorative lettering.

The first reference study is the uppercase **A**. All subsequent studies must follow this production standard.

## 2. Fixed visual language

- Format: DIN A4 landscape, aspect ratio 1.414:1.
- Background: light neutral drawing paper.
- Grid: fine, subtle construction grid.
- Main drawing: black.
- Construction lines: restrained violet.
- Guides and measurement marks: yellow.
- Language: German.
- Typography: clear, legible technical labels.
- Style: precise editorial lettering study with a subtle blackbook character.
- Diagrams must be large enough to inspect.
- No decorative collage elements that compete with the analysis.

## 3. Mandatory study structure

Every letter study contains these nine sections, in this exact order:

### A — Grundform
Show the neutral letter as a geometric base construction.

### B — Buchstabenanatomie
Label only components that actually occur in the letter, for example stem, arm, crossbar, bowl, apex, foot, counter, terminal or join.

### C — Proportionen und Raster
Show cap height, x-height where relevant, baseline, width, component ratios and the position and size of counters/negative spaces.

### D — Konstruktionsanalyse
Show a coherent construction sequence:
1. Skeleton
2. Stroke weight
3. Form build-up
4. Connections
5. Finished basic form

Each step must visibly follow from the previous one.

### E — Formvariationen
Show at least five distinct variants. Initially vary only width, height, slant, stroke weight, angles and curvature. Do not add graffiti effects at this stage.

### F — Formcharakter
Compare the variants using useful descriptors such as geometric, organic, compact, wide, narrow, dynamic and static. Avoid treating these descriptors as rigid universal categories.

### G — Kalligrafie und Duktus
Explain how movement, rhythm and tool behaviour can influence the visible form. A static outline is not proof of stroke order, speed, pressure or tool movement; label inferred movement as a hypothesis unless supported by process evidence.

### H — Graffiti-Transfer
Show how the neutral form could be interpreted in different lettering directions. Clearly label this as a design exercise, not a historically mandatory development sequence. Do not copy identifiable writers, signatures, crews or existing pieces.

### I — Lernkontrolle
Provide three to five practical exercises that let the learner reconstruct, compare and vary the letter.

## 4. Technical and editorial quality rules

- Keep anatomy accurate and construction logically explainable.
- Distinguish skeleton, contour and filled shape.
- Treat counters and negative spaces as active design elements.
- Do not assume every letter follows the same construction logic.
- Account for meaningful alternate forms where relevant (for example one-storey and two-storey forms).
- Do not make unsupported historical claims.
- Treat calligraphy, typography and graffiti as related but distinct fields.
- Do not infer ductus from a static image alone.
- Do not present Tag → Throw-up → Piece as a universal development ladder.
- Keep letterform, movement/ductus, tool behaviour and presentation effects conceptually separate.

## 5. Consistency rules

Every new study must:
- use the same page structure and section order;
- use the same colour and line conventions;
- maintain comparable scale and detail;
- use correct German terminology;
- separate neutral construction from stylistic interpretation;
- preserve the visual identity of the Academy.

Do not redesign the layout for individual letters. Adapt the actual anatomy to the letter; never force a letter into an unsuitable template.

## 6. Final quality-control checklist

Before delivery, verify:

1. All nine sections are present and in order.
2. The letterform is correct and recognisable.
3. Construction and proportions are coherent.
4. Construction lines are distinguishable from final contours.
5. Labels are legible, accurate and in German.
6. The basic form is free of decorative effects.
7. Style interpretations are clearly identified as exercises.
8. Ductus claims are appropriately qualified.
9. The page matches the established Academy visual standard.

Correct errors before presenting the study.

## 7. Production instruction

Create a complete study for the requested character only.

**Current reference character: A**

Do not create an alphabet overview when an individual study is requested. The result must work as a standalone teaching sheet and as part of a coherent series.

**Quality before speed. Construction before decoration. Understanding before style.**
